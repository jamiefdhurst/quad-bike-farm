# What the game is

A draft for Jamie to edit: the few things every part of the game is for. `/idea` checks each new idea against them, and `/tidy` checks open issues against them, so something that fits none of them is questioned before it's built. The look is in `docs/style.md`.

## A first-person day on a real British farm, with Amy and Jess

You turn up, the girls are there, the day moves on, and what you do with it is up to you: ride, mend, work, mess about. There is no story to finish and no score to chase, only a farm that keeps going and two people you spend the day with.

## The principles

1. **A real farm, not a theme park.** Things are where and how they would really be on a working British farm: the machines, the jobs, the weather, the mud, the lane, the village. No floating icons, glowing pickups or arcade rules. Makers are made up; everything else is true to life.
2. **Amy and Jess are the heart.** Every part of the game gives them something to do, say, notice or have an opinion on. A feature they can't touch or react to is a weak one; the best ones are something you do together. They are different people (Amy careful and knowing, Jess restless and loud) and rivals for you, and that shows in everything.
3. **Machines with character, which go wrong.** Each engine and vehicle has its own temperament, wear and faults, dealt per farm. Mending them by hand, step by step, at the toolbox and the bench, is play, not a chore to skip.
4. **The world shows it.** Information comes from the world first: the chalkboard, the cork board, the dash, the notebook, a sound, the girls telling you. A screen opens only when you are looking at a real thing close up. The HUD stays small.
5. **The farm remembers, and the day moves.** What you do lasts: a moved bale, a mended fault, a girl's favour. The time, the light, the weather and the seasons change the place while you play.
6. **Deep before wide.** One farm, its lane, a neighbour and a village, done properly, before anywhere new. A new area or system comes when the existing ones are finished and hang together.

## Using this

- An idea that serves none of the principles is questioned, not filed.
- An idea that serves one but leaves the girls out is asked how they come into it (principle 2).
- An idea that adds a screen is asked whether the world could show it instead (principle 4).
- An idea for somewhere new is asked what is unfinished where we already are (principle 6).
