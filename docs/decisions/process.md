# How work is done

The stack, issues and pull requests, releases, CI, the instructions, and loading and download size.

## Where things stand

What is true now, with the sections below to read for the detail. Keep it current: an item that changes how something stands fixes the line here as well as adding its section.

- **How work flows.** GitHub issues are the to-do list; `/todo` builds one in a worktree beside the main checkout and opens a PR with the issue's labels that merges itself on the Test check; `/idea` files new ones; `/test` tries merged work with Jamie from `🧪 testing` issues. Merge commits only, because commit subjects are the FAQ's "Recently changed". "Issues, pull requests and releases (TODO 106)", "Testing tickets (#350)".
- **Releases.** Every merge that changes what ships is released as `vX.Y.Z`; the bump comes from the PR's labels (`tools/nextVersion.mjs`), and the "Version label" check reads them live. Breaking means an old farm save stops loading or loses something. Branch protection was set by hand with `gh api`; the Settings app doesn't apply it on this private repo. "Issues, pull requests and releases (TODO 106)", "The Version label check reads labels live (#266)".
- **CI.** `Unit 1/3` to `3/3` (Vitest shards), and `Smoke` (build, then the smoke test against a page with the GL draw calls stubbed, one seed, weather and time changed in the page) run side by side; the `Test` gate waits for all of them and is the only required check. Node comes from `mise.toml`. "A faster smoke test (#265)", "Unit and smoke tests side by side (#284)", "Unit tests in shards (#286)".
- **The frame guard.** Each feature's update in `game.js` runs inside `FeatureGuard`, so one feature's error is logged and counted rather than stopping the frame. "A smoke test in CI, and a guard round each feature's update (#262)".
- **Loading and download size.** "Ready" in about 1.7 s warm (keep it under 2.5 s); a cold load fetches about 35 MB: voice files are fetched and decoded only when a line in them is wanted, engine recordings are FLAC, an engine's bank is fetched only when it is on or you come within 45 m, Rapier's wasm is its own file. "Loading faster (2026-10-09)", "Smaller downloads (TODO 104, 2026-10-18)".
- **Instructions.** CLAUDE.md holds what every session needs; area rules are in `.claude/rules/`. New DECISIONS sections are about ten lines and go in the area's file here. "A shorter CLAUDE.md (#352)", "Splitting DECISIONS by area (#353)".
- **How new things should look.** An issue that adds something you'll see carries a **Looks like** list (the real thing and the 8 to 15 features you'd know it by); the look is built first and checked by one look in the running game, two rounds at most (`.claude/rules/looks.md`). "Making new things look right (#383)".
- **Stack.** Three.js, Vite, plain ES modules, Rapier, Vitest; a fixed 60 Hz physics step with interpolation; Y up, a vehicle's forward is +Z. "Stack".

## Working agreement (2026-10-04)

### Getting stuck (built)

- Three bogs (`layout.bogs`): two on the track between the puddles and one in the field's lowest ground at (-30, -38), clear of the spawn spots. `bogAt` in the height model; they count as mud for spray, ruts and the mud coat, sit in a 12 cm dip, are painted dark and churned on the ground texture, and have a glossy wet-mud surface draped over the dip. Grass isn't grown in them.
- `src/vehicle/bog.js` (pure, tested): per quad, a sink from 0 to 1. It grows with wheelspin (0.1 per s per m/s), slowly sitting there, and falls with real movement and quickly once out. In a bog the tyres lose up to 70% of their grip, the mud drags at the quad (1 N per kg per m/s at the middle), and sinking brings in a suction hold that rises with the square of the sink to 1.1 times the quad's weight at `stuckAt` 0.55: more than the tyres can push. The hold is taken off the drive force and also cancels movement, like static friction; when it's holding a quad with the power on, the wheels churn (spin shown, mud thrown, and digging in) rather than the tyre model seeing no slip. Rocking: each change of drive direction loosens it by 0.07 while it's under 0.85. Sunk, the quad sits up to 16 cm lower.
- Tuned headless in the field bog: from rest in the middle, full throttle sank it to 0.4 in 6 s and it barely moved; entering at about 15 mph it just crawled out at 5 mph; slower is stuck; a run at 12 m/s or more gets through having lost a lot of speed. ## Loading faster (2026-10-09)

Jamie asked to start optimising. Measured first (`.scratch/pp/loadtime.mjs` times each loading step and lists the big downloads; `loadprof.mjs` profiles the load by function): a warm load on the local dev server took 12.4 s to "Ready": 0.2 s of models, 6.3 s building the world and 5.6 s of sounds. Agreed with him: fit the clothes once, overlap the sounds with the world, and split the voice banks; download size later (TODO 111).

- **The girls' clothes, 4 s.** The world itself builds in under a second; the rest was fitting the wellies, whose rays were cast at the skinned body, and three's `SkinnedMesh.raycast` re-poses every vertex of every triangle on every ray. Setting each girl's shirt colour (`reshirt`) then refits every garment, so it ran twice per girl. `withPosedBody` in `outfit.js` poses a plain copy of the body once (positions posed, every other attribute and the material shared, same transform) and all the fitting's rays hit that. Checked: every garment's vertices hash the same before and after, both girls (`.scratch/pp/outfithash.mjs`). The first go left out the uv, which `shirtFrontTop` reads the shirt colour by, and moved the placket; the hash caught it.
- **The voice banks, 5.6 s and about 2.3 GB.** The two banks hold 5,587 s and 6,228 s of speech (58 kbps MP3, 40 and 45 MB), which decoded whole at the context's rate is about a gigabyte each. ## Smaller downloads (TODO 104, 2026-10-18)

Measured first in a production build (`.scratch/pp/payload.mjs`: loads the game and sums every response): a cold load fetched **160.5 MB**, of which 125.6 MB was the girls' voice MP3s, 61 MB of WAV recordings, a 6.8 MB script and 6.7 MB of models. Now **34.9 MB**: 23 MB FLAC, 3.6 MB script, 3.1 MB wasm, 2.7 MB models, 2.3 MB json.

- **Voice files fetched when wanted.** The voice banks were said to be lazy, but only their decoding was; every file was downloaded at load. `companionAudio.js` now fetches a file the first time a line in it is wanted and keeps the bytes. The cost is that the first line from a file waits for its fetch (about half a megabyte) as well as its decode; `say` already waits for the decode. Re-encoding the voices smaller (mono, 32 to 40 kbps, about 30%) was turned down for now: it would need a full re-bank and the lazy fetch already means a game only pays for what it hears.
- **FLAC, not WAV.** `tools/wav_to_flac.py` moves each `public/audio/*.wav` to `source/audio/` (in git, not shipped) and writes a FLAC beside where it was, pointing the json's `file` at it. Sample-exact, so the pulse slicing still lines up; 61 MB became 34 MB. Run it after the slicer tools. `tools/render_engine_sound.mjs` reads the kept WAV. The json is written compact. The companion and ride slicers now name flac in their json.
- **Banks only for what's near.** `bankWant.js`: at load only the banks of vehicles within 45 m of where you start are fetched (`startBanks`), and after that an engine fetches its bank when it is on in any way or you come within 45 m (`wantsBank`), so a stopped vehicle across the farm costs nothing until you go there. Engines that are off and far away have no voice node until then. The tuning panel's list no longer offers `engine-bank-tractor-eq` (kept in `source/audio`, its json too; the test reads it there).
- **Rapier's wasm as a file.** `@dimforge/rapier3d` (the standard build) with `vite-plugin-wasm`, instead of `-compat`, which carried 3 MB of wasm as 4 MB of base64 inside the script. The script went from 6.8 MB to 3.1 MB; the wasm is its own cached file. The tests still use `-compat` (same version, no wasm plumbing in node). Checked in a production build and preview: it reaches "Ready" with no errors.
- **The oaks' textures as WebP.** Both oak files carried the same three 1024 px PNGs (2.2 MB each). `tools/shrink_glb_textures.py` re-encodes them as WebP at quality 88 with the colour under clear pixels kept (so leaf edges don't go dark when mip-mapped): the files went from 2.5 MB each to 0.54 and 0.48.
