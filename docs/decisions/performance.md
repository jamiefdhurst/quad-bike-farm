# Performance

How fast the game runs and loads: the frame budget, drawing less, frame time on a real GPU, stutters and load time. Issues labelled `🚀 performance`, worked with `/perf` (`.claude/commands/perf.md`), which measures before and after with `tools/perf.mjs`.

## Holding the frame to its budget in CI (#364)

- The budget (2,600 calls, 3.5M triangles at the spawn) had no enforcement and the spawn was over it after the village (4,477 calls, 4.36M). The smoke test now counts draw calls and triangles from `renderer.info` at five spots and fails over them: `tools/frameBudget.mjs` (`BUDGETS`), measured in the page smoke already loads. Frame ms is not checked, because software GL makes it meaningless; `perf.mjs` stays the local tool for that.
- A spot is measured as you on foot there looking north, east, south and west, worst view kept, so a view into a hedge can't hide a busy one. Spawn is the one exception: the view the game opens on. The game doesn't draw on every animation frame, so the counts are divided by renders (`info.render.frame`), not rAF ticks.
- Measured when set: spawn 4,477 calls and 4.36M; yard 5,652 and 4.31M; village street 5,227 and 4.24M; wood gate 3,424 and 4.14M; top of the pasture 4,271 and 7.54M. The last four got about 8% headroom (yard 6,100 and 4.7M, village 5,650 and 4.6M, wood 3,700 and 4.5M, hilltop 4,600 and 8.2M). They are strict from the start; the figures are the same on repeat runs.
- Spawn is in `KNOWN_OVER`: reported but not failed on until the "drawing less far away" issue brings it back under, then the list is emptied.
- The Smoke job's summary shows each spot against its budget with the change from main, measured on main's build the same way (`tools/budget.mjs`). If main can't be built or measured the table has no change column and the job still passes or fails on the budget alone.
- Raising a budget is deliberate: change `BUDGETS` and add a line here saying what was added and why it is worth it.
- `FeatureGuard` also times each guarded feature (smoothed, a feature run twice in a frame adds up) so the "farm features" stage can be split: the console's `perf` gives `slowestFeatures` and the Tune tab's "Frame time" folder lists the five slowest.

## Draw less far away (#365)

- At the spawn the game drew 4,470 things a frame: vehicles about 160 meshes each at any distance (and again for shadows), the village 960, the barn's loose props 270. `src/world/farDetail.js` (`FarDetail`, run from `World.update` every quarter second and in photo mode) now leaves things out by distance, all in the Graphics params (Tune tab, `tune.graphics`).
- **Set pieces** (`setPieceDistance` 160 m from the nearest edge of their bounds, found once): the neighbour's farm, brushwood, the log stacks, the cone courses. The village keeps its own `drawDistance`, now 150 m from the edge of its area (it was 260 m from its centre, which still drew it from the spawn).
- **Barn fixtures** (`barnPropsDistance` 25 m, nearest edge; it was 15 m until #379's testing found the racks at the far end vanishing from inside the two-span barn, about 21 m corner to corner): the tyre and spares racks, charger, generator, helmet hooks, jumper rack, jacks, fuel tank. The issue said about 40 m, but the spawn is 34 m from the racks, so 40 saved nothing there; from outside the barn they sit behind a wall.
- **Vehicles** (`quad`, `trailer`, `tipTrailer`, `sprayer`): a part with a bounding sphere under 15 cm (bolts, cables, decals, brake bits; not a light's lens or anything that glows) never casts a shadow, and is hidden beyond `vehicleDetailDistance` 60 m. Past `shadowDistance` 80 m the whole vehicle stops casting. Found by size rather than by tagging each kit part, so every model gets it. Hidden parts come back only if we hid them, so a part the game hid stays hidden. This replaces the issue's invisible shadow boxes, which the shadow cut-off made unnecessary. A level change needs 5 m of margin, so a vehicle on the line doesn't flicker.
- **Tussocks and bracken** `roughDistance` 90 to 75 m: the triangles were still 3.85M at the spawn after the above; 60 m took it to 2.98M and 75 m to 3.29M.
- **Start screen tips** come from `vite.config.js` (`__FAQ_TIPS__`) so the first page no longer imports `faqContent.js`. The dev server's time to Ready measured 2.75 s afterwards (2.70 before); the saving is in the built start page, not measured.
- **Result** (`tools/budget.mjs` on a build, same on any machine): spawn 4,470 calls to 2,207 and 3.40M triangles; yard 5,652 to 2,945; village street 5,227 to 3,957; wood gate 3,424 to 1,901; hilltop 4,271 to 2,413 (6.97M triangles). Spawn is strict now (`KNOWN_OVER` is empty) and the other budgets are tightened to about 8% over these.
- Not done: merging static props (`mergeStatic`) in the barn and village, which the distance cut-offs made less pressing.

## A /perf command and a performance label (#396)

- Performance was becoming crucial and was only measured when a session remembered to. Settled with Jamie: a `🚀 performance` area label; `/todo` still takes issues in number order, performance ones included, and only `/perf` takes performance issues first; `/perf <n>` measures main before touching anything and the branch after, the same way, and a change that doesn't move the numbers is reverted rather than shipped; `/perf` alone is a check-up that finds the biggest cost and proposes an issue; the before and after table goes in the PR body and here.
- `tools/perf.mjs` replaces `.scratch/pp/perf.mjs` and `loadtime.mjs`: it builds and serves the checkout (or origin/main in a throwaway worktree with `--main`), loads it in Chrome on the real GPU, times "Ready" from the loading screen, and measures the frame budget's five spots with `measureInPage`'s new `dwell` (ms a frame, the worst frame and the three slowest features, worst view kept). Two runs by default, medians kept; `tools/perfReport.mjs` combines them and writes the table.
- Noise: two builds of the same game differed by up to 1 ms a frame at a spot and 3 ms in the worst frame, so `NOISE` calls less than 1.2 ms (and 4 ms worst) "same". Calls and triangles drift a little as main moves.
- Measured on main when it landed: spawn 2,204 calls, 3.40M triangles, 12.5 ms; yard 2,936, 13.1 ms; village 3,955, 15.2 ms; wood 1,913, 11.5 ms; hilltop 2,442, 6.97M, 14.6 ms; the worst frame at the spawn 31.8 ms. The 8 ms local budget is over everywhere: the first thing for `/perf`.
- Load is now timed from a fresh browser on a build (2.7 s to Ready), not the warm dev server the 2.5 s budget was set on (1.7 s then).

## A far version of the village and the neighbour's farm (#400)

- The village's own draw distance (150 m) and the neighbour's (230 m) made both pop out of view from the farm. Both are now always there: a cheap far version is drawn beyond `fullDistance` (Graphics params, 45 m from the piece's nearest edge, so the swap happens around the lane and not at the farm) and the full piece within it.
- The far version (`src/world/farVersion.js`, `buildFarVersion`) is one mesh per piece with flat vertex colours: each building a box with gables and a gabled roof in its wall and roof colours, plus the neighbour's low yard wall. No windows, doors, signs, props, cars or tractors, no shadow casting, no textures. The village's is 5 buildings' worth of 16 triangles each, the yard's two buildings and five wall boxes.
- It hangs on the full group's `userData.farVersion` (`villageFar`, `neighbourFarmFar` in the scene). `FarDetail.swap` toggles the pair from the camera's position every quarter second; the full piece, once drawn, holds 10 m past `fullDistance` so walking along the line does not flicker. `fullDistance` 0 always draws the full one. `village` joins `neighbourFarm` in `SET_PIECE_NAMES`.
- The full piece is still built at load and only hidden, since the shop, pump and rack interactions and the colliders belong to it; the saving is in drawing, not loading. `villageHost`'s `drawDistance` param is gone, and `neighbourHost` no longer sets visibility. The neighbour herself shows only with the full yard.
- This is the pattern for simplifying other big things far away: build a far group, hang it on `userData.farVersion`, name the full group in `SET_PIECE_NAMES`.

## Shadows only where you can see them (#405)

- The sun's shadow camera was a 90 m square on you, so everything behind you cast a second time. It is now 64 m (`shadowSize`) and its middle sits 22 m (`shadowAhead`) ahead of you along the way you look (`src/world/shadowFit.js`, used by `Sky.update`): about 10 m behind you on the square's axis, 55 m ahead. Texels are finer too (3.1 cm against 4.4). Both are Graphics params, so `shadowAhead` 0 and `shadowSize` 90 is the old square.
- No stretch towards the sun: the shadow camera is orthographic and its depth range (1 to 220 m) is unchanged, so a caster behind or beside you whose shadow lands in view sits on the same ray as the ground it shades and is drawn whatever the window. The barn's shadow over the yard with the sun behind you still lands.
- The window follows the view's ground direction, eased (about a quarter second to cover most of a turn, the short way round) so it glides instead of jumping; looking at the sky holds the last direction; photo mode snaps. The centre is snapped to whole texels, as before, so a steady view does not shimmer.
- Small things (`isSmallPart`: bounding sphere under 15 cm, nothing that glows) stop casting beyond `smallShadowDistance` 20 m, with a 5 m margin, for everything in the scene that isn't a vehicle: found once for each scene child when it first appears (mugs, bottles, tools, bolts on fixtures, the bench's clutter), then switched by distance every quarter second. A mesh taken out of the scene gets its shadow back. Instanced meshes are left alone.
- Measured on Jamie's Mac (1600 by 1000 at 2x, seed 3, sunny, 15:30), shadow pass in draw calls, looking north, east, south, west. Spawn: 1,244, 1,243, 1,243, 1,243 before; 868 to 955 after (about 25% down). Village street: 1,699 each way before; 1,628, 1,444, 350, 354 after (the last two with the street behind you). 64 m gave 25% where 70 m gave 20%; 60 m a little more but only 5 m behind you.

## Four ideas measured and not built (#150, #152, #406, #374)

- Each was measured on main (2026-10-26) before anything was built, and closed because it could not move the frame by more than the 1.2 ms the tool can tell from noise. Main then: spawn 1,961 calls, 9.9 ms; yard 2,580, 10.5; village 3,921, 13.1; wood 1,916, 10.0; hilltop 2,454, 12.0.
- **Parked vehicles sleeping (#150).** A 10 s profile at the spawn: the whole physics step is 0.43 to 0.49 ms of about 9 ms a frame, the quad's part 0.25. Sleeping could save some of that, and every wake path (contact, input, a girl getting on, a tow, a hitch, the parked hold) is a risk.
- **Less work far away (#152).** No girl or audio function reaches the profile's top 40 (the smallest entry is about 0.23 ms), and the slowest feature at every spot is 0.07 ms or less.
- **Culling the girls and the lane (#406).** Built and measured, then undone: skinned meshes culled by a sphere with 1 m to spare, the lane in 40 m pieces, the village roads and apron allowed to cull. Spawn 1,957 to 1,956 calls, village 3,915 to 3,875, wood 1,905 to 1,890, yard and hilltop up by 12 to 15; ms a frame the same within noise. The never-culled meshes are about 5% of the calls, and the worst of the four views at a spot is one where little of it is behind you.
- **Batching vehicles (#374).** The 16 models have 78 to 268 meshes each and 24 to 62 materials, but 60 to 70% of the meshes are in moving parts (`userData.keep`) or named for the game to find. Only 30 to 40% could batch: about 250 to 300 of the spawn's 1,961 calls, roughly 0.5 ms, for a change across all the models, the wear bake, the mud coat and the lights.
- **What the numbers say about the frame.** Comparing the five spots, ms a frame is about 6 ms fixed plus about 2 ms per 1,000 calls, and triangles barely matter (wood has 0.7M more than the spawn at the same ms). Removing every vehicle would still leave about 8 ms. The fixed part is a fit to five points, not a measurement of a cause.

## The fixed part of the frame is the scene, not the GPU (#442)

- Question: ms a frame fits about 6 ms plus 2 ms per 1,000 draw calls. Measured at the spawn on Jamie's Mac (in-page, frame stages from `perf`): the game's own updates are about 2.1 ms (physics 0.45, vehicles 0.8, farm features 0.55, girls 0.2) and drawing 5.4 ms. Looking at the sky with shadows off (about 100 calls) drawing is still 2.0 ms, and 1x against 1.5x pixel ratio only moved 0.7 ms. So the floor is CPU work that grows with the number of objects (8,195 in the scene, 6,370 of them meshes), not fill rate or post-processing (there is none). Shadows off took 1.7 ms off the spawn.
- The profile (`--profile 10 --dev`) put the scene-wide costs at: `updateMatrixWorld` 1.1 ms (every object composed and multiplied each frame, hidden or not; the village is 1,288 objects and the neighbour's farm 927), lil-gui's `listen()` 0.55 ms (13,030 rows polled every frame, with the panel hidden), `getObjectByName` 0.34 to 0.5 ms (pose functions searching a whole vehicle model for the same lever each frame), `projectObject` 0.9 ms, `bakeWear` 0.37 ms for the first seconds only.
- Done: the debug panel's rows only listen while it is open (`settleListening`); `FarDetail` stops the matrix update of a set piece or fixture while it is hidden (`skipMatricesWhileHidden`, caught up the frame it is shown; `getWorldPosition` and `Box3.setFromObject` still update their own chain); `part(model, name)` (`src/vehicle/part.js`) remembers a vehicle's levers, doors and pedals for the pose functions, rechecking a missing one each second.
- Measured, main against the branch, alternating five browser loads each so the Mac's other work hits both: spawn 8.27 to 7.51 ms, looking at the sky 5.84 to 5.37 (medians of 8). `tools/perf.mjs` with `--runs 3`: spawn 9.6 to 9.0, yard 10.7 to 10.1, village 13.5 to 12.8, wood 10.5 to 9.6, hilltop 12.4 to 11.3; draw calls and triangles unchanged. Each is inside the tool's own "same" band, so the tool shows no arrow; the alternating loads are what resolve it.
- Noise: with other sessions running (load average 4 to 7) the tool's runs differed by 1.5 ms between identical builds. Do the A/B in one session, alternating, or wait for a quiet Mac.
- Not done, and where the rest is: 8,195 objects are still walked for matrices (static visible groups such as the barn could stop composing, but a door or a gate inside would stop moving, so each needs checking); vehicles update their dash and pose when parked far away (#225); the render's own scene walk and the shadow pass (draw calls, #374).

## Switched-off vehicles cost nothing: nothing to save (#225, was TODO 334)

- Measured on main (2026-10-26) before building, and closed. At the spawn, yard, village street, wood gate and hilltop the slowest feature is 0.07 ms or less (`updateUtv`, the nearest to a vehicle's own work), against 8.5, 10.1, 12.4, 8.5 and 10.8 ms a frame.

All of `quad.js`'s `update` is 0.24 ms and its `fixedUpdate` 0.22 ms, for every vehicle together including the one you are riding, which could not be skipped. - Revisit only if item 332 adds enough vehicles that their updates reach about 1 ms: the check-up will show it as `updateUtv` or the vehicles stage climbing.

## Rubbish made every frame (#174): not the problem

- Measured on main (2026-10-26, Jamie's Mac, real GPU) before touching anything. The 10 s CPU profile at the spawn has no garbage collector entry above 0.06 ms a frame. Driving a quad flat out for 20 s with `--trace-gc`: 337 minor collections (115 ms in all, longest 4.5 ms) and 13 major ones (24 ms in all, longest 8.3 ms), under 1% of the time, about 0.2 ms a frame.
- A heap allocation sample shows about 1 MB a frame while driving, roughly 80% of it three.js's own render and uniform upload (`setValueM4`, `setValueV3f`, `getParameters`, `projectObject`). The game's own code is the rest: `barnShape` (`src/world/barn.js`) about 30 KB a frame, `availability` (`src/npc/companion.js`) 12 KB, `seatRide.update` and `quad.fixedUpdate` about 9 KB each.
- Removing all of ours would save a few percent of 0.2 ms, inside the tool's 1 ms noise, so nothing was built and the issue closed. The frame time is in drawing: the village spot (about 3,950 calls, 12.7 ms) is the slowest, which is #375.
- To measure it again: a heap sample with `HeapProfiler.startSampling` and `includeObjectsCollectedByMinorGC`, and Chrome launched with `--js-flags=--trace-gc`, while holding `KeyW` on a mounted quad (`game.mount(game.units[0])`).

## Parts too small to see (#451)

- The question was what the 4 ms floor and the shadow pass cost. Measured with `perf.mjs --stages` (new: the frame breakdown at each spot) and in-page experiments on the hilltop: `ms a frame` is the game's JS time, not the GPU's. With the whole scene hidden it is 4 ms, and all but 0.9 ms of that is the game's own updates (physics 0.5, vehicles 0.6 to 1.1, farm features 0.6, girls 0.2 to 0.4), so the "floor" is not the renderer. The drawing stage is 5.4 ms at the spawn and 8 to 11 ms at the village and hilltop, about 3 microseconds a draw call: hiding 506 vehicle calls saved 1.1 ms. The sun's shadow pass off saved 0.6 ms on the hilltop (8.6 to 8.0 ms drawing), and render scale 1.5 to 0.5 saved nothing. So the frame is bound by calls, and the earlier note that it was GPU-bound was wrong.
- A census by scene child at the hilltop looking at the farm: the barn 298 calls, the vehicles about 100 each (about 270 meshes a quad, in over a hundred distinct materials), the farm animals 156. Most of them are a pixel or two across from there: 900 of 2,400 visible meshes span under 1 point, 1,270 under 1.6.
- `FarDetail.leaveOutSpecks` (every quarter second, like the rest of `farDetail.js`): every small mesh (bounding sphere under 1.5 m, not skinned or instanced, not a lens or a glow, not `keepFar`) in any scene child except the set pieces and the barn's props whose sphere would span under `partPixels` (Graphics params, default 1 point of radius, from the camera's field of view and the window's height) is moved to layer 1, and back at 1.25 times that size so it doesn't flicker. A layer rather than `visible` because the game toggles `visible` on bowls, tarps and halos itself and the two would fight; neither the camera nor the shadow pass draws layer 1. Photo mode draws everything.
- Chosen 1 rather than 1.6 (the size the old bolt rule already works at, 15 cm at 60 m): at 1.6 the hilltop's worst view went from 2,529 calls and 12.7 ms to 1,169 and 6.9 ms, at 1 to 1,523 and 9.5 ms, but at 1.6 a wheel (0.3 m) goes at 120 m, at 1 at 195 m. It is one slider (`tune.graphics.partPixels`) if Jamie wants more.
- The frame budget is counted as in a window 1000 points high (`viewHeight`, `MEASURE_ARGS`): CI's window is 320 by 200, where everything is five times smaller and the cull would have taken most of the farm. `measureInPage` also brings the far detail up to date after each move, since it only looks every quarter second.
- Result (`perf.mjs --runs 3`, main then the branch on the same main): spawn 1,988 to 1,903 calls, 9.7 to 9.0 ms (same, inside the noise); yard 2,608 to 2,299, 10.2 to 9.3 ms (same); village 3,947 to 3,168, 12.8 to 10.8 ms; wood 1,933 to 1,220, 9.2 to 7.2 ms; hilltop 2,481 to 1,541, 11.5 to 8.7 ms. Triangles down 4 to 6%. Load unchanged (2.64 s). Every guarded feature still under 0.1 ms: the check costs nothing the tool can see. The CI budgets are tightened to about 8% over (yard from the smoke test's 2,432, which differs from `budget.mjs`'s 2,238 between runs).
- Not done: the spawn and yard stay at 9 to 10 ms, 1.5 to 3 ms of it the game's updates and the baking of the vehicles' wear (`bakeWear`, up to 2 ms a frame until every vehicle is baked; it showed as 4.4 ms in the "input and prompts" stage at the spawn right after loading). Merging a quad's meshes is #374.

## Shaders built before they are needed (#372)

- Three.js builds a material's shader the first time it is drawn. From a fresh load the farm had 77 programs when "Ready" showed and 157 after walking the yard, the village, the wood and the hill, and the first look west from the yard cost one frame of 697 ms (66 programs at once), the village another of 103 ms.
- `Game.prepareShaders()` (`src/core/shaderPrep.js`, called from `main.js` after `game.start()`) now calls `renderer.compileAsync` for every top-level child of the scene, with hidden objects (the far-hidden set pieces, parts hidden by distance) made visible just for that call, because three only prepares what it would draw. It runs in 8 ms slices so the start screen and a quick click stay smooth, and nothing waits for it: "Ready" comes when it did. `game.shadersReady` is the promise and `game.shaderPrep` says what it cost.
- Walked the farm from a fresh load on this Mac (a throwaway script, not kept: 4 views at each of yard, village, wood and hilltop, 0.8 s each). Before: worst frame 697 to 708 ms, 5 frames over 33 ms, 2 over 50 ms. After: worst 66 to 75 ms, 3 over 33 ms, 2 over 50 ms, with no frame left that is a shader (the two over 50 are not compiles, left for #373). Programs: 77 to 180 or so at the start screen, 2 to 3 more later (wear baking).
- Cost: the build takes about 1.5 s after "Ready" (4.5 s with a pause after every child, so it is sliced by time), with no long task of its own (the page's longest task from Ready was 368 ms against 372 ms before, both the world's first frames). `perf.mjs`, three runs each: load 2.66 to 2.71 s to Ready, ms a frame the same at all five spots (within 0.4 ms), draw calls and triangles unchanged. About 30 programs more than a walk compiles, for things hidden at the start.
- Not done: vehicles bought in the classifieds turn up the next morning, built at load, so nothing is built mid-game that would need compiling then. The two remaining 66 ms frames are not shaders; counting them is #373.

## Counting stutters and tracking the budget (#373)

- Settled with Jamie: the history is a workflow artifact, not a committed file; a long frame is named a shader, a clean-up, a stage or a feature; `perf.mjs` reports the stutter figures too.
- **Stutters.** `FrameStats` keeps ten seconds of frames: the 1% low (`p99Ms`, the worst when there are fewer than 100 frames), the frames over 33 ms and a running total. `core/stutters.js` names each long frame (over 33 ms of the game's own work, so a slow GPU that holds up the handover counts, a GPU stall in the browser's compositor does not): shaders prepared in that frame (`renderer.info.programs` grew), else the heap fell 1.5 MB or more (Chrome's `performance.memory`, coarse), else the stage that took 40% or more of it (the farm feature, when one has half of "farm features"), else "spread across the frame". The last five are kept. They show in the console's `perf` and the Tune tab's "Frame time" folder, which also shows the heap and how fast it is allocating (growth between frames, falls left out). `checking.md` says how to find the allocators with Chrome's recorder.
- **`perf.mjs`.** Each view's table row adds the 1% low, the frames over 33 ms and the causes seen while it was held; a main from before this has none, so those cells have no "before".
- **Over time.** `.github/workflows/perf-history.yml` runs on each push to main that changes what ships, measures its build with `tools/budget.mjs` and adds a line to `history.json`, kept as the artifact `budget-history` (the last 60, 90 days of retention; the next run fetches the last successful run's). The Smoke job's summary fetches it and prints the last ten merges per spot with a bar and the change over them. It records the commit and its subject, not the version: the release workflow names that afterwards. A quiet quarter starts the history afresh.
- **Measured** (`perf.mjs`, three runs each, same main): draw calls, triangles, ms a frame and load all the same within noise (spawn 9.3 ms both, village 11.0 to 11.1, Ready 2.48 to 2.50 s). It costs a few comparisons a frame.

## The ground in tiles (#665)

- The ground is a rectangle in the layout (`layout.ground`, whole metres, not centred), sampled once at 1 m from `terrainHeight.js` into a `GroundGrid`. That one grid is the physics heightfield and the source of every drawn tile, so the world is asked for each height once, and a wheel is always on the ground the tiles are drawn from. The painted texture covered `terrainSize` (4096 px over 420 m, about 10 px a metre), and a test failed if the ground was ever bigger than it; #759 paints the ground in tiles instead and `terrainSize` is gone. The coarse ring beyond the ground is now `outerMargin` (290 m) round it, not a centred 1,000 m square.
- Physics: one heightfield or tiles of heightfield round the vehicles? Measured in node over 1,420 by 1,040 m: sampling at 1 m 820 ms (1.05 microseconds a point, the same whichever way it is cut), the collider 13 ms, 5.9 MB. A 64 m tile of heightfield: 5.9 ms to sample, 0.09 ms to create and 0.11 ms to remove. Tiles would move the same 0.8 s into play (6 ms a tile, every tile any vehicle, girl or lane car stood on) and add a collider the girls' and vehicles' ray filters (`terrain.collider.handle`) would need to know in a set, to save 6 MB. One heightfield keeps every wheel on real ground everywhere with nothing to build in play, so it is the one. It costs a single 0.8 s of the load for a 1.4 km ground (today's 420 m: 0.18 s, as before). If the load goes over its budget once the map is big, the sampling is the thing to hide behind the start screen or spread out; Rapier needs the whole array to make the collider.
- Drawing: tiles of about 64 m (a shorter last one), 1 m within 150 m, 2 m to 400 m, 4 m to 800 m, 8 m beyond (`DEFAULT_GROUND_PARAMS`, in the Tune tab as "Ground tiles"), 10% hysteresis so a tile on a line does not flicker. A tile next to a coarser one lays its edge vertices on the straight line between the neighbour's, so tiles meet to float precision with no skirt (a test walks every seam of a 1,400 by 1,000 m ground, with every step meeting its neighbours, at three camera positions and finds the worst gap under 1 cm). Normals are the grid's slope at the tile's own step, so same-step tiles shade the same across a seam. Each tile is its own mesh, culled by the view, with the one ground material.
- Built as needed: `prime` builds, behind the start screen and before the shaders are prepared, every tile within 400 m of where you start and every one at 4 or 8 m, so there is never a hole; `updateGround` (timed by the frame guard) then builds the nearest tile that is wrong, at least one and no more than 2 ms a frame. Measured in node on the real heights and fields: a 1 m tile 1.2 ms (worst 3.5), 2 m 0.3, 4 m 0.07, 8 m 0.02; priming the 1,400 by 1,000 m ground 74 ms, today's farm 42 ms. Nothing comes near the 8 ms hitch line, so no tile build is split across frames.
- A 1,420 by 1,000 m ground from the middle of the farm: 352 tiles, 528,000 triangles drawn if every tile were in view (29 at 1 m, 104 at 2 m, 139 at 4 m, 80 at 8 m), 15.5 MB of vertex data (5.6 MB of it the grid) against 2.8 million triangles and 22 MB for the whole ground at 1 m. The draw calls are the cost: at about 3 microseconds each (above) 250 is about 0.75 ms a frame, most of them far tiles of a few hundred triangles, so merging the far tiles is the next saving if the map's budget needs it (filed as its own idea).

## The ground painted in tiles (#759)

- The painted ground was one 4096 px canvas over a centred square, and the tarmac map one texel every 0.5 m over the same square. On #681's 1,420 by 1,040 m ground that is 2.5 pixels a metre, blurred road edges, 11 million tarmac lookups and a load of 5.6 s (3.3 s today). Measured on the real game with a throwaway layout (#681's ground, outline and fields, a hedge on every edge, a 3 km winding road): "Ready" 5.61 s before.
- **Painted as you come near, a tile at a time** (`groundPaint.js`, `terrain.js`). The ground's 64 m tiles are painted at 10 pixels a metre (656 px with 0.8 m of the next tile's painting each side, so the filter has something to read at a seam) once you are within `paintNear` (200 m), nearest first, and let go 25% further off, so the painted ground is at most about 45 tiles, about 100 MB with mipmaps, whatever the map's size (the old texture was 85 MB for 420 m). A tile's painting is one texture and one material, all sharing the one shader program; the map is read in the shader from where the pixel is on the ground (`groundOrigin` and `groundSize` uniforms), not from uvs, which is what lets a tile swap between its own painting and the overview's. Chosen over one big texture (the memory and the load), a texture atlas (filtering across tiles, and mipmaps would bleed) and virtual texturing (far more machinery than a farm needs).
- **The overview**: all of `layout.ground` painted once at about a pixel a metre (1,420 by 1,040 px at full size), with detail finer than a pixel left out (`fine` is off under 4 px a metre: grit cells grow to 4 times the size, specks and drill rows are not drawn). It is what shows beyond 200 m, and on a tile until its own painting is done. 122 ms at full size, 50 ms today (dev server).
- **Same ground, any window.** `groundTexture.js` paints a window of the ground onto a canvas that holds just that, and no random number is drawn in order any more: each belongs to its cell or its item (`hash01`, `itemRandom`), so two windows paint the same marks where they overlap (`tests/groundPaintWindows.test.js`, over the pit, the wood, the hill, the stubble and the road). Painting is a generator that pauses every 2,500 canvas calls, so a tile is painted a millisecond or so at a time. The farm looks the same (one look at the farm road's edge and the lane's, before and after), though each speck is a new one, as its random number is now its own.
- **Frame cost.** `updateGround` paints for up to `paintMs` (2 ms; the first try of 3 gave 7.0 ms) and never starts a tile's hand-over (the texture upload, `renderer.initTexture`, so it is counted in that frame and not in whichever draws first) once the budget is spent, so a frame holds at most one. Jumping 150 m six times across today's farm in the dev server: the longest `updateGround` call 6.1 ms (under 8), 99th 5.8, the hand-over at worst 0.7 ms; a tile in the pit is the slowest to paint, 32 ms in node over 74 slices. Tiles within 100 m (`paintPrime`) are painted behind the start screen: 15 tiles, 95 ms today, 135 ms at full size.
- **The tarmac map near roads** (`tarmacMaskSteps` in `roadWet.js`, `hardBoxes` in `roads.js`). A road's index already lists the 16 m cells its segments, pads and bays reach, so each cell grown by the road's reach is a box where tarmac could be; `fields.tarmacBoxes()` adds the village's car park and forecourt. A tile's mask (0.5 m, the same as before) and the overview's (2 m) ask `tarmacAt` once a texel and only inside the boxes, and a tile no box reaches has none (a blank texel far away). ## The smoke test's load on CI, and why triangles differed (#760)

- **Where the load goes.** The smoke test now prints the loading steps with the time each first showed (`loadStepLines`, `tools/frameBudgetRun.mjs`) and the Chrome version and cores. On CI (4 cores, Chrome 156, software GL) a load took 22, 14 and 17 s in the three Smoke runs of this PR, of which "Building world" is 18 s of the 22 (1.7 s to 19.6 s), against 7.2 s of 7.6 on Jamie's Mac. A CPU profile of the load locally (an unminified build, `Profiler` over CDP) puts 7.3 s of the 8 in three.js's `onFirstUse`, which waits for each shader program to link: 125 programs on SwiftShader, about 55 ms each. The runs before #758 took 79 to 134 s and none of these three came near them, so what made those slow was not seen again; the steps will say which one it was if it recurs.
- **No cheap cut.** The programs are the ones the frames and the budget draw, and `prepareShaders` (#372) asks for the 30 or so that are hidden at the start as well. Leaving those out would spare about a quarter of the programs (not measured as time) and loses the check that every shader compiles. Not done.
- **One limit.** `LOAD_TIMEOUT_MS` (300 s) in `tools/frameBudgetRun.mjs` serves the smoke test, the budget run, `perf.mjs` and `shot.mjs`, which each had their own (150 s, 150 s, 150 s and 180 s). A timeout now says which of the game object, the companions and the Ready status were missing, with the steps seen so far (`tests/loadWait.test.js`). The CI job's own limit is 12 minutes.
- **Why CI and local triangles differed.** The ground builds its fine tiles a few milliseconds a frame (`TileSet.update`, #665), and the count only settled three frames after a move, so a slower machine had rebuilt fewer tiles to the step the new spot wants, and was still drawing finer ones left from the last spot (more triangles). On Jamie's Mac with every core busy the village read 2.666M and the hilltop 5.603M against 2.643M and 5.597M idle. `measureInPage` now builds every tile with no time limit before each count. Idle and loaded runs agree (village 2.611M, hilltop 5.503M, the others unchanged), and CI's Smoke job gave the same figures as local: 2.47M, 2.95M, 2.61M, 3.08M and 5.50M triangles, 1.43M for the movers, calls as before. The village and hilltop are 1% and 2% below what an idle run read before, for the same reason; no budget needed moving.
