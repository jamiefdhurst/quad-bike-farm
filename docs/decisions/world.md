## Mud (2026-10-05)

- Mud had a `mudAt` hook but made little difference: Rapier's raycast wheels count only half the drive force against grip and four wheels shared it, so tyres never lost traction; mud only cut cornering grip and puddles were plain mud. Now grip goes from 2.2 on grass to 0.7 on the track and 0.42 in puddles (new `puddleAt`), multiplied by tyre pressure.
- `src/vehicle/traction.js` (pure, tested) limits drive force to what the tyres can take. Driven wheels are treated as locked together and spin up together when you ask for more, losing some drive and a little sideways grip (15%; 35% made the yellow loop off at 11 m/s). The engine load now uses the drive fraction that really reaches the ground, so the revs flare when the wheels really spin. The yellow is rear-wheel drive (`fourWheelDrive: 0`): on the track it spins 0.8 for the first 1.5 s of a launch, its tail steps out but it's controllable at 11 m/s and needs lifting off before bends at 14. Red and blue never spin on the track and barely in puddles. Cornering on the track is about 0.5 g against 1.8 g on grass.
- Spray is a capped point buffer per quad like the smoke (clumps off each tyre, spray from spinning wheels, a burst into puddles). Mud on the quads is a per-quad shader blend over cloned materials, driven by a coat amount that builds with speed and wheelspin through mud (tyres in about 10 s, bodywork half-coated after a lap), favouring tyres, arches and low parts, wet and glossy at first and drying lighter over 4 minutes. Ruts are ring-buffered ribbons per wheel (4,000 segments a quad), fading over 150 s in mud and 40 s on grass, laid on a new lookup of the terrain mesh's own height so they sit on the drawn ground. All of it costs about 0.16 ms of CPU a frame for three quads and 6 draw calls.
- Made much muddier later (TODO 1): body build rate 0.0011 to 0.004 (half-coated after about 20 s through mud at 9 m/s, not a lap), tyres 0.014 to 0.03, puddle splash and drying doubled or slowed, tyres shed less on grass. The shader's coat now reaches up to 1.3 m (was 0.95), tops of panels catch more, and the mask is 30% more generous. Judged by the numbers and one screenshot, not by feel.
- There's no way to wash the mud off in the game yet (the debug folder can). The puddle surface is still a grey disc.

## Drawing less (frame time) (2026-10-12)

Jamie: the game was working his M4 Max MacBook Pro hard, and more is coming. Measured first, headless at 1728 by 1117 points at Retina 2x (the 16" default), with `--disable-gpu-vsync --disable-frame-rate-limit` so the frame rate shows the real cost (`.scratch/pp/perf.mjs`, `tris.mjs`, `ab2.mjs`; headless fps drifts by a fifth over a run, so each change was alternated with the baseline three times).

- **What it was.** The game's own work was small: about 2 ms a frame for the girls, the vehicles and the physics, and 6.7 ms including handing the frame to the GPU. The GPU was the problem: 7.6 million triangles and 2,100 draw calls a frame at 3200 by 2000 pixels with 4x antialiasing. Uncapped it managed about 80 fps (12 ms), and a ProMotion screen asks for 120, so the laptop ran flat out and still missed. About 6.5 million of the triangles were ground cover and trees: tussocks 2.3M (16,000 of them, 144 triangles each), stubble 1.4M, grass 0.6M (90,000 clumps), bracken 0.3M, hedges and trees 1.2M. Each was one InstancedMesh spanning the farm, which three.js culls as a whole, so every stalk was drawn every frame, behind you or half a kilometre away. Each of these was worth about 2 ms: rendering at 1.5x instead of 2x (2.4), no ground cover (2.0), no instanced trees and hedges (2.0), no shadow pass (1.7). Small vehicle parts not casting shadows saved nothing. The engine sound worklets never sleep, but one renders 27 s of sound in 0.7 s, so a dozen cost little and were left.
- **Tiles.** Everything scattered in numbers is split into square tiles of ground, one InstancedMesh per tile with its own bounding sphere (`src/world/tiles.js`, pure and tested; `tiledInstances.js` builds them). The renderer leaves out tiles out of view, and out of the sun's view in the shadow pass. Ground cover uses 30 m tiles (`COVER_TILE`), hedges, trees, bushes and gorse 60 m (`MODEL_TILE`, through `instanceModel`, so every model scatter gets it). The layout is unchanged: the same random draws in the same order, so a seed lays out the same farm.
- **Draw distance.** Grass and stubble stop at 70 m (`coverDistance`), tussocks and bracken at 90 m (`roughDistance`): at eye height that is just under the horizon line, where the ground's own colour and the haze take over, and a side-by-side screenshot across the grass field with and without it couldn't be told apart. A tile goes when its nearest instance is beyond the distance (`DrawDistance`, updated by `World.update` from the camera).
- **60 fps cap.** `maxFps` 60: the physics already steps at 60 Hz, so 120 fps only added interpolated frames for twice the work. A schedule (`nextFrameAt`), not a gap since the last frame, so it holds 60 on a 120 Hz, 144 Hz or unthrottled screen (a gap with a jitter allowance let 80 through unthrottled). A skipped tick does nothing: no time passes and keys wait for the next frame. 0 lifts it.
- **Render scale 1.5.** `renderScale` caps the pixel ratio (it was the screen's, up to 2). With the antialiasing on, the difference is hard to see on a Retina screen.
- **Paused.** Behind the start screen and the pause card nothing moves, so the farm is drawn when the pause starts or the window changes, and twice a second in case something finished loading, rather than every tick.
- **Result.** Uncapped, about 175 fps (5.4 ms a frame) from about 80 (12 ms); 2.8 million triangles from 7.6 at the spawn view (draw calls about the same, 2,200: more tiles, fewer of them drawn). Capped, 60 frames drawn a second and the main thread about 30% busy, from 42%. "Graphics" in the Tune tab (World) and `tune.graphics` in the console set all four; the console's `perf` gives the frame rate, the game's own time a frame (`FrameStats`) and the draw calls and triangles.
- **Keeping it there.** Anything new scattered in numbers goes through `tiledInstances` or `instanceModel`, never one InstancedMesh for the lot, and small ground cover registers with the draw distance. After adding to the world, run `node .scratch/pp/perf.mjs` (dev server on 5180), which checks the spawn view against a budget. Not done, and measured after the tiles: the whole shadow pass is about 1.5 ms of the 5.3; the vehicles' shadows are 730 of its draw calls (about 110 casting parts each) and 0.5 ms; hiding every hedge saves 0.06 ms, so a simpler hedge model isn't worth it. If the budget gets tight, the vehicles' shadows are next: a few invisible boxes per vehicle that only the sun's shadow camera sees, standing in for the parts, rather than merging the parts (many move, change colour or get fitted and taken off).

## Seasons across games (#30, was TODO 135)

Jamie settled: the farm has its own real calendar (real month lengths, 365 days, no leap years), not today's date; a new farm starts on a date dealt from its farm seed and every game is the next day; everything in the proposal except spring blossom and new job kinds; a few seasonal girl lines; `?season=` takes all four; old saves land in the right place from their day number, so nothing breaks.

- **The date** (`world/seasons.js`, pure, `tests/seasons.test.js`): `startDayOfYear` deals a day of the year from `farmSeed ^ 0x5ea5`, and `farmDate({ start, day })` gives the month, day of the month, year, weekday and season of farm day `day`. Seasons are the meteorological ones (March to May spring, and so on). The farm date is separate from the game's day number, so a save needs nothing new: `day` and `farmSeed` already say where the farm is in its year. A year is 365 games, so most farms stay in one season for 90 games or so; `?season=` and the Girls tab's "Load it as..." buttons are how to see the others (`reloadAsSeason` loads again with the parameter, because the season shapes the world as it is built). A forced season is the middle of that season (15 January, 15 April, 15 July, 15 October).
- **The weather pool.** `SEASON_WEATHER` holds multipliers on each kind's weight (`pickWeatherKind`) and on each temperature band's odds (`pickTemperature`). Winter has snow (3 times its weight), fog, rain and overcast; summer has no snow, no frost, more thunder (2.5 times) and a new `hot` band (22 to 29 degrees) that only a summer sunny or fair day can draw; autumn is wetter and foggier; spring is showery. Seeded games use the same tables, so a seed still repeats, but a seed now deals a different day than before.
- **Daylight.** `daylightFor(dayOfYear)` runs sunrise and sunset along a cosine from the shortest day (21 December: 7.8 hours of daylight, sun 0.24 rad high) to the longest (21 June: 16.2 hours, 1.0 rad), 10.3 hours in mid-October as the old fixed day was. `daylightParams` copies the defaults with those, and keeps the start window at least 1.5 hours before sunset so a winter game does not begin in the dark. Calling it a day is allowed from the earlier of `endDayHour` and a quarter of an hour before sunset.
- **The ground.** `SEASON_GROUND`: `wet` is the least the ground dries to (`Weather.groundWet`: winter 0.4, spring 0.25, autumn 0.2, summer 0), so the mud and the bogs follow the existing wetness; `dust` multiplies the stubble dust (`dust.js` `seasonDust`: summer 1.4, winter 0.1).
- **The trees.** Autumn is now the autumn season, in place of the old one day in six (`autumnToday` and `AUTUMN_CHANCE` are gone); everything built on `game.autumn` is as it was. Winter dulls and darkens the leaves with `WINTER_TINTS` through the same tint pass. Not done: bare trees (the models have no bare version), spring blossom, and summer-only decoration.
- **The calendar** (`wallCalendar.js`): the page is the real month, the days under their weekdays (up to six rows, so the row height is 33 px), crossed off up to today. Every farm used to start in April with 30-day pages.
- **The girls** (`npc/seasonLines.js`): each says one remark a game about the time of year, 45 seconds after the weather remark (`companionVoice.watchWeather`, from `weather.season`). Recorded with the rest.
- **The panel and console.** `weather.season` (settable: loads again with the season), `weather.date`; the Girls tab's farm folder shows the date and has a button per season. The dust factor shows in the Dust tuning.
- **Left for later:** hay and baling are #31, which can key on `game.season`. Jobs on the board are unchanged.

## Snow on everything that faces up (#95, was TODO 201)

Snow was only in the ground shader, so a snowy farm was a white field with a green surround. Now the rest of the world whitens with the same weather `cover`.

- **One patch, not new materials** (`world/snowCoat.js`, `snowCoatView.js`): `coatMaterial` chains an `onBeforeCompile` onto every lit, opaque, static material, after any patch it has (wind sway, rust), and keeps its programme-cache key distinct. The fragment block turns the world-space normal into "faces up" (`holdEdge` 0.3, so walls and the undersides of branches stay bare), lays patchy white by a three-dimensional noise on the world position, and roughens it. Walls stay dark, which is what gives the hedges their dark sides.
- **Left out** (`coatable`): glass and water (transparent), lamps (emissive), the girls (skinned), the ground (`userData.snowOwn`; it has its own) and anything low under a roof (a floor, a bench, a bale: `indoorRoof` 2.4 m with `world.covered`), or the barn's floor would go white.
- **Tops go first.** The cover the shader sees is the ground's times `topsRate` 1.45, so roofs and hedges whiten sooner and keep it longer. The outer ring takes the same, which gives the white hills. The terrain shader's tarmac takes 0.4 more cover to whiten, so the road is grey-white first and clears first.
- **Melt shows** (`SnowLook`): while the ground's cover is falling, bare surfaces go dark and glossy in patches, more on steep and downward faces (the eaves). It builds over `wetEase` 45 s to a full melt of 0.0012 cover a second (a mild day loses 0.0025) and fades when the melt stops.
- **Vehicles** (`vehicleSnowAfter`): each has its own uniforms, so its snow is its own. It gathers while it snows (`carSettle` 90 s), never exceeds the tops, slides off at speed (clear in `carShedSeconds` 6 s above `carShedSpeed` 3 m/s) and thaws indoors (150 s). Not kept in the save.
- **Only a snowy game pays for it.** The coat is applied while a snowy game loads, or on the first frame snow or cover is set by hand, so a game without snow keeps its plain shaders. Things added mid-game (a bought vehicle, a donor) are not coated until the next load.
- **Not done:** evergreens heavier than other trees (nothing marks them), snow on the girls (#518), and a visibly different south face on melt (there is no sun direction in the patch).
- **Debug:** Tune "Snow coat". Console `weather.snowTops`, `weather.snowMelt`.
