## Engine and drivetrain tuning

- Mixture is the single idea behind the engine: `choke enrichment - enrichment needed at this temperature`. Catch chance, idle, roughness, power and plug fouling all derive from it. Opening the throttle dilutes the choke a little, so cranking with the throttle open clears a flood.
- A stalled-from-flooding engine keeps its fouling and dries out slowly, so it's hard to restart straight away. Choke off and throttle open while cranking is the cure.
- The drivetrain is an automatic: centrifugal clutch into a CVT. Braking drags on the engine only while the drivetrain is "coupled" (the clutch has locked with the bike moving). Coupling releases when the bike nearly stops.
- Tuned so a hard stop from about 20 mph or more stalls a stone-cold engine on choke, gentler or slower stops don't, and a warm engine never stalls from braking. Checked with a seeded headless sim across 40 seeds.
- Warm idle lowered to 1150 rpm (feedback, 2026-10-04). That needed the stall threshold dropped to 350 rpm and braking drag reduced, because a single misfire costs about 140 rpm and a warm engine under braking load must stay clear of the stall line by more than that.
- Drive force keeps a floor of 40% even when engine power is low, so a cold bike is sluggish but rideable.
- The quad never rolls over in normal riding: the Rapier vehicle slides before it tips. There's a "right the quad" action (T when riding, or E when looking at a tipped quad) in case it does.

## Engine load (2026-10-05)

- `src/vehicle/engineLoad.js` measures each physics step how much of the engine's effort goes into resistance (hills, mud, obstacles): the force the tyres put down minus mass times measured acceleration. While pulling away in the CVT's lowest ratio, the effort of getting moving counts too. Grip comes from each tyre's suspension force (less in mud); asking for more spins the wheels, which drops the load. Rapier's vehicle never actually skids at these settings, so wheelspin only exists in the engine.
- The engine pulls its target revs toward idle in proportion to load, so a warm engine sags at most halfway and can't stall from load. A weak (cold, choked) engine under a heavy climbing load is dragged below idle and stalls: stone cold on choke it stalls pulling away up the 11 degree yard bank; from about a third warm it labours but keeps going. Warm, 6 seeds x 3 quads x 90 s of track riding gave no stalls.
- Drive force still comes from the revs the engine is trying for, so the bike goes exactly as before (same speeds at the same times on the flat and on a 20 degree ramp); only sound and feel change. Red flat out now cruises at about 5,700 rpm, climbing 4,400 to 5,000 (the ATV recordings sit at 4,000 to 5,500), and the climbing sound is measurably deeper.
- Throttle shut with the clutch coupled to a rolling bike, the wheels hold the engine above idle (70 rpm per m/s) and half the engine braking carries on; before, engine braking vanished 0.2 s after closing the throttle. `overrunBrake` 0 restores the old coast.
- Cruising lower made a tank last 15 to 20% longer, so fuel per revolution went up 12% and the idle fraction down by the same factor: tank life is back to about 25 minutes of riding and idle burn (tap-off timing) is unchanged.

## Faults that develop (2026-10-05)

The TODO item settled the symptoms; I chose the rest without asking: the four faults all go in, fixed by four instant jobs in the toolbox panel (a hands-on version belongs to the workshop item), and the girls notice but never fix.

- Pure logic in `src/vehicle/faults.js` (`Faults`, one per quad, tests in `tests/faults.test.js`; tunables under `<quad>/Faults` in the tuning panel). A fault starts with a random chance per second of the right kind of use, at level 0.15, and rises to 1 until fixed: puncture while moving (average 2400 s, takes 120 s to go flat, and goes on parked), belt while moving with the throttle over 0.6 (1800 s, 300 s of hard riding to its worst), cable and carb while the engine runs (2700 s and 2400 s, taking 600 s and 900 s). One of each at a time. Over ten minutes of riding a quad averages somewhere between 0.3 and 2 faults (a test bounds it); they start only on a quad that is running, so a parked one stays well.
- Puncture: the physics steer gets `steerPull` added (0.09 rad at worst towards the flat tyre, half for a rear one; the visible bars don't move, only the quad pulls) and the drive loses up to 12%. Belt: drive loses up to 80%, and in `EngineModel` the `slip` control cancels the load that holds the revs down and adds up to 1800 rpm at full throttle (`beltFlareRpm`), so the revs flare and the quad hardly goes. Cable: `stickThrottle` holds the throttle where you left it and eases it back over up to 5 s (a fully sticking cable); below level 0.2 it's only stiff and doesn't hang; pressing the throttle is never held back and switching off frees it. Carb: the `dirt` control adds up to 0.3 roughness, takes up to 25% of the power and drives `erratic` (so the existing idle hunting and stumbling get worse and worse).
- Fixes: toolbox panel keys `T` tyres, `R` belt, `L` cable, `C` carb (buttons too). Each does its job if there's a fault and otherwise says it was fine, so pressing them doesn't diagnose. All instant.

## The day's air in the engine (#10, was TODO 115)

The item settled the behaviour; I chose the numbers and where it lives. Two-strokes already took the day's temperature through their jet (TODO 4), so this adds the damp, and gives every engine the power.

- Pure logic in `src/engine/airDensity.js` (tests in `tests/airDensity.test.js`; tunables with the other jetting ones in `engineParams.js`). `airCharge` is how much denser than the reference day the air is: `(12 - °C) * airPerDegree` 0.006, less `(damp - airRefDamp) * airPerDamp` 0.15, scaled up when warm because warm air holds more water. So about +0.10 at -5 °C, -0.09 on a warm day in the rain, 0 on the reference day.
- Damp is `Weather.damp`: 0.2 always, plus 0.5 of the rain and 0.3 of the wet ground, so 0.2 to 1. It reaches the engine as the `dayDamp` control beside `dayTemp`; the engine's default is the reference damp, so every engine test that passes nothing is unchanged.
- Effects: power times `1 + airPower` 0.6 times the charge (about +6% on a frosty dry day, -5% on a muggy one), for every engine. On a carburettor with a fixed jet (a four-stroke that is not injected or a diesel) the charge also takes `airLean` 0.4 times itself off the mixture, so dense air runs lean and thin air rich; the existing lean and rich running does the rest. Injection and diesels keep their mixture. A two-stroke's mixture comes from its jet error, which now includes the damp (`jetPerDamp` 0.5 sizes smaller per whole unit of damp), so the toolbox's advice and the "spot on" bonus follow the day's air, not the temperature alone.
- Panel and console: "the day's air" in Running, `weather.damp` in the console. The toolbox's jet row explains it.
- Not checked: how it feels. Unit tests only; the numbers are first guesses.

## Piston slap (#11, was TODO 116)

A worn engine knocks when cold: a hollow clack at idle and pulling away that goes as it warms. Heard only, no new visuals.

- Pure logic in `src/engine/pistonSlap.js` (tests in `tests/pistonSlap.test.js`; its tunables and ranges merge into the engine params, so they show in the engine's Tune group). `slapLevel` is the piston's looseness (`slapFrom` 0.3 to `slapFull` 0.8) times how cold it is (gone by `slapWarmBelow` 0.7 of warm). `slapCarry` keeps it full at idle or pulling and `slapRevsQuiet` 0.6 quieter at revs with the throttle off.
- **Its own number, `pistonWear`, not `wear`.** A service clears `wear` but cannot fix a loose bore, and the issue wants a top-end rebuild to be the cure. `pistonWear` starts null, takes the wear the engine was dealt the first time it runs (so every place that deals wear, and old saves, work without change), then rises by whatever `wear` rises by. `rebuildTopEnd` and the big-bore `fitBore` set it back to `slapNewPistonWear` (0). It is in `ENGINE_KEPT`, so the farm remembers it.
- Applies to every engine, but only the two-strokes have a rebuild, so a worn four-stroke or diesel knocks cold for good. I left that: the rebuild is item 3 and there is no four-stroke equivalent yet.
- Sound: the engine emits `slap` at `slapRate` 7 a second at full slap with a little scatter (not one per firing, so it costs nothing at high revs); `engineAudio` carries the engine's `slap` as the strength and the worklet plays a dull 340 to 460 Hz clack (`slapVolume` 0.1 in the audio tuning), lower and rounder than the tappet and cooling ticks. It adds `slapRoughness` 0.04 at most.
- Panel and console: "slap" in Running, "piston looseness" settable in Problems (`engineHealth.pistonLooseness`), and "Put it right" clears it.
- Not checked: how it sounds. The numbers are first guesses; no recording or listening round.
