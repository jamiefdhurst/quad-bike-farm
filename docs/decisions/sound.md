# Sound

Engine sound, the banks and how they are judged, ride sounds, the girls' voices and the radio.

## Where things stand

What is true now, with the sections below to read for the detail. Keep it current: an item that changes how something stands fixes the line here as well as adding its section.

- **Engine sound.** The default is `pulse`: every firing from the engine model plays a recorded firing at its own speed, cut off at the next one, so the pipe's resonances stay put as the revs rise. That measured closer to real engines than resampled rev recordings did. Each idle firing plays from the bank's bare pulses (the recording with its resonances divided out once at load), its burst shortened to fit the gap once firings meet (half of it in pitch, each firing varied 3%, `pulseBurstFollow`, `pulseRateJitter`), under the recording's own resonances put back and half the rev EQ (`pulseModelEq`), so the revs rise in pitch at the top (`pulseModel`, "Engines rise in pitch at the top (#855)"); with it off, an EQ that comes in with the revs turns idle firings into revs. `pulseRevLo`/`Hi` can blend in a bank's real rev firings (the green and yellow do). An idle of whole cycles is cut into its firings at the quietest moments (`pulseSplit`, the engine's cylinders), so the cars, the Crag and the Yak build their revs from the idle alone with every firing played ("The cars and the diesels fire every cylinder as they rev (#853)"). Above idle the singles replay their last firing a share of the time (`pulseSteady`), so their revs ring as a note, and a several-cylinder engine's cylinders each keep their own strength and lateness (`pulseCylGain`, `pulseCylTime`), with each firing varied a little; a late firing never leaves a gap. The values are fitted to real rev recordings per type and passed by ear ("Revs with a note you can follow (#854)"). `pulseStretch` and `pulseBoomCut` exist for a slow single and a boomy recording. `recorded` and `synth` stay selectable for comparison. ## Engine audio, second pass (2026-10-04)

- Replaced per-pulse sample playback with an AudioWorklet synth (`src/audio/engineWorklet.js`). The old approach pitch-shifted one thump sample with rpm, which sounded like a sped-up recording at high revs.
- The new model: each firing pulse is a pressure kick (sharp blowdown, then falling flow for about a third of the cycle) into a delay-line exhaust pipe with an inverting open-end reflection, then two fixed silencer resonances and a load-dependent tone filter. Rising revs mean faster pulses through fixed resonances, as in a real engine.
- Pulses are scaled down as revs rise (`densityComp`) so overlapping pulses don't run away into clipping. Full throttle ends up about three times idle loudness.
- Every pulse varies a little in strength (`combustionJitter`) even when warm, to avoid a synthetic perfectly periodic buzz.
- Starter: the engine model now slows the crank before each compression stroke and surges after it. The worklet's starter is armature whine with commutator ripple, brush hiss and gear-mesh rattle, following that speed, plus a solenoid clunk.
- Tuned by rendering timelines offline (OfflineAudioContext in headless Chrome) and reading spectrograms, not by ear. Judging by ear is still to do.

## Recorded engine sound (2026-10-04)

- The synth still didn't sound real, so the default is now recorded: each firing pulse from the engine model plays one recorded pulse ("grain"). The synth stays selectable in the tuning panel for comparison.
- Chose per-pulse grains over steady-rpm loops so the engine model's lumpy idle, misfires and stalls still come through, in sync with vibration and smoke.
- Sources are CC0 Freesound recordings (see ASSETS.md). The main quad recording idles at about 11.5 pulses a second, so it's a four-stroke single at roughly 1,350 rpm, the same engine type the model simulates. Its revs are blips without a steady rhythm, so pulses above 1,500 rpm come from a second recording with a clean rev sweep. Rising-rev pulses play with the throttle open, falling-rev pulses with it closed.
- The second machine is level-matched to the first using both recordings' idle pulses, and the two are blended across 1,500 to 2,100 rpm.
- The starter's chug in the recording is irregular, so cranking is an engage sound followed by a crossfaded loop whose speed follows the cranking speed, rather than one grain per compression stroke.
- Cutting is done offline by `tools/slice_engine_audio.py` into one WAV plus a JSON index. WAV rather than MP3 so browser MP3 decoder delay can't shift the cut points.
- Using Freesound's MP3 previews for now; the original WAVs need a Freesound login.
- Choppiness fix: the first cut placed each cut a fixed 4 ms before the next thump's peak, but the attack starts earlier, so most grains ended with a scrap of the next thump. The slicer now finds each attack's start and cuts at the quietest zero crossing just before it, and rejects grains with an odd length or a loud tail (about a third of them). At playback each grain carries a few milliseconds of overlap and crossfades into the next, and every pulse carries its own cycle length from the engine model so its grain is stretched to fit exactly, which closes the gaps a rough idle used to leave.

## Engine sound from idle (2026-10-05)

- Feedback: acceleration still sounded very wrong on all of them; could the idle be extrapolated to high revs instead?
- Diagnosis: every rev sound so far came from a different machine than its idle, and played back faster or slower to fit each engine cycle. Resampling shifts the exhaust's resonances along with the firing rate, which a real engine doesn't do: its pipe and silencer resonate in the same place at any revs, and only the firings come quicker.
- New default engine sound, `pulse`: every firing plays a recorded idle firing at its own speed (`pulsePitchFollow` 0), cut off with a crossfade over 40% of a cycle when the next one fires. Below idle speed it's stretched as before. Each firing gets louder with revs, `(rpm / pulseRefRpm) ^ pulseRevLoudness`, capped at `pulseLoudnessCap`. An EQ that comes in with revs cuts the deep thump (low shelf at 160 Hz, up to -16 dB) and lifts the rasp (peak at 1.5 kHz, up to +8 dB), and a fixed 6 kHz low-pass tames the edges of the cut-off firings. `recorded` and `synth` are still selectable.
- Fitted against real engines rather than by ear: two recordings that each have an idle and revs from the same machine on the same microphone (the ATV behind the blue's idle, revving to 6,000 rpm, and the two-stroke motocross bike behind the yellow's idle, blipping to 3,700). For each, how the spectrum changes from idle to higher revs, in third-octave bands, compared with a render of the same quad held at the same revs. Average error, four-stroke: recorded mode 6.4 dB, pulse mode 3.4 dB. Two-stroke: recorded 8.2 dB, pulse 3.1 dB. Loudness now rises like the real engines too: +8, +12, +14 dB at 3,000, 4,500 and 6,000 rpm against the real +6, +10, +15 (four-stroke); recorded mode only managed +6, +8, +10.
- The same EQ settings fitted both engines. The yellow two-stroke needed its loudness to rise faster (`pulseRevLoudness` 1.1) and a lower cap (2) so it doesn't clip flat out at 10,500 rpm. Each quad has its own `pulseRefRpm` near its idle.
- Over a full rev range on all three: no gaps, fewer clicks than recorded mode on the red and yellow and about the same on the blue, and steadier loudness. A spectrogram shows continuous harmonics from idle to full revs, without the vertical streaks recorded mode's joins leave.

## Ride sounds and Doppler (2026-10-05)

Six parallel agents this round (engine load, ride sounds and Doppler, mud, pillion body language, her driving, a toolbox), each in its own worktree, merged as they finish.

- Each quad's sound (engine, tyres, splashes, thuds) reaches the listener through its own delay line set to distance over the speed of sound, so Doppler comes from the travel time rather than a computed pitch factor. It shifts everything the quad makes, needs no change to the engine worklet, and measures +2.9% / -2.9% for a 10 m/s pass-by (textbook: +3.0 / -2.8), and +2.7 / -2.6% on a riderless quad circling at 9.4 m/s in game. The delay is smoothed (80 ms) and limited to a 40 m/s closing speed so getting on and off doesn't chirp. A low-pass falling with distance stands in for air absorption. Quads are now heard with their real travel time (about 29 ms at 10 m).
- Ride sounds: looped filtered noise through built-in Web Audio nodes for the continuous layers (rumble, grass, gravel grit, water churn), following speed and the surface under each wheel (grass, dirt, mud, water shares); synthesised knocks and rattles; five CC0 recordings for squelches, splashes and landing thuds. Knocks and landings come from the physics suspension. The terrain is too smooth for many physics knocks, so small knocks come per metre travelled, and on a riderless quad from the same ground jolts that shake it. Wind for the rider rises with speed. Levels: tyres on dirt at 10 m/s about 10 dB under the engine at full throttle. Not judged by ear; grit brightness, wind level and squelch density are the likeliest to want a nudge.
- Her voice doesn't go through the delay path, so it has no Doppler.

## Sound cutting out for good (2026-10-06)

Jamie: the sound sometimes stops altogether, perhaps only in Firefox, with nothing in the console.

- Cause: the worklet turns each grain's and clip's time into a sample index at the audio context's rate, usually 48 kHz on a Mac, while the banks are 44.1 kHz WAVs resampled on decoding. The engine-off clip ends exactly at the end of `engine-bank` and `engine-bank-2t`, and at 48 kHz its last read lands one past the decoded data when the browser rounds the length down (Chrome and Firefox round differently, hence "maybe Firefox"). That read is `undefined`, the sample becomes NaN, the NaN sticks in the worklet's tone filter, and once it reaches the master every sound in the game is silent for good. No exception, so nothing in the console.
- Fix: every grain and clip read is clamped inside the decoded data. And as a backstop, a worklet that ever outputs NaN resets its filters, pipe and voices, outputs silence for that sample and warns once in the console ("Engine sound went NaN"), so if anything else ever does it, the sound recovers and there's a clue.
- Tests in `tests/engineWorkletNaN.test.js` load the real worklet in a sandbox at 48 kHz: a bank a sample short plays its last clip to the end without a NaN, and a NaN forced into a filter is cleared. Both fail on the old worklet.
- Not checked in Firefox itself: I couldn't reproduce it there, so this is the most likely cause found by reading, not a confirmed one.

## The UTV idles at its recording's speed (2026-10-07)

Jamie found the UTV rough and heavy, like the tractor. Two causes, one only in my renders:

- **The renders.** `sweep2.mjs` never told the engine model what was in the tank. A diesel engine (`runsOnDiesel`, `petrolFussy`) reads a missing `diesel` control as all petrol, so the render ran it soaked in petrol: roughness 0.32, misfires, beats 31% uneven, and a stall after full throttle. The game fuels it with red diesel, so none of that was in the game. ## The engine review: one rule per engine type (#851)

The review of 14 and 15 November 2026 found why every engine stops changing above about 3k rpm and fitted a fix to real rev recordings. It is kept in `tools/engine-review/` (README: what each tool does and how to run it), with the prototype worklet and `calibrated.json` beside them in `prototype/`, and the record with every number in `docs/briefs/engine-review.md`. The issues that build it (#852 to #859) port from these, not from memory.

- **Why they stop changing.** In pulse mode each firing plays an idle firing at its recorded speed, picked afresh each time, so above about 3k the firings come faster than a bang lasts and you hear the start of a different bang each time: no note, and no change in level or brightness past about 5k. The cars' rev grains also stop at about 3k (the Finch's at 5.4k).
- **The rule, per engine type**, fitted to real rev recordings of that type (note by the firing, and by the cycle for several cylinders, five rpms each, two seeds). Four-stroke singles: steadiness 0.2 (the first fit's note error, on the older worklet: 0.220 as now, 0.148). Two-stroke singles: 0.4 (0.193, 0.143). Diesels: nothing but their true cylinders and the cycle cut into firings (0.230 either way). Petrol fours: cylinders 4 dB and 0.2 of a period apart, each firing up to 0.1 late and 30% in strength (0.555, 0.477). Finch on its own revs: steadiness 0.3 (0.054, 0.044). Griffin on its own revs: the petrol four's values (0.597, 0.458). A click guard rejects any setting with a quarter as many clicks again as today's. **0.2 stays for four-stroke singles**, the value to port, because it is what Jamie heard and passed on page versions 4 to 6 ("quads and bikes work very well"). Run today on the prototype worklet it scores 0.114 against 0.3's 0.147 (0.193 as now), and the guard rejects it by 141 clicks against 137.5, so `calibrate.mjs` picks 0.3 and prints 0.2 as the best without the guard. The click count only catches steep low-frequency steps (it was the wrong measure for the Griffin's ticking), so missing by that margin is no reason to change a value passed by ear. `prototype/calibrated.json` holds 0.2.
- **Cars and diesels.** Cut each whole-cycle grain into its firings at the quietest millisecond near each even point (not the even point, which lands on a bang and ticks), with the engine's true cylinders (the Yak 3, the Crag 4: with one, a cycle fires once and plays a third or a quarter of itself). The Griffin 3 dB lower, or its loudest firings drive the soft clipper. The Tor keeps its own settings and takes only the fitted steadiness.
- **Tried and dropped.** The source-filter rebuild (split each recording into its resonances and bare pulses, play the pulses scaled by the revs): 3.35 dB off real revs at best against 3.81 as now, and 2.96 against 3.03 on the long recordings, so it did not earn its place. Steady 0.85 on the diesels: Jamie liked it by ear before the cylinders were fixed, but it measures worse (note error 0.362 against 0.230: real diesel revs are rough firing to firing) and overshoots on singles (about 0.9 where real ones are 0.3 to 0.6). Pitch beyond the recording on the singles: real tone changes only 1 to 4 dB across the revs, and anything that follows pitch overshoots, which is why it sounded artificial.
- **Left open for #855.** The top of the revs, where every engine just gets faster. Two candidates are rendered: shorter pulses from half the top revs, and "burst fits the gap" (a firing plays as recorded until the gap between firings is no longer than its burst, then shortens to fit, the resonances fixed; that point is 53% of the top on the Farm 300, 75% on the RB300, about 31% on the two-strokes, about 24% on the cars and the Crag). Both measure more ticks on the Griffin and the TZ65.
- **The shared bank.** Five machines share `engine-bank`; their distinct voices are an ear-judged question with nothing to fit them to, parked.

## The cars and the diesels fire every cylinder as they rev (#853)

- **Each cycle cut into its firings.** The Finch's, Griffin's, Vauclair's, Crag's and Yak's idles are whole cycles of a four (a three on the Yak). ## Revs with a note you can follow (#854)

- **Why.** Above about 3,000 rpm every firing was a fresh one from anywhere in the recording, so the revs had no note. Real four-stroke singles repeat firing to firing at 0.3 to 0.6 (ours 0 to 0.3), and real fours repeat once a cycle more than once a firing, because their cylinders differ. The fit and every number are in `docs/briefs/engine-review.md`; this ports the prototype's steadiness, cylinders and late-firing release into the game's worklet, leaving the prototype's other experiments (the top of the revs, the source-filter model) out.
- **Steadiness** (`pulseSteady`) comes in from 1.4 to 2.5 times the recorded idle: that share of firings replays the last one, or for an idle cut into firings starts the same cycle again at its end. It also holds the blend's firing on a bank that blends into real revs (the Hornet), as the prototype did.
- **Each cylinder its own** (`pulseCylGain`, `pulseCylTime`, from a fixed pattern over `pulseCylinders`, 0 taking `pulseSplit`), and each firing varied (`pulseFireJitter`, `pulseStrengthJitter`), from 1.2 to 2 times idle: at idle the recording carries its own engine's unevenness.
- **A late firing leaves no gap:** the one before plays on until it starts, reading further into its recording where it had been cut to fit its cycle. At a fifth of a period apart that never shows; the test spreads cylinders half a period apart, where a release at once left 5 ms of silence.
- **The random draws happen only where a setting is above 0**, so an engine without them plays exactly what it did before (the test holds the old output's fingerprint).
- **The values** are as approved by ear on the review page: singles 0.2, two-strokes 0.4, the Finch and the Tor 0.3, the Griffin cylinders 4 dB and 0.2 apart with firing variation 0.1 and 30%, the diesels none. The RB300 builds its revs from its idle alone, as heard on the page; the Hornet keeps its blend.
- **The fit can't settle 0.2 against 0.3 for the singles.** `calibrate.mjs` on the game's worklet gives 0.3 at 0.150 and 0.2 at 0.151; the prototype on today's banks picks 0.3 too (0.2 rejected by the click guard), and with six seeds the order and the guard both change again. - **The Griffin's fit has moved too:** on today's banks the game's worklet fits it best at 8 dB between cylinders (note error 0.368 against 0.528 with none), inside the click guard. The 8 dB pick was the one whose pops Jamie heard, so the approved 4 dB stands.

## Engines rise in pitch at the top (#855)

- **Why.** Near the top every engine only got faster: a firing was cut to fit the gap, so it never sounded higher. A real exhaust pulse shortens with the revs while the pipe's and body's resonances stay, and that is heard once the gap between firings is no longer than a firing's main burst (80% of its energy). The physics and Jamie's pick by ear (15 November 2026) are in `docs/briefs/engine-review.md`.
- **Measured per bank** by `tools/engine-bank-model.mjs` (from the review's `model.mjs`): up to 12 peaking filters from 1.5 times the idle's firing rate up over a 4-octave slope, from single firings, and the burst on the bare pulses, written into the bank's JSON as `model["<idle kind>/<cut>"]`. Bursts run from 13 ms (the Hornet) to 96 ms (the tractor); the Farm 300's 40 ms meets the gap at about 3,000 rpm, the cars' 17 to 31 ms at about a quarter of their top. Slicing a bank again drops it, and `tests/engineBanks.test.js` fails until the tool is run.
- **In the worklet** the bare copy of the idle's stretch is made once, 8,192 samples a block so the Plover's 49 s never stalls one (one copy for each running vehicle, as its bank's data already is; no bank is shared by two vehicles today); firings from it are summed apart, through the peaks and not the rev EQ. Rev firings blended in (the Hornet's), starters and shutdowns stay on the old path. A firing plays at `max(1, burst / gap)` times its speed, and any voice read faster low-passes its source as it reads it (sixth-order, at 0.8 of its own Nyquist): without it, a tone above that folded back as loud as the one it kept; with it, 65 dB under.
- **Faster than real time:** the filters as flat arrays, every voice the same shape and the grain pick working out each grain's distance once rather than on every comparison of its sort (a quarter of the time; the same order) keep `tools/render_engine_sound.mjs` as fast as before: 28 s of the TZ65 in 1.0 s (main 1.06) and the Plover in 1.1 s (main 0.96), start-up included.
- **Jamie on the first build (27 November 2026):** a little artificial, most on the Farm 300 near the top. Of six versions rendered for him (all or half the shortening, firings varied 3% or not, none or half the rev EQ back on the model's firings, and the old sound) he picked half the shortening, varied, with half the EQ. With none of the EQ the Farm 300's centroid had climbed only 121 to 168 Hz up the range (121 to 650 with all of it) and the top was about 5 dB louder. The rule itself stays testable at `pulseBurstFollow` 1, `pulseRateJitter` 0.
- **Ticks** (`ticks.mjs`, two runs each): with all the shortening the Griffin's ladder went from 0.97 and 1.17 a second to 2.2 to 2.9, the TZ65's from 2.5 and 3.4 to 8.4 to 9.8, bunched where firings first meet; capping a firing to fill its gap or fading it in longer made no difference, and shortening the plain recording ticked more (21.9 on the TZ65). With Jamie's pick: the TZ65's ladder 3.4 and 3.7, its revving 1.8 and 2.6 (1.3 before); the Griffin's ladder 1.75 and 2.1, its revving 0.24 and 0.80 (0.48 before). Real rev recordings in the banks measure 0 to 23 a second on the same count. The Griffin's idle has a spike in one recorded cycle that comes round up the revs (#1275).
