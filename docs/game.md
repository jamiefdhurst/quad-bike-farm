# Playing the game

Everything the game does, for a player, in more detail than the FAQ. The [FAQ page](../faq.html) (linked from the start screen) is the place to start: it answers the questions a player asks and folds the secrets away. The controls here are also in the game (`H`) and the FAQ, both read from `src/hud/controls.js`.

## The quads

- **Red quad**: Honda-style farm quad. Soft and steady, with a low warm idle around 1,150 rpm. Styled on a 1990s four-wheel-drive farm quad: black wing flares, twin rectangular headlights with an oil-cooler grille between them, racks with tie-down hooks, a pillion seat, a tow hitch on the back, and the real machine's switch pod on the left bar (lights, main beam, engine stop, start and a sliding choke): `P` cycles its lights.
- **Blue quad**: Yamaha-style farm quad. Idles higher (about 1,450 rpm warm), revs harder and higher, needs more choke from cold and runs lumpier until it's warm, warms a bit quicker and pulls a little harder. Styled on a 2000s four-wheel-drive farm quad (a Grizzly 660): big painted fenders with round headlights in black pods at the corners of the nose, a vertical-bar grille between them with the maker's badge, a steel bumper hung from the front rack's black mounts, flat grid racks front and back, a black body between the fenders, a tail lamp panel and a tow hitch on the back, silver slotted wheels and front disc brakes. The left bar has the real machine's switch pod (a grey lights rocker marked HI, LO and OFF, a red engine stop, a start button and the choke lever hanging under it), `P` cycles its lights like the red's, the thumb throttle moves under your thumb, and the diff lock button sits by the 2WD / 4WD switch. The racks are black powder-coat, not bare steel, so they grime rather than rust. Its idle comes from a different recorded machine, so it sounds different too.
- **Yellow quad**: a two-stroke sport quad, Blaster-style. It fires every revolution, so it buzzes rather than thumps, and ring-dings at idle. It's weak low down, then comes on hard around 6,000 rpm and revs past 10,000. It has almost no engine braking, and it slowly loads up its plug if you leave it idling (blip the throttle to clear it). It trails blue-white oil smoke. It's lighter and quicker, with an expansion-chamber exhaust and motocross bars that run level and then rise steeply, with a straight crossbar between the arms. It is the Tamura Hornet 200: the wheels are the edge of it, the plastics are small and the engine is out in the open, with the carb, the choke knob and the fuel tap on its left side. The headlamp is on the bars and turns with them, and a plain on / off rocker on the left bar (`P`) switches it. She can squeeze onto a slim pad at the back. It idles on a Yamaha DT 125 trail bike and revs on a Honda CR85 motocross bike ridden flat out.

- **Green quad**: a Kawasaki-style farm workhorse, older and heavier. A big lazy four-stroke that idles low and lumpy (it idles on a Yamaha Ténéré 250 recording, its quick firings stretched half way to its slow tickover so each thump has a short gap after it, and revs on kyles's Honda ATV pulling away, played through in order so it sounds like one engine revving), revs slowly but pulls hard low down, takes an age to warm up, wants plenty of choke, runs rougher than the others (at tickover: it evens out as it pulls) and engine-brakes hard. Slower at the top (about 33 mph), with a bigger tank. Styled on a 1990s four-wheel-drive workhorse (a KLF300 Bayou): deep green fenders with black rubber flares, a black tank cover, a short body that stops high at the front, with two square headlights and a slatted grille on a frame ahead of it inside a grey tubular cage with the winch (in its housing, a roller fairlead at the front) behind it, a back that leans (out at the bottom) with a tail lamp each side, a short side panel so the engine case shows below it, full tube racks front and back, a black seat, steel wheels with hub caps, a tow hitch with a drawbar and a pin, bars that rise in a V from a low middle with a round speedo on a bracket over the clamp, and the real machine's switch housing on the left bar with a lights rocker (`P`) and a sliding choke. A cracked mudguard is held with cable ties, and oil weeps from the engine case.
- **Orange quad**: a Suzuki LT250R-style two-stroke race quad. Lighter and lower than the yellow, revs to 11,500 and comes on harder still around 7,000, rear-wheel drive, barely any engine braking, and smokes like a chimney. It is the Viper Adder 250, a triangle of a quad: a narrow orange nose and tank shroud with three louvres, a blue seat that widens to the back, big flat triangles down each side, flat blade fenders over the front tyres and a broad duck-tail with wings that kick up behind it, all on a blue tube frame, with a short silver silencer under the tail. A square headlamp on the bars in a wire guard, a pod on the left bar with the lights rocker (`P`, a plain on / off switch like the yellow's), the choke lever and the kill switch, and no number plates. Single race seat, so she can't ride on the back (she can still start or drive it). It idles on a Honda CRM250 recording, so it sounds different from the yellow.

- **Kids' quad**: a Suzuki LT50-style 50cc two-stroke, the Viper Asp 50, two thirds the size of the others and faded pink with a faded blue seat. Bulky plastics (one big front body from the bars down to the fenders, deep fenders over fat square-block tyres, a thick flat seat, mud flaps), a small silver exhaust, and the little engine in the open: the carb with the choke knob on its left side (you have to be all but stopped to reach it) and the fuel tap beside it. A thumb throttle and a brake lever on the V bars, the key on the clamp and nothing else: no lamps and no switches. Stickers: the Viper badge on the seat, a "50" and a warning label on the front body, and a stripe on the side plates. Pull-start as well as the key (`O`), no reverse, one seat. It sounds like an angry wasp.

- **Loan quad**: the dealer's loan, a silver Tamura Ursa 450 after a Yamaha Kodiak 450. A fuel injected four-stroke automatic, always four-wheel drive, smooth and quiet with a quick fast idle that eases off as it warms, and almost no wear. **It has no choke**: the injection meters a cold start itself, so `Q` and `Z` do nothing on it and the choke has no knob, lever or debug setting. It has no fuel tap either (the fuel always flows, so `F` does nothing), and none of a carb's troubles: no carb icing, no spitting back, nothing to clean or jet. It is the blue's cousin in the bodywork (painted decks leaning back at the front with a black strip along each edge, black inner wings and sills and arch trims, slim angular lamps with a daytime strip, a black steel bumper, black alloy wheels, a bright silver muffler under the rear fender on the right, a dealer's sticker on the left rear fender), with lower bars than the blue's and its pod on the left bar: lights rocker (`P`), engine stop and start button.

The blue, orange, kids' and loan quads are automatics; the red, green and yellow are manual (below). All start cold. Through the CVT the red tops out at 6,200 rpm and the blue at 6,800 (the yellow revs past 10,000), but the engine works against the ground: it bogs a little as you pull away, cruises nearer 5,700 flat out, labours lower up hills and through mud, flares when the wheels spin or leave the ground, and engine-brakes on a closed throttle with the revs rising with speed downhill. Try climbing the steep bank by the yard on a stone-cold engine with the choke out and it will probably stall. Each quad has its own character, the same every game: how it idles, how rough it runs and how it shakes. The red is the smooth one; the blue idles fast and keen with a quicker buzz; the yellow ring-dings and buzzes through the bars more than the body; the green idles slow and lumpy with a big slow heave like a tractor; the orange hunts nervously and shakes harsh and hard. Where each quad is parked, where the jerry can has been left, and each quad's exact warm idle, roughness and starting fuel vary a little every game, picked from ranges in `src/vehicle/quadSpecs.js`:

| | `idleRange` (rpm) | `roughnessRange` | `fuelRange` (litres) | Body shake at idle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red | 1,050 to 1,150 | 0.04 to 0.12 | 2.5 to 11 of 12 | 2 mm, gentle |
| Blue | 1,400 to 1,550 | 0.20 to 0.30 | 1.2 to 8 of 11 | 3.5 mm, quick |
| Yellow | 1,500 to 1,800 | 0.35 to 0.50 | 1.5 to 8 of 9 | 3 mm, buzzy bars |
| Green | 800 to 900 | 0.55 to 0.70 | 3 to 13 of 14 | 12 mm, slow heave |
| Orange | 2,000 to 2,300 | 0.75 to 0.90 | 1.5 to 7.5 of 8 | 10 mm, harsh |

Running wears an engine and makes it rougher still, all the way to fully rough if you keep at it (about half an hour of hard riding). Past about 0.8 (its build roughness plus wear), it turns erratic: it stumbles through runs of misfires, losing nearly all its power for a moment and nearly dying, catches again sometimes with a bang and a puff of smoke, flares up at idle, and won't hold a steady tickover. Fully worn, that's a stumble every two or three seconds and it dies on you now and then, and the girls will tell you it needs looking at. A service from the toolbox brings it back to its own character. A well-worn engine also knocks while it is cold, a hollow piston slap at idle and pulling away that goes as it warms; it lives in the piston, so a service doesn't clear it, but a new piston and rings (the top-end rebuild, the big-bore kit) do.

**Mechanical sympathy.** Revving an engine hard before it has warmed up wears it several times faster, and the game keeps count of who was riding each quad while it wore: you, Amy or Jess. Amy tells Jess off when she's thrashing a cold engine, and tells you to go easy when you are (Jess cheers you on). Stood by a worn quad, a girl says who she reckons has been caning it, and she's right, because it comes from the real tally. A service wipes the slate.

The idle and roughness are printed in the console and shown in the tuning panel.

**Revs on the clutch vehicles.** The red, green and yellow quads, the old three-wheeler, the tractor and both Land Rovers are tied to the road when the clutch is up and a gear is in: the engine's revs are your speed over the gear's top speed times its full-throttle revs, whatever the throttle does (it decides the torque), so they climb and fall with your speed and each gear sounds different. Pulling away, with the clutch slipping or the road speed under idle's worth, they are still the throttle's, and the existing stalling and labouring apply. The automatics keep their CVT-style revs. In `engine.update` as `gearRpm` and `gearLock`.

**The farm that remembers.** Each time you load the game is the next day on the same farm, and the start screen says "Continue: day 4" (any click on it plays) with "New farm" beside it, which asks first and then deals a whole new farm. With no saved farm, clicking to play starts a new one as day 1. It saves in the browser every 30 seconds while you play and when you hide or close the tab, from the moment you first start playing. What carries over: which vehicles are on the farm, and each one's condition: hours and miles, wear, faults left unfixed, the toolbox settings, tyre pressures and tread, the fuel and its oil mix, the sump, the coolant, its quirks, where the two pods are fitted and the barn find's progress. Overnight a battery loses a little (or goes flat if the key was left on with the engine stopped; a running quad is fine) and half the mud dries and drops off. What's new each day: where everything is parked, the weather, the temperature and the time of day. The bucket list keeps its ticks, the records board its bests (marked "today" when set today), and Amy's logbook its last 40 entries, earlier days' marked "Yesterday" or "Day 3". A wall calendar in the barn, between the two chalkboards, has a vintage garage pin-up across the top and the month under it: each day gone is crossed off in red and today is circled, with a new page every thirty days. Once it is evening (from 17:00) and both girls are back at the barn (the bell calls them), pressing `E` on the calendar calls it a day: a card comes up with Amy's logbook for the day (the last five entries), what you ticked off the bucket list today with the running total, and how far you rode, while each girl says a general goodnight (`E`, Enter or a click carries on after a second); then the screen fades to black, says the day is done, the farm saves and the next day loads (click Continue to play it). The girls keep half their favour, all you taught them, a romance once she is yours, and a falling-out not made up leaves them cool the next morning. `?seed=N` ignores the save and never writes it, so a seeded game repeats exactly. The Girls tab's "Both girls and the farm" shows the day and when it last saved, with "Save the farm now" and "Wipe the farm"; in the console `save` says the same, `save copy` copies the save, `save load <json>` stores one to play from the next reload, and `save wipe` forgets it. "Farm that remembers" in the Tune tab sets the overnight numbers and when you can call it a day; `save day` in the console, or "Call it a day now" in the Girls tab, does it at any time.

**Which vehicles are here.** Every vehicle is in one pool: the red, blue, yellow, green and orange quads, the kids' quad, the loan quad, the mini moto, the old three-wheeler, the tractor, the UTV and the two Land Rovers (the Series III and the 200TDi). Each new farm has between four and all thirteen of them, usually somewhere in the middle, with at least two of the five regular quads so there's one for each girl. The farm keeps the same ones from day to day. Anything not on the farm is simply missing: no tractor means no tow chain, mudguard rides or go on the tractor, and the bale trailer stands there with nothing to pull it. The same goes for the UTV and the Land Rovers, and their bucket-list items are left off the list. The trailers always stand where they are.

She starts beside a random quad in the yard. No more than four start in the yard (hers among them), spread round it 8 to 12 m apart at any angle, sometimes facing the other way; the tractor, UTV and Land Rovers never do. The rest are left somewhere else on the farm (each quad has a 40% chance of that even when there's room in the yard): by the field gate, out from the yard, by the south, east, north or west hedge, down the west hedge, in the far corner, by the straw bales, out past the far bend, at the bottom of the track, in the middle of the field, or out east (the Dutch barn's bay, the road, the barley or wheat field, the field shed). The tractor, UTV and Land Rovers are never left in a tight spot (the Dutch barn's bay, the road), and keep further from their neighbours. Nothing starts on a trailer, the fuel tank or bowser, the barn or its doorway, the silage, a straw bale, the paddock, the jump, the drag strip, the bog or the track: a random nudge that would put it there is tried again. The console says where each one is. The browser console prints the layout's seed; add `?seed=<number>` to the URL to get the same layout again.

Everything below applies to any of them.

## Starting a cold engine

The engine starts stone cold and won't fire without choke.

1. Walk up to the quad and press `E` to get on.
2. Hold `Q` to pull the choke to 100%. The yellow lever on the left handlebar moves.
3. Turn the fuel tap ON with `F`. The quads are parked with it OFF; forget and it'll start, run for a few seconds on what's in the carburettor, then stumble and die. **Done.** When both are stood about, one dares the other to a race: they pick two quads, start them, line up at cones by the yard, count down and race one lap of the track. The winner crows, the loser has excuses, and the score is kept: the one well ahead goes on about it.
4. Press `K` for ignition. The green IGN light comes on and the blue TEMP light shows the engine is cold.
5. Hold `R` to crank. It should catch within a couple of seconds and settle into a high, lumpy idle. The whole bike shakes. **Done.** On a cold or wet day a girl stood about near a quad that's idling walks over and holds her hands over its engine; her friend often joins her, and they complain if it's switched off.
6. Let it warm. With the choke left on, the idle creeps up as it warms. Ease the choke off with `Z`, a notch or two at a time. Too early and it stumbles and dies. Too late and it starts to hunt, runs rich and eventually floods and stalls. **Done.** With a girl on the back and you riding along, she reaches round and opens the throttle for five to ten seconds over your hand. Jess keeps doing it; Amy does it once and apologises.
7. Once it's warm (TEMP light out, a couple of minutes) it idles low and steady with no choke. **Done.** Amy coughs and waves away the smoke from a running two-stroke when she's downwind of it, and hangs further back following one; Jess loves it and tucks in close.

Things to know:

- A warm engine with lots of choke is hard to start.
- Braking hard from speed while it's still cold can stall it. Roll off early and brake gently until it's warm.
- If you flood it, take the choke off and crank with the throttle held open (`W` + `R`) to clear it.
- The bike keeps idling when you get off, and you can watch and hear it shake from outside. With nobody on it, it also jolts over the ground as it moves, harder the faster it goes, so a quad driving itself round in circles visibly bounces. Riding it, the shake is as it always was.

## Mud

The track and especially its puddles are slippery: grip drops from 2.2 on grass to 0.7 on the track and 0.42 in a puddle (all scaled by tyre pressure). The yellow quad is rear-wheel drive and spins its back wheels if you give it everything in the mud; the red and blue have a switch on the bars (`G`) between 2WD and 4WD and start every game in 2WD: in 2WD they spin their back wheels in the mud like the yellow, and the bars are lighter and quicker. 4WD grips in the mud at the cost of a little steering weight and a few per cent of the drive. The blue's diff lock (`U`, which brings 4WD with it) pulls hardest in the mud and pushes wide on grass. The switch itself shows the mode (the rocker's position, and the blue's lock button sinks when it's in); the dash doesn't, but the analogue and digital pods have a 4WD lamp (and LOCK on the blue), and a quad stays as it was left. The girls sometimes remember to put it in 4WD when they take the wheel (Amy more than Jess); the ones who forget get bogged, work out why, flip it, and the other says so. The green and loan quads are always four-wheel drive. Tyres throw clumps and puddles splash, wheels leave ruts that fade over a couple of minutes, and the quads get muddier the more you ride, dark and wet at first and drying lighter. To wash one, pick up the hose on its reel at the south end of the petrol tank (`E`; it reaches 25 m), and hold `E` at a quad: the wheels come clean first, then the bodywork, over about 8 seconds for a filthy one, leaving the paint wet and shiny until it dries. `G` puts the hose back. Mind where you point it if she's about. Each quad's Mud folder in the tuning panel has the spray and rut settings, and "Mud on it" cleans it off.

The weather's different every game, but mostly sunny (about 70% of games, with sunny intervals another 15%); only now and then is it overcast, drizzly, raining or foggy (the console says which; `?weather=rain` and so on forces one, and the seed repeats it). Showers come and go during a game, more on a showery day: the sky darkens and the mist thickens as rain comes in, rain streaks past you with its own patter, and the wind pushes the exhaust smoke harder. Rain wets the ground, which darkens and dries slowly after: wet grass and especially wet mud are slipperier, and the bogs are worse. The girls have something to say about the day, and about the rain coming and going.

There are three bogs: two on the track and one in the low ground of the field, dark churned patches with a wet shine. Go into one slowly, or stop in it, and the mud drags you down and grips the wheels: keep the throttle on and they churn and dig you in, and once you're sunk past halfway it holds you against anything the tyres can do. Charge it with a run-up and you'll get through, much slower than you went in. Stuck but not too deep, rock it: forwards, backwards, forwards, and each change of direction works it a little looser. Too deep for that, use the green quad's winch: look at the green and press `X` to take the hook off it, walk the cable (30 m of it) to the stuck quad and press `E` on it to hook on, then hold `X` near the green (its remote is on a lead) or sitting on it to wind in. It pulls the stuck quad out towards the green. `E` on the hooked quad unhooks it, `X` while carrying the hook puts it back. The girls steer round the bogs on their own drives, but following you they'll go where you go; stuck on her own drive, a girl gives up after a couple of goes and calls for you (below), and one on the back of yours will tell you what she thinks.

**Calling you to help.** A girl who has bogged her quad stays up on it, stands on the footboards and waves, shouting where she is (the nearest of about twenty named spots: "I am stuck by the ford!"); one who has tipped hers climbs out and waves beside it. The farm map rings her in red. She shouts every 14 s or so with you within 80 m, every 45 s beyond. Get her out with the winch, the tractor's chain, or by holding `V` to shove the quad while she works the throttle (a shove loosens a bog sunk less than 0.6; deeper wants the winch or chain); a tipped quad is righted with `E`. Her favour of you goes up by 2, and by up to 1 more the sooner you came (full inside a moment, nothing extra after 2 minutes). Left for 3 minutes without you coming near, it goes down by 1.5; at 4 minutes she gives up and gets down (down 0.5 if you never came). Girls' tab, "Make her do": "bogs her quad" and "tips her quad" (she has to be driving); the console's `girl amy bog` and `girl amy tip`. Tuning: "Girl calling for help".

The ground changes as you play. Wherever quads keep driving over the field (you, and the girls on their usual routes), the grass wears: it darkens, goes soft like the track, and loses grip. Keep at it, with the ground wet, and a new bog forms there, the same dark glossy mud, deep enough to stick you. Rain swells the worn patches into bogs and a long dry spell shrinks them away; left alone the grass grows back in about ten minutes in the dry and takes far longer in the rain. The yard, the drag strip, the barn floor (and the floors of the Dutch barn and the field shed) and the farm road are hard and never wear; the stubble fields wear like the grass. The girls plan their laps round new bogs as well as the old ones, so late in a long game the field isn't the one you started with.

**The east fields.** A gateway in the grass field's east hedge, just past the track's far bend, has a cattle grid across it (steel tubes over a dark pit, with post-and-rail wings closing the rest of the gap, and a steel gate across the road just beyond it, hedge to hedge, shut at the start of a game: get off and open it before you can ride through): cross it and the whole vehicle buzzes, the handlebars and your view shake, and the bars knock under the tyres, more the faster you go. Beyond it the gateway opens onto a tarmac farm road that runs straight east for about 100 m between hedges to a round hardstanding at the far end, and on to a big pair of galvanised gates in the farm's east hedge, shut for now. Either side of it is a stubble field, each about 120 by 75 m, reached through gaps in the road's hedges or round the open ends by the hardstanding. They're different fields: the north one was barley, its stubble greyer, shorter and thicker, drilled north to south, and it sits in a shallow hollow (about 1.3 m deep in the middle) that's darker and damper, holds the wet and turns to bog sooner in the rain; the south one was wheat, golden, drilled east to west, and rises about 4 m towards its far hedge. Both have the dark tramlines of the sprayer. Stubble is firm: dry, it grips like dry grass but a quad runs on quicker over it (about a quarter less drag); wet, it's greasier than wet grass. Tarmac grips about 30% harder than grass (15% in the wet) and rolls easier still, and the tyres squeal on it when you slide through a corner, brake hard or spin them up. Tyres carry mud off the track, the bogs and any ground you've churned soft, and leave it on the road in brown smears along their lines, most of it in the first 60 m; a smear is slippery like mud, the rain washes it off in a few minutes, and in the dry it stays. The girls drive, wander and leave quads out here too, going round by the gateways on foot and when they bring a quad home. On the tarmac the girls ride about 70% faster than they do on grass, and say so: Jess whoops as she joins the road and eggs you on while you ride it with her (or with her on your back); Amy tuts at the road being easier, and tells you off if you squeal the tyres. The tuning panel has "Surfaces", "Road mud", "Tyre squeal" and "Tarmac" folders, and the tyre squeal's volume is in the audio settings.

**Out in the east fields.** At the far end of the road, in the corner of the barley field by the hardstanding, is a Dutch barn: steel stanchions, lattice arches and a curved tin roof gone rusty, open along the front and the west end, with a block wall and sheeting along the back and the east end. Thirty round bales are stacked three high in its two east bays; the west bay is clear, and a quad is sometimes left in it. Two more bales lie on the floor at the front, and two out in the open beside it. In the barley field's far corner, by the grass field, is an old stone field shed with a rusty tin roof and an open doorway on its south side. A muck heap waits to be spread in the wheat field by its first gateway (you can ride up it), and a galvanised water trough stands by the barley field's second gateway, the ground poached round it. No rain falls under either roof, so a quad parked under one stays dry, though only the main barn keeps an engine warm. When it rains, a girl out east runs for whichever cover is nearest: she sits on one of the bales at the front of the Dutch barn, or stands just inside the shed's doorway, out of the wet. On a fine day the two bales beside the Dutch barn are somewhere to sit, and count as "the bales" when she asks to be taken there. A quad can have been left in the Dutch barn, along the road, out in either stubble field or by the field shed, and a jerry can can start by the Dutch barn or the shed.

**The hill.** The field gate in the grass field's south hedge starts tied open (look at it and press `E` to shut it, and again to open it). Through it a green lane, sunk between banks with tall hedges on top, runs south about 55 m down to a ford across a shallow stream, muddy on both approaches. Beyond the stream a rough hill pasture climbs about 19 m in a series of banks: gorse on the steep ones (you bump off it), bracken and tussocks in the rough, a small oak wood up the west side, and from the top a view back over the whole farm. The banks are the steepest ground in the game, up to about 38 degrees. Ride straight up them: side-on across them, a quad with you aboard rolls over at about 35 degrees of lean, sooner with someone on the back (`T` puts it back on its wheels), and nose-up it goes over backwards past about 45. Stop on a bank off the throttle and it rolls back down: hold `S`, or set the parking brake with `Space`. Take the ford slowly: above about 15 mph the bow wave drowns the engine. The east end of the pasture is the gentler way up, about 25 degrees at its steepest. The girls don't take quads up there. Along the lane there is no hedge on either side, only its banks, and halfway down each bank is eased into a ramp: to the west a wet meadow with a pond (ruts of mud down to the water, oaks and reeds round it) and to the east a rolling sheep field with two humps to ride up, a drystone wall across it with a gap in it, and thorn bushes. Following you, the girls come through the gateways too. Following you, they come down the lane and through the ford, then pull up at the foot of the hill and wait (Amy won't go up; Jess says Amy won't let her). On the back with you, they tell you when it gets steep. "riderTopple" in each vehicle's tuning folder sets how hard the rider's weight tips it.

**The wood and the Hollow Run.** The grass field's north hedge has a field gate of its own, tied open like the south one (look at it and press `E` to shut it and open it again). Through it is a hedged wood the width of the grass field and 120 m deep: about 170 oaks with trunks you can't ride through, hazel and holly between them, and open glades. A short muddy track runs in from the gate and joins the Hollow Run, a narrow loop (3.4 m across, against the Home Loop's 4.6) about 590 m round that winds back and forth through the trees in long rows with tight turns at the ends. It is a different lap from the Home Loop: four whoops (small humps, 0.5 m high, one after another) on the first straight, bends banked up on the outside, a broad swell the middle rows climb over (about 3 m), two bogs, and five logs lying across the track in pairs for a chicane, each leaving a gap about as wide as a quad. There is a pond in the middle of the wood with reeds round it, shin to knee deep at the edge like the meadow's: it drowns an engine the same way. The Home Loop and the Hollow Run are named on the farm map. A girl following you waits just inside the gate rather than driving in among the trees, and they do not ride the Hollow Run themselves. No race on it yet (TODO). The wood's east side lines up with the barley field's north hedge, so it can grow east along the stubble fields later.

**The stream after rain.** The stream follows the weather: in steady rain it comes up over five or six minutes (the water stands up to about 22 cm higher, so the ford is nearly twice as deep and wider across), runs browner and faster, and drops back slowly over a dry spell of ten minutes or more. A quad ploughing through the ford when it is up drowns more easily. A girl stood near you says when it is up and when it has gone down. A game that starts on a wet day starts with the stream partly up. "Stream" in the Tune tab sets how fast it rises and falls and how high it gets; the Girls tab and the console's `weather.stream` set the level.

**The sheep.** A second, smaller flock of five grazes in the sheep field beside the lane, on its big side south of the drystone wall, and behaves the same way (`fieldCount` in the "Sheep" tuning). Nine sheep graze on the pasture past the stream, somewhere on its gentler ground each game. They put their heads down to eat, drift a few metres, bunch up again and call to each other now and then, each in her own voice. Ride, walk or bring a girl near and they move off, from further away the faster you come, and the others run with them for a bit. They stay on the pasture (they don't go in the gorse, the wood or the stream) and can't be hurt, so riding through them just scatters them. "Sheep" in the Tune tab sets how many, how keen they are to bunch, how easily they are scared and how often they call. The gate to the lane is still tied open: shutting it and herding are on the TODO list.

## Tinkering

Pick up the red toolbox from the workbench in the barn (`E`), take it to a quad that's switched off and press `E` to get the spanners out (look at the workbench and press `E` to put it back). The panel has six adjustments (seven on the yellow and orange, which have a jet kit), each with its current and stock values and a note on the trade-off:

- **Idle screw**: the warm idle, within safe limits.
- **Carb mixture**: leaner pulls a bit harder and uses less fuel but runs rougher; richer is easier from cold but smokier and thirstier, and fully rich slowly fouls the plug.
- **Main jet** (yellow and orange only): the jet kit. A two-stroke wants a bigger jet in the cold and a smaller one in the heat; the stock jet is right at 12 °C on an ordinary day, and the panel tells you what the day's temperature and damp want (rain and wet ground thin the air, so a damp day wants a smaller jet too). Jetted spot on it is the cleanest power the engine makes (about 6% more pull and a smoother tickover). A size or so out is merely a bit lean or rich, but jetted well lean for the day (a stock jet on a frosty morning, or a smaller one still) it runs hot, scuffs, nips up and seizes, slower than on straight petrol but the same way. Too rich is sluggish and smoky. Three sizes either way of stock. Amy says when you've gone too lean for the day, or got it right.
- **Throttle stop (speed limiter)**: farm quads come with it screwed in. Each turn out adds about 7% top speed (fully off is about +20%: the red goes from 35 to 42 mph), plus a few more revs and more fuel.
- **Rev limiter**: 5% a click, six clicks either way (about 30% up or down). Raise it and it revs on higher and louder flat out, with the dash redline moving too, but revs past what the engine was built for wear it faster (flat out, 10% over wears it about twice as fast, 30% over about four times, so it needs a service sooner); the top speed is the throttle stop's job. Each game a quad's full-throttle revs also start a few per cent either side of its usual (about 4%; `?seed=` repeats them), so the panel shows what this one came with.
- **Clutch springs**: softer bites sooner, stiffer later; it always bites above the idle.
- **Tyre pressures**: softer grips better (especially in mud) but bounces more. Below the stock setting is a **tyre pump and gauge** with a row per wheel: click − to let some air out of one tyre or + to pump it up (half a psi a click), so you can run the rear soft for mud and the front hard, or level an odd one out. Let a tyre right down (under about 2 psi) and it squashes off the rim: the bars pull towards it and it drags.

**Silencer off** (`X`) is a separate job: the quad gets a lot louder and a more open, rasping tone, about 8% more power, and it crackles and bangs far more when you shut the throttle at speed. `X` again puts it back. Jess loves it; Amy covers her ears and tells you to turn it off.

**The tractor and the UTV** open the same panel with the toolbox, but it only shows what a diesel has: the idle screw, the clutch springs and the tyre pressures (no carb mixture, throttle stop or rev limiter), a service that also changes the oil, the air filter, the throttle cable, the tyres and, on the UTV, the drive belt. There's no carb or silencer, and the UTV has no pods; the tractor takes them, on a post beside its dials. Petrol in either one is pumped out from the panel. The tractor's and UTV's `Y` dipstick works as on the quads.

**The tractor's looks.** A red-and-grey Hartley 35, after a Massey Ferguson 35: a rounded red bonnet with a raised lid down its crown over a grey three-cylinder diesel left open below it, with the cast manifold, the injection pump and two blue-banded filters on its left; a tall grey grille in two banks of slats either side of a strip, with the maker's name on the bonnet's sides; round headlamps in red cups on the sides of the nose; a red air cleaner stack with a round cap; an upright exhaust outside the bonnet on the left with a flap on top. Behind the bonnet the cowl (the fuel tank) slopes up to the driver with the instruments set straight into its red metal, no housing and no plastic: a big rev counter with its hours drum and the PTO speed marked, oil pressure, water temperature and fuel in chrome bezels, four warning lamps in a row, a brass ignition switch, the lights switch and a small data plate, and a thin hand throttle lever over its quadrant at the right; the steering wheel is a thin black three-spoke on a raked column through a rubber boot. A red pressed-steel pan seat with a grey cushion sits between two guards over big lugged rear wheels, with ball-topped gear levers standing out of the grey gearbox casting through cover plates and gaiters, grid-plate footplates and plain pedals, and the lift arms, top link, PTO stub and drawbar at the back. The wheels are plain pressed steel painted grey with a cast hub on a ring of studs. It's worn: paint rubbed to primer and rust along the guards' lips and the bonnet's sides, oil weeping from the gearbox and mud on the front pedestal. The front wheels turn with the steering, with their steering arms swinging on the inside. About 3 m long and 1.9 m to the exhaust's tip, smaller than the old model (a 28 inch rear rim, not a 36), so the steering wheel, the dash and the seat are lower than they were.

**Pods** (`Y`, `U`, `O`): at a switched-off quad the panel says which pod is on it and which are in the toolbox. `Y` fits the analogue pod and `U` the digital one (each takes about three seconds, and the quad or tractor must have none already); `O` takes the one that's there off, back into the toolbox. To move a pod you take it off one quad and fit it to another.

**Air filter.** Every quad has one, and it slowly clogs with mud and puddle splashes while you drive (about seven minutes of hard mud to choke it; splashing through puddles is a bit worse; stood still or on clean ground, not at all). Dust off the dry stubble fields clogs it five times faster than mud, whether it is your own or the cloud of a quad ahead (about 80 seconds to choke it). A clogged filter makes it run rich, so it smokes darker and fouls, and it loses power at the top end (up to 30% at full revs; hardly anything at idle). At a switched-off quad with the toolbox, **hold `I`** (or the button) for three seconds to clean it out; let go and it starts again. The panel says how clean it is. In `src/vehicle/airFilter.js`; the numbers are `clogRich` and `clogPowerLoss` in each quad's engine folder.

**Wet and binding brakes.** Drive through the ford or a deep puddle and the brakes get wet: for a few seconds they barely bite, and the quad runs on when you stand on the pedal. They dry as you drive, quicker if you keep dabbing the brake. Now and then (about one quad in five) a quad has stood so long that its brake binds: it drags as if the handbrake were half on, and frees with a clunk after a few metres of riding. The parking brake still holds either way. The Vehicles tab shows the wetness under Running and both under Problems, and "Put it right" clears them.

**A frozen throttle cable.** On a frosty morning a quad left out in the open may have water frozen in its throttle cable (about seven in ten do). Either it won't open past a crack, so the engine idles and little more, or it opens and then won't shut, so it stays where you left it. The ice goes as the engine warms (a minute or two of running), as the sun gets on it or as the day mildens, and it comes back if the quad stands out in a hard frost with the engine off. Quads in the barn or the lean-to never freeze. A girl switches off a quad whose throttle has frozen open. "Frozen throttle cable" in the Tune tab sets how likely, how fast it freezes and thaws; the Vehicles tab's Problems folder and the console's `iceCable` set and clear it.

**Diesel waxing.** In a hard frost (below about -2 degrees, worst from -6) the diesel in the tractor, the UTV and the Land Rovers waxes: the paraffin comes out of the fuel and clogs the filter. One left out in the open on a hard frosty morning may start the game partly waxed, and one stood out with the engine off waxes in about twelve minutes at the worst. Waxed, it loses power, runs rough and cuts out, and from about a third waxed it gets harder to start, until solid, when it won't catch at all. It is never damaged. In the barn it is fine. A running, warm engine melts it, so does a milder spell or a little sun, and so does a dose of anti-gel: take the bottle by the coolant (pick it up with `E`), look at the vehicle and hold `Y` to treat the tank. Treated fuel doesn't wax and dissolves what has; topping up with untreated diesel thins it out. Petrol never waxes. Amy knows what it is and says so; Jess thinks the engine has given up. "Diesel waxing" and "Anti-gel bottle" in the Tune tab set it; the Vehicles tab has "diesel waxed" under Running and "waxed diesel" and "anti-gel in the tank" under Problems; the console has `dieselWax`.

**Chain drive.** The sport quad, the mini moto and the old three-wheeler turn their back wheels with a chain, and it needs looking after. It oils off as you ride (a dry chain stretches faster) and stretches until it hangs slack: from about a third slack it slaps the chainguard over bumps, and when it is nearly off the sprocket, give it a handful of throttle and it jumps off, the engine races and nothing drives the wheels. Amy and Jess notice both, Amy knowing what it is. In the yard `N` in the toolbox puts it back on the sprocket and gives it a squirt of oil, which gets you home, but only the workbench takes the stretch out: "Adjust and oil the chain" is the eleventh job, on the minus key. Slacken the axle nuts, draw the axle back to a finger of play, oil it a little and do the nuts up; too slack, too tight or too dry and some of it comes back. How slack it is and how dry it is shows in the Vehicles tab (Running, and Problems, where "Put it right" clears it) and the console's `chain` path, and the farm keeps both overnight.

**Crash damage.** Hit something hard (a gatepost, a wall) or land badly off the ramp and something may bend. A hit on a front wheel can bend that wheel's track rod: the bars are pulled to that side and the wheel toes out of line where you can see it. A hit or a landing can bend a rim: that wheel wobbles once a turn, you lose a little drive, and you can feel it. The harder the knock, the likelier and the worse. Neither can be mended in the yard, so there is no toolbox button: at the barn bench, "Mend the crash damage" (the `[` key) has you jack it up, spin the wheels and sight along the rods (it tells you which is bent), take the wheel and rod off, straighten the rod or fit a straight rim (firm, not forced) and do the nuts up. Done carelessly it comes back a little bent. The girls remark on the pull or the wobble, and a quad can start the day already bent from yesterday. Braking hard and bumping something slowly do nothing, and parked quads are never hurt. "Crash damage" in the quad's own Tune folder sets how hard a knock has to be and how likely each part is to bend; "rod" and "rim" are in Problems on the Vehicles tab, with a "Hit a post head-on" button, and the console's `faults` has both.

**Valve clearances.** Every four-stroke's valve clearances wear wide as it runs: a tappet tick from the top of the engine that gets louder over the game and costs a little power, and which the girls remark on. At the workbench, "Set the valve clearances" (the equals key) uses a feeler gauge: look first to learn whether the gap is wide, right or too tight, then slacken the locknuts, turn the screws until the gauge just drags and do the locknuts up. Turn too little and it still ticks; turn too far and the valves are too tight, which is silent but makes the engine start badly when hot and lose power until it is set again. Two-strokes have none. "Valves too tight" and the valve fault level are in the Problems folder on the Vehicles tab, and the console has `valvesTight`.

**An air leak at the carb boot.** Now and then the rubber intake boot between the carb (or throttle body) and the engine perishes and splits on any petrol vehicle; diesels have no carb to leak at. A small split makes the idle rise and fall by itself in a slow hunt, which settles once you open the throttle, the mixture runs lean, and when you lift off at speed it pops in the exhaust. It gets worse the longer the engine runs, the girls remark on it, and the toolbox cannot touch it: at the workbench, "Renew the intake boot" (the semicolon key) has you spray water round the rubber to find the split (the revs jump where it sucks), then undo the clips, fit a new boot and nip the clips up snug. Clips left loose or over-tightened leave a faint hiss. "Intake boot" shows its level in the Problems folder on the Vehicles tab (`leak` in the console's faults), and the leak settings in each engine's tuning set how lean it runs, how much it hunts and how many pops it adds (how often it starts is in the quad's Faults group).

**A worn clutch.** The four manual quads wear their clutch plates when you slip them: holding the clutch at the bite with the throttle open, pulling away up a bank with the pedal half up, or creeping along in first or second. Do it enough and the plate wears. A worn clutch slips under power: the revs flare without the speed and the quad hardly pulls, more the worse it is and the more throttle you give it, and the girls say it smells hot. It never mends itself and the toolbox cannot touch it: at the workbench, "Fit a new clutch plate" (the quote key) has a look at the plates first. Some quads start a game with a worn clutch. The Vehicles tab's Problems folder sets and clears it; "Clutch" is under the faults in the Tune tab and the console's faults path.

**Dust.** Ride fast over the stubble fields when the ground is dry and the quad throws up a tan cloud that hangs along your track. It thins out as the ground gets damp and is gone once it is properly wet; grass, mud and the road raise none, and nor does crawling. Following a dusty quad is horrible: Amy drops well back (2.2 times her usual gap) and says so; Jess tucks in close (0.6 times), coughs and loves it. Anyone in the cloud, you included, breathes it into the air filter. The tuning panel has a "Dust" folder.

"Back to stock" puts everything back exactly, silencer included. Engines also wear as they run (faster when worked hard), getting rougher: a lumpy, hunting idle and a lot more shake. The panel says how it's running, and **Service it** (`V`) clears the wear and leaves it as sweet as that quad gets. Changes last for the game. `G` puts the toolbox down; you can't ride while carrying it. She has opinions about all of this.

**Engine mounts.** The tractor, the UTV and the Land Rovers have their engines on soft rubber mounts, so the engine rocks on its own: you feel a quick shudder at idle that is not the frame's, and a lurch as it catches and as it stops. The quads' single-cylinder engines are bolted close to the frame and thump the whole thing instead. Mounts perish with use (a fifth fault, listed under Problems on the Vehicles tab, cleared by "Put it right"): worn ones let much more of the shake through to the seat and the bars, and clonk when you stab the throttle on or off. The girls notice it by feel (Amy knows what it is, Jess says it has character) and may know one had it yesterday. In the toolbox, `M` nips the bolts up (a rough fix); at the workbench, renew the engine mounts for good. Tuning: the vehicle's Vibration folder (`mountGain` and friends) and "Faults" (`mountsEvery`, `mountsSeconds`).

**Faults differ per quad.** Each farm deals every quad its own luck: for each of the five faults (puncture, belt, cable, carb, engine mounts), how often it starts and how fast it gets worse, each from 0.4 to 2.5 times the usual rate, picked at random (from the layout seed, so `?seed=` repeats it). So one quad might be belt-prone and slow to puncture, another the reverse. The tuning panel shows the quad you're looking at, under "Faults (live)", and `proneSpread` sets the range.

## The day

Each game starts at its own time, morning or afternoon, and the day runs on about twelve times faster than real time. The sun crosses the sky from the east through the south to the west, the light goes golden late in the afternoon, the sun sets about a quarter past five and the dusk fades into a blue night that lasts the rest of the game. After dark the sky fills with stars (cloud hides them) and the moon comes up: each game has its own phase, and a full one rises at sunset, crosses the south at midnight and gives enough light to ride by, where a new moon leaves only starlight. A clock, a thermometer and a barometer hang on the barn wall past the end of the workbench (the barometer drops ahead of rain), and the dashes' clocks keep the same time.

**A day that changes, and the forecast board.** About two games in five the weather turns during the day: sunny or sunny intervals cloud over to overcast, drizzle or rain; overcast turns to drizzle or rain; rain eases to drizzle, overcast or sunny intervals; fog burns off. Snow stays as it is. The turn is dealt from the game's seed at the start (`?seed=N` repeats it), happens between one and five game hours in, and takes about three quarters of a game hour: the sky and the wind blend across, the rain comes on part-way through, the barometer follows and the day's warmest moves a degree or two (wetter is cooler). Under the clock in the barn hangs the forecast board, a slate with chalk pictures for the morning, the afternoon and the evening, the current part boxed and the past ones faded. It is right about whether the day turns four times in five; otherwise it promises a turn that never comes or misses one that does. Amy reads it and tells you what it says; Jess hardly looks. Both say something as the sky turns, and say so when the board never mentioned it. `?weather=` forces a kind and the day then stays put, as does setting the weather by hand. "Forecast" in the Tune tab sets how often it turns, when, how fast and how accurate the board is; the Girls tab's "Both girls and the farm" shows what the board says and what the day will do, with a button to turn it now; the console's `weather.forecast` and `weather.turn` do the same.

**The barn cat.** One tabby cat lives on the farm: it prowls the yard and sits washing, and when an open quad's engine is still warm from a run (or the sun is on its seat) it goes over, jumps up and curls up asleep there, purring if you are close. It stays asleep however near you walk: look at it and press `E` to shoo it off, and it hisses, hops down and runs, and keeps off that quad for a few minutes. Starting the quad, sitting on it or revving anything beside it also sends it off, and it runs from a vehicle coming at it fast. Now and then it goes over to a girl who is stood about and rubs round her. Jess talks to it and says so when it settles on something; Amy says she does not like cats, and then gets caught stroking it. When it is raining, snowing, frosty or dark it goes into the barn by the door and sleeps on the floor. It is all procedural (a built-up tabby with white paws, walking, running, sitting and curling) with a synthesised mew, hiss and purr. "Cat" in the Tune tab sets how keen it is on perches, how easily it is scared and how long it sleeps; the Girls tab's "Animals, gates and the lane" has buttons to call it, put it asleep on the nearest quad, shoo it and keep it in the barn, and the console's `cat` path does the same (`girl <name> cat` sends it to a girl). Stroking it, feeding it and a kitten are not done.

**The farm dog.** Meg, a black and white border collie, lives on the farm and keeps you company without sticking to you: she sits, sniffs and mooches about within a few metres of you, and only comes after you once you have got a dozen metres away (she runs flat out to catch up if you ride off). Pull up on a quad with a rear rack (the utility quads and the farm ones) and stay still for a moment and she runs over and hops up on the rack, where she sits upright with her tongue out; get off, or let a girl on the back, and she hops down again and keeps off the rack for a few seconds. Now and then she goes after a quad that a girl is riding, running alongside barking until she tires of it, and she goes after the heels of a girl who is walking. She jumps out of the way of a vehicle coming at her, with a yelp, and is never hurt. She is put back near you if she is left a long way behind or held up behind a hedge. Jess loves her; Amy tells her off, and says so when she gets on your rack. It is all procedural, with synthesised barks, yelps and panting. "Dog" in the Tune tab sets how keen she is on chasing and getting up, how far she follows and how fast; the Girls tab's "Animals, gates and the lane" has buttons to call her, put her on your rack, get her down, send her after a vehicle and make her bark, each girl has "The dog goes after her boots", and the console's `dog` path does the same. Herding with her, riding on the tractor or in a car, and anything she remembers between loads are not done (the herding is TODO item 195).

**Dozing in the sun.** Each girl has a sleepiness that creeps up all game (Jess's sooner than Amy's, and faster while she is on the go). The sleepier she is, the less she does and the longer she takes to think of anything. On a warm, bright, dry afternoon a sleepy girl goes and sits on the straw bales, nods off with her head dropping, and snores softly (close by only). Only a doze cures it. She wakes herself when she has rested, after a few minutes, or when the sun goes in (Amy says she was only resting her eyes). Press `E` at her to wake her gently, or rev a quad, sound the horn or make a bang nearby and she jumps awake: Jess laughs it off, Amy is cross with you for a while. A girl does not pose for a photo while she is asleep; a photo with her in it ticks "Caught napping" on the bucket list. Her sleepiness is among her needs in the Girls tab's "Why" folder (settable), the Girls tab has buttons to send her to doze and wake her either way, the console's `girl <name> doze`, `wake` and `wake-rudely` do the same, and the Tune tab's "Places" group sets how long she dozes, how bright it must be and what wakes her.

**Wildlife.** Fourteen rabbits live on the grass field, sat up or hopping about a few metres out from the hedges. Ride, walk or bring a girl near and they bolt for the hedge (from further off the faster you come), sometimes thumping a warning with a back foot, and stay in for a minute or two. After dark, a rabbit in your headlights freezes with its eyes shining and lets you get quite close before it goes. Three pheasants lie hidden at the foot of the hedges round the grass field and the two stubble fields; ride past close or walk up to one and it bursts out with a clatter of wings and a double "kok-kok", flying off away from you. Amy jumps almost every time, Jess mostly laughs, and a new pheasant takes its place somewhere else after a minute or two. Now and then you hear one crowing from a hedge. All procedural, with synthesised sound. "Wildlife" in the Tune tab sets the numbers and how easily they are scared; the Girls tab's "Both girls and the farm" has a "Flush the nearest pheasant" button, and the console's `wildlife` path counts them and flushes one. Rabbits on the hill and in the sheep field, and a girl remarking on them, are not done.

**The lane and the gates.** Outside the farm's east hedge, past the big gate at the end of the road, runs a quiet public lane (a strip of tarmac with a worn dashed line). Every few minutes, more rarely after dark, a van, a Land Rover, a tractor or a UTV goes by on it, on the left, heard coming and going with its pitch dropping as it passes, with its lights on at night. Now and then a visitor turns in at the gate: the post van (a red 200TDi), whose driver opens the gate, drives up the farm road to the barn, leaves a parcel by the door and drives round the yard to leave, or a neighbour in a Land Rover who parks at the barn and stands about for a while. Both are women, on the same model as Amy and Jess. They shut the gate behind them, or one time in four leave it open. If you are in their way they wait and then hoot. Jess waves at anyone going by, Amy less often, and they remark on the post, the neighbour, a parcel and a gate left open. The gate by the cattle grid works the same way, and the post van opens it and shuts it behind them, in and out; the girls open it as they reach it on foot or driving, and shut it once they have gone through and nobody is near, but leave one you opened for you to shut. All three gates work: look at the south gate, the big gate or the grid gate and press `E` to open or shut it (it swings, and won't shut with a quad, a girl or you stood within 3 m of it). A shut gate is solid and the girls won't route through it. "Traffic", "Gates" and "Girls and the grid gate" in the debug panel's Tune tab set how often things come, how fast, and how they mind the gate; the Girls tab and the console's `lane` path send a vehicle, the post van or a neighbour and swing the gates.

**Things walk off a shaking vehicle.** Loose things move with the vehicle's own shake, standing still or not. A girl's mug on a quad's rear rack creeps towards the edge and shakes off in the end, and she says so; things standing in the UTV's bed or a Land Rover's tub shuffle about their spots; a jerry can goes over if the shake stays hard for about twelve seconds. A smooth warm engine leaves them be. Pick a tipped can up and stand it in the bed again to put it right. "Things walk off" in the Tune tab sets how rough, how fast and how far.

**Waving.** Press `6`, on foot or on a quad. A girl within 30 metres who is stood about or walking waves back, and Jess sometimes pulls a face instead. Further off (out to 90 metres) a girl who is free to be asked, as for the wheel's "Come here", walks over; this is how to call her over an engine. A girl who is driving in an ordinary drive (not a race, a trick, tidying a quad into the row or giving you a ride) pulls up and waits 12 seconds, then carries on. Each girl answers at most once every 3 seconds. The Tune tab's "Waving" group sets the ranges and the wait, the Girls tab has "answers your wave" for each girl, and the console's `waving.wave` waves for you.

**Photos.** Press `5` on foot or on a quad and the camera comes up: any girl in the picture (ahead of you, within about 14 metres) reacts, and a moment later the shutter goes with a white flash. Amy mostly poses, a hand on her hip and her head tilted, but when she is feeling neglected she puts a hand up and tells you to put that away. Jess mostly pulls a face, both hands by her cheeks. A shot of nothing but sky or a blank wall is thrown away. Each shot is pinned on a corkboard on the barn's back wall, south of the chalkboards, at a random spot and a little askew, with its day; only the last twelve stay up. Press `E` at the board for a bigger view with when each was taken and who is in it. The wall is kept from one day to the next. Bucket list: Say cheese and Gallery. "Photos" in the Tune tab sets the cooldown, the shutter delay and the picture; the Girls tab has buttons for taking one, taking them all down, and for each girl's pose, face and put-it-away; the console's `photos` path does the same. Photo mode (a free camera) is TODO item 122.

**Phone calls.** Now and then, when a girl is stood about with nothing to do, her phone goes: a two-tone trill from her pocket, and she says whose it is. She wanders off out of earshot (nine to sixteen metres from you), takes it with the phone at her ear and a hand on her hip, talks for fifteen seconds or half a minute, and comes back to tell you. Usually it is news (Amy's mum, the vet, the college; Jess's mates and her brother). Sometimes it is a bad call and she comes back in a mood: for a few minutes (Amy six, Jess two and a half) she stands lower with you, as a neglected girl does, so she is sulkier, likelier to say no and likelier to take a quad off on her own. Jess is on the phone more often; Amy's moods last longer. "Phone calls" in the Tune tab sets the timing and distance; the Girls tab has a phone call and a bad call button for each girl, and a line saying who is on the phone and who is in a mood; the console's `phoneCall` path says the same.

**Wind.** Each game has its own wind, blowing a different way every time and stronger on some days than others (a still, foggy day hardly stirs; an overcast or rainy one blows). It comes in gusts, and it veers a little over the day. It carries the exhaust smoke and the dust off the stubble downwind, slants the rain and the snow, and rocks the hedges and trees, the tall ones more. When a strong wind blows out through the hay door (towards the east) its shutter on the barn, hooked back against the wall, is lifted off and flaps about and bangs back, which makes the girls jump if they are near. On a gusty day with the wind blowing out of that door, loose hay from the loft blows out into the yard. "Wind" in the Tune tab sets how gusty, how often and how far the hedges and trees lean; `weather.windHeading`, `weather.windNow` and `weather.gust` in the console turn it, read it and start a gust.

**The electric fence.** A line of white stakes with two strands of yellow tape runs across the hill pasture, about two thirds of the way up, with a gap in it (a pair of stout posts, a yellow lightning-bolt sign on one) where the climb goes through. The energiser box on the east hedge ticks every second or so with a red light that blinks with it. Touch the tape with the nose or tail of a quad, or walk into it, and it gives a jolt: the quad and the view shake, it loses some speed, "Ow! That fence is live." comes up, and whoever is on the quad yelps and flinches, whichever girl it is. A girl who saw it says something, and both girls hear the snap and may jump. One brush is one zap. The girls on foot know better and are not zapped. Jess's dares use it. "Electric fence" in the Tune tab switches it off and sets the numbers; the Girls tab's "Both girls and the farm" has a "Zap you on the electric fence" button, and the console's `fence` path switches it and zaps.

**Jess's dares.** Now and then, when she is free and you are within earshot, Jess dares you to do something daft: jump the ramp in the field, take the ford flat out, touch the electric fence, or ride the mini moto down the steepest bank on the hill (the big one the fence runs along). There is nothing to accept: just do it within about two minutes. Do it and she is delighted, she likes you a little more and Amy a little less, and Amy says what she thinks of it, or checks you are all right if you ended up on your side. Let it lapse and Jess clucks at you. One dare at a time, never the same twice running, and a few minutes between. Each of the four is on the bucket list (the mini moto one only if the mini moto is on the farm). "Dares" in the Tune tab sets how often, how long you have and what counts; the Girls tab's Jess folder has a button for each dare, and the console has `girl jess dare-ramp` (or `-ford`, `-fence`, `-bank`).

**Towing a quad with a quad.** Look at the red or blue quad on foot and press `X` to take the tow rope off its rack (the green's `X` is its winch), walk the hook to a dead quad and press `E` on it, then ride off. `E` on the dead quad unhooks it. The rope stretches and goes slack, so pulling away hard snatches the dead quad, and it has its brakes off, so it rolls into the back of you if you stop. Ask a girl on her wheel to "Steer the towed quad" and she sits on it, steers it after you and works the brake: Amy brakes early and keeps her eyes on the rope, Jess brakes later and sometimes looks away, and either of them shouts if you snatch the rope or stop sharply. She gets off when you unhook it. "Tow rope" and "Girl steering a tow" in the Tune tab set the rope's stretch and how far ahead she brakes; the Vehicles tab has a button to hook the rope on and a line for its pull, the Girls tab has "steer-tow" for each girl, and the console's `vehicle.<id>.towRope` and `towRope` paths do the same. The rope doesn't snap yet.

**Autumn.** About one game in six is an autumn day (`?season=autumn` forces it, `?season=summer` stops it). The oaks, hazel and hedges turn orange, gold and russet, the wood's floor is thick with fallen leaves, more leaves drift down round you under the trees, and there are conkers lying round the foot of every oak. The leaves are slippery: in the wood you get less grip on them, a little when it is dry and a good deal when it is wet. Jess, stood about in the wood with you a few metres away, may pick up a conker and throw it at you: it is an arc, not a good one, and a hit is a thock and a laugh, a miss a groan. It never hurts. Amy tuts at the throwing, says once how lovely the wood is, and warns you the first time you ride the wet leaves. "Autumn" in the Tune tab sets how many leaves come down and how keen and accurate Jess is, and the wet-leaf grip is in "Surfaces"; the Girls tab has a button for making Jess throw one, and the console's `weather.autumn` says whether it is an autumn day. Making more of the farm seasonal is TODO item 341.

**Snow.** Now and then (about one game in sixteen) the day is a snowy one, on a frosty or cold day: flakes fall around you in flurries, the sky goes white-grey and the ground whitens in patches that join up the longer it falls. Your tyres leave tracks that stay, the seats turn white (sit on one to clear it), and the quads grip less and drag more on it. It melts slowly when the day turns mild, and rain washes it off. The girls warm their hands as they do on any cold day. Snowballs are a TODO item. `?weather=snow` forces it. The weather in the console (`weather.snow`, `weather.snowCover`) and "Snow underfoot" in the Tune tab's surfaces set it.

**Ice on the puddles.** On a frosty day the puddles on the track freeze over, white-blue and glossy, and a game that starts on a frosty morning starts with them already frozen. Ice grips like glass: a quad on it slides, and the throttle only spins the wheels. It does not hold you for long. Each wheel that crosses loads it, and it cracks (a sharp crack, and white lines across it) and then gives way with a splash into the puddle underneath, which then behaves as a puddle does. Thin ice, a heavy quad and speed all break it sooner. Once broken the water stays open for a minute or two before it can freeze again, and when the day warms the ice melts, faster in rain. A quad on unbroken ice is not in the water, so it takes none up the intake. "Puddle ice" in the Tune tab sets when it freezes and melts, how slippery it is and how strong; the Girls tab's "The day and the weather" shows the puddles and has a slider and a break button, and the console's `weather.puddleIce` and `weather.breakIce` do the same. Walking on it (you and the girls) makes no difference yet.

**Thunderstorms.** Rarely (about one game in thirty, half as often as snow) the day is a storm: the sky goes nearly black, the rain is heavy and the wind strong. Lightning lights the whole sky in a flicker or two, and the thunder follows after the time sound takes to cover the distance: a close strike cracks and rolls, a far one only rumbles. Nothing is ever struck and nothing can be hurt. The closer claps make the girls jump and say something; Amy won't get on or drive a quad in a storm (the wheel's ride and drive options give her refusal, and she gets off a quad stood still), Jess would and says so. A storm always blows over to rain within a game hour or two (about five minutes of play an hour). The forecast board shows it as rain. `?weather=storm` forces it. "Thunderstorm" in the Tune tab sets how often and how near the strikes are; the Girls tab has buttons for a close and a far strike, and the console's `weather.kind storm` and `weather.strike` do the same.

**A flask at the top of the hill.** At the top of the hill pasture, on the level ground by the far hedge, there is a flat grey rock looking back over the whole farm. Ride up with a girl, get off, stand by the rock and press `E` ("Sit down with Amy and have a cup of tea from the flask"): she gets off the quad if she is on it, walks to the rock, sits and pours two mugs, and you stay and look for about forty seconds. She talks about the view and the weather (a cold day is better for it) and, if she is the girl who likes you more, says something quieter. Finish the cup and she likes you a little more; walk off and she packs up, put out. A girl stood about up there when you arrive may suggest it herself. It is off in rain and in the dark. Bucket list: Tea at the top. "Flask at the top of the hill" in the Tune tab; the Girls tab has "Take me to the rock at the top of the hill" and a flask action for each girl, and the console has `girl amy flask`.

**Muddy girls.** The girls get dirty. Riding on a muddy quad splashes mud up their jeans and onto their faces (the girl on the back takes the most, more through puddles), and riding in the dust off the stubble puts it in their hair and faces. Amy minds and says so, Jess laughs. The mud stays on while it is wet, then dries and flakes off slowly, faster while she walks about; the hose takes it off in a couple of seconds (and she reacts as before). Her wellies and shorts stay clean. "Muddy girls" in the Tune tab sets how quickly; the Girls tab shows her mud and dust, sets them and has a Splash and a Scrub button, and the console has `girl amy mud`, `girl amy dust`.

**Asleep on the back.** On a long smooth ride (a steady 3 to 14 m/s, no hard braking, a gentle seat shake) the girl on the pillion seat grows drowsy over about 45 seconds, quicker if she is sleepy and for Amy more than Jess. Her head drops onto your shoulder, her grip goes slack and she stops talking (she snores quietly if you are close). A bump, a hard brake or a rough engine wakes her with a start and a line; stopping, or talking to her, wakes her gently, and she will not drop off again for two minutes. "Asleep on the back" in the Tune tab sets the thresholds, and the Girls tab and `girl amy nap` drop her off or wake her.

**Wear and tread.** Every vehicle shows its age: paint and plastic rubbed through on its sharp edges, colour faded by the sun, rust starting in the folds of its metal and old dried mud packed into the nooks where its parts meet, and polished, worn patches where hands and feet go (the gear knob, the pedals, the footboards, the edge of the seat you slide onto), more on the old ones (the three-wheeler, the red, the green and the Series) and none on the loan quad. The barn find is the worst of all. The insides of the cabs wear too but never get muddy. Hosing takes off the day's mud, not the years; fresh mud gathers in those nooks first. Each kind of tyre has its own tread: chevrons on the quads (square blocks on the older ones), knobs on the mini moto, chunky square blocks on the three-wheeler's balloons, tall bar lugs on the tractor's rears and ribs on its fronts, all-terrain blocks on the UTV and the Land Rovers, and road tread on the cars. The Vehicles tab's "Wear" sets how worn the one picked looks. The wear is worked out in the background after the farm loads, so for the first few seconds the edges are clean.

**Mud on your view.** On an open vehicle (the quads, the mini moto, the three-wheeler and the tractor) mud splatters your view as brown blobs over the screen: close behind another vehicle on the mud, through a puddle or the ford at speed, and a few drops in hard rain. Press `X` (on a pad, the ring's "Wipe the lens") to wipe: the first wipe smears it wide and thin, the second clears it, and raindrops go at once. It dries off and drops away by itself in a few minutes. The UTV, the Land Rovers and the cars have a windscreen and stay clean. Goggles (see below) keep most of it off. "Mud on the lens" in the Tune tab sets how easily it splatters and how fast it dries; the Vehicles tab shows how much is on your view and can splat or wipe it; the console's `lens` path does the same.

**Helmets and goggles.** Four hooks on the inside of the barn's south wall hold your red helmet, your goggles, and Amy's and Jess's helmets. Look at a hook and press `E` to put yours on or hang it back up. A helmet does nothing to the physics, but Amy minds: ride an open vehicle (quad, mini moto, three-wheeler, tractor) without one, moving, with her in sight, and she tells you off (then not again for a couple of minutes); put one on and she says thank you, and likes you a little more. Goggles keep most of the mud off your view (the lens's `guard` goes to 0.85) and show a rubber frame round the edges; your helmet's peak shows along the top of the view. Both are kept in the save. The girls fetch their own helmets from the hooks, as something to do (Amy first, always; Jess when she remembers), wear them on an open vehicle and mostly take them off again on getting off: Amy lays hers on the seat, Jess drops hers on the grass or wears it pushed back on her head, with her goggles up on the front. They pick it up as they get on if it is within 30 metres; a helmet left on a seat falls off once the quad is moving. "Helmets and goggles" in the Tune tab sets how keen they are, how big the helmets sit on their heads and how long before one comes off; the Girls tab shows where each girl's helmet is and has a fetch-or-hang-up button for her, and buttons for your own; the console's `kit` path and `girl amy helmet` do the same. Amy's and Jess's helmets are sized from their measured heads, and may need nudging (fit and lift in the Tune tab, and per girl in `characters.js`).

The temperature follows the day: coldest at dawn, warmest at half past two, then falling by four to eight degrees into the night. So a frosty morning thaws (the ground and the seats clear as it warms), and a cold evening frosts over again after dark. Cold starts, the girls' need for warmth and the rest follow the temperature as it is now, not as it was when the game began.

After dark the headlights matter: a lit lamp throws real light, as bright and as yellow as that vehicle's lamps are (the old quads' brighten with the revs and flicker when the engine runs rough). There are six real lights, given to the lit vehicles that matter most: yours first, then whichever is lighting the way for you, then the nearest. The barn's bulb is left on, and the girls say it's getting dark, then that they want to go in, and head for the barn doorway or a warm cab.

**Lights to work by.** After dark, ask a girl to "Light me up" from her wheel and she brings a vehicle round to light you: a Land Rover or the UTV if one's free (it has a roof and a passenger seat), else a quad with working lamps. She drives it to about six metres off, facing you, puts its lights on and leaves it running, then most of the time moves over to the passenger side (onto the back of a quad), and otherwise gets out and stands by it. Amy does it unasked once she's seen you stood at a quad or a gate in the dark for a while. It's over when you ride off, walk away, or say "That'll do for light, thanks" on her wheel; she switches off what she brought. A girl driving a Land Rover or the UTV in the dark puts its lights on.

`?time=16.5` starts a game at half past four; the debug panel (Girls tab, "Both girls and the farm") and the console (`weather.time`) set the time of day.

## Fuel

Each quad has a fuel tank and a fuel tap, and a fuel gauge on the loan quad's and the blue's LCD or on a pod. Taps start OFF. How much is in each tank varies every game (see `fuelRange` above); the blue can start below its reserve, so it'll only run on RESERVE until it's filled.

- A full tank lasts roughly 25 minutes of hard riding, and much longer at idle.
- The tap has three positions. On **ON** it can't reach the last couple of litres, so when it starts to cough and stumble, flick it to **RESERVE** with `F` and head for fuel. **OFF** shuts the fuel off; the engine runs on what's in the carburettor for a moment, then dies.
- If it runs completely dry, it stalls and won't restart until it has fuel.
- To refuel: find the red jerry can (it's left somewhere around the yard, the paddock or the gate) and pick it up with `E` (it holds 20 litres). Look at a quad and hold `E` to pour, with a glug. Refill the can by holding `E` at the green petrol tank. `G` puts the can down. You can't ride holding the can, so put it down first.

**Two-stroke oil.** The yellow and orange run on pre-mix, about 50:1 oil in the petrol. By the petrol tank there's a bottle of oil and, somewhere about the farm, a second, yellow jerry can of pre-mix and a bottle of two-stroke oil: hold `E` on the bottle with a can in your hands to add oil to it. Refilling any can at the tank adds plain petrol, so it thins the mix, and the prompts tell you what's in the can you're holding. Nothing tells you what's in a quad's tank except how it behaves. Straight petrol in a two-stroke and it runs hot (the temperature needle climbs into the red), then it starts to lose power and run raggedly (nipping up), and in the end it seizes solid and won't turn over, not even on the pull cord. Flat out takes a couple of minutes; idling takes far longer. Take the toolbox to it and press `F` ("Strip it down") to free it, which leaves it worn until you service it. Too much oil, or any pre-mix in the red, blue or green, smokes like a bonfire and slowly fouls the plug. Amy says so if you pour the wrong can nearby, and reads what's gone wrong when it goes; Jess blames the quad.

**Engine oil on the four-strokes.** The red, blue, green and loan quads, the tractor and the UTV each have oil in the sump. Stood looking at one, press `Y` to pull the dipstick: a toast says whether it's up to the top mark, between the marks, on the bottom one or below it (it reads to within a hair either way). To top it up, park it near the oil bottle by the petrol tank and hold `Y`, or pick the bottle up (`E`, empty-handed) and carry it over: like the toolbox and the jump leads it goes down with `G`, in the tipping trailer, or back in its place on the workbench. The bench has the same job (`5`, petrol four-strokes only). Each game one of them burns oil: it starts with a low sump, loses it far faster than the rest, and puffs smoke on the overrun with the throttle shut. Run a quad low and the tappets tick, then the engine gets rough and starts to knock and lose power, and run dry it seizes just as a two-stroke does on straight petrol (a seized engine needs its top end rebuilt at the bench, and top the oil up first or it goes again). The red's OIL lamp (it was a temperature warning, now an oil pressure one) and an OIL lamp on either accessory pod light with the ignition on and the engine not running, then only when the sump is low while it's running, flickering at idle, so fitting a pod to another quad shows it too. Amy checks the dipstick before she rides and tops it up if it's low; Jess never has, so it's usually Jess's quad that starts ticking. The tractor and the UTV have a sump too, and one of the five four-strokes (diesels included) is the one that burns oil; the two-strokes aren't affected. Hold `Y` past the top mark and it carries on pouring, so you can overfill it: the stick says "Over the top mark", the exhaust smokes blue, and working it that way soaks the air box with oil until it is sluggish and rich. Hold `Y` away from the bottle to let the extra out at the drain plug (the air box dries over a few minutes); the bench job drains it too, and Amy drains an overfilled one before she rides. "Engine oil" in the Tune tab has the numbers.

**The barn.** The big doors are always open, and you can walk or ride straight in. Inside there's a concrete floor, two straw bales, a workbench along the north wall with the toolbox and a pegboard of tools, and a bare bulb that comes on in the gloom (when the generator outside is running). A second span of the same size stands on its south side with its own door and roof, open to the first between a row of posts: it has the big bay for the tractor, the UTV, the Land Rovers and the cars, a second bulb, the helmet hooks and the jumper rail (two jumpers the girls put on when it's cold or wet, and at its far end two Barbour-style wax jackets with corduroy collars that they put on when it has been raining on them a while, over the jumper if they have one, with the hood up in proper rain; each farm deals the jumpers' and the coats' colours for itself and keeps them). The generator now stands behind the south wall, the bell north of the doors and the silage along the west wall. Under the roof no rain falls, the light is dimmer, and rain on the tin drums loudly (you can hear a little of it from the doorway); an engine inside booms and echoes, and so does anything you hear from in there. A quad parked inside takes no mud, and a warm engine switched off in there cools two and a half times slower and never goes fully stone cold, so it starts better the next morning than one left in the yard. The tuning panel has a "Barn" folder for the sound, and the engine's "barn" settings for the warmth.

**The bucket list.** Press `Tab` for a notebook of things to do on the farm: ruled pages in biro, each item with a box that gets a tick and a line through it when you do it, a score (each is worth 1 to 3 points) and an "x of y" count. It opens as a two-page spread; `←` and `→` turn the page, and `Tab` again puts it away. Every one shows what to do, so it doubles as a to-do list. When you tick one off a toast says so and a soft chime sounds. There are about 60, in groups: riding each vehicle (and every one on the farm, and getting every quad running at once), speed and distance, tricks (the ramp, a big jump, a clean landing, a doughnut, rolling a quad and righting it), mud (getting bogged, getting out, being winched or chained out), company (being driven by each girl, giving one a lift, both on one quad, winning each girl over, the horn, racing and drag racing), places (the ford, the hill pasture, the wood, the Dutch barn, the field shed, the cattle grid, the end of the road, and all of them) and farm jobs (the bell, the hayloft, hiding, the dipstick, oil, petrol, the hose, the toolbox, the bench, the jump leads, bump-starting, a trailer, tipping, the parking brake, the map, the barn find). The line-up changes with the farm, so an achievement for a vehicle that isn't on the farm is left off the list and the total. Ticks are kept from day to day; a new farm starts the list again.

**Plug-saver for the two-strokes.** A green-labelled bottle of plug-saver stands a little way from the oil bottle by the petrol tank. Carry the yellow pre-mix can over, hold `E` on the bottle and the fuel in the can is treated (about five seconds for a full can); the prompts then say the pre-mix is "with plug-saver". Fill a two-stroke from it and its plug loads up far less at idle and with a touch too much oil (about 85% less), so it keeps ticking over without needing a blip of throttle to clear it. It lasts as long as the treated fuel does: top the tank up with untreated petrol and it thins out, run it dry and it is gone, so treat the can each time you mix it. It does nothing for a flooded engine or too much choke. Whoever is nearest remarks when you do it (Amy knows what it is for, Jess thinks you have paid for magic fuel). The treated share is kept between games. "Plug-saver" in the Tune tab sets the pour rate and the engines' "additiveFoulCut" the strength; the Running folder on the Vehicles tab shows and sets a two-stroke's treated share, and the console has `plugSaver`.

**The farm map.** Press `M` for a map of the farm over the middle of the screen, and again to put it away; the game carries on underneath, so you can glance at it as you ride. North is up. It shows the grass field with the track, its bogs, the jump and the drag strip; the yard, the barn and the paddock; the farm road and the two stubble fields with the Dutch barn, the field shed, the muck heap and the trough; the lane down to the hill pasture with the stream, the ford and the wood; and the wood through the north gate, with the Hollow Run, its bogs and its pond. Hedges are drawn with their gaps, so you can see the way through. On top of it are you (a white arrow pointing the way you're looking), Amy and Jess (blue and burgundy dots), every vehicle in its own colour, pointing the way it faces, the two trailers, the three jerry cans, the toolbox, the jump leads and the oil bottle, with a key in the bottom corner. Anything that has got off the map is pinned to its edge in a dotted ring. "Map" in the tuning panel sets its size, how solid it is and how big the markers are.

**The bell.** A brass bell hangs on a post just north of the barn doors. Look at it and press `E` to pull the rope: it swings and rings a few times, dying away, and pulling again while it swings keeps it going. The girls hear it anywhere on the farm. Whatever they're doing (driving, racing, warming up, sat on a bale), they stop, say they're coming and gather in front of the barn doors; a race or follow the leader is called off. Keep ringing once they're on their way and one may tell you she heard it the first time. Once there they carry on as usual. The sound is made in the game, not recorded (tunable under "Bell" in the tuning panel); "Call the girls back" in the tuning panel's Girls folder does the same without the walk.

**Keeping a sick one alive.** A quad that is hard to keep going counts as sick: the girl's last start of it took two or more goes, the plug is fouled and the engine erratic together, the battery is weak, the engine is worn out, its choke creeps on, or it runs erratically for want of a service (any one heavy cause does it, which includes a weak battery; two light ones do). The girl who got one going doesn't switch it off. She walks back and sits on the pillion seat, leaning forward with a hand on the throttle, holding a fast idle and catching it when it falters, because if it dies it won't start again. Her reason is practical ("you can hear it better from here", "I'm out of the way when you get on"), and you can get on the front and go. The real one is that it buzzes more on the back: Jess owns up after a while, and Amy only if you come up to her after a long sit. Take the quad and she lets go, and a sick one she has been holding may die. If nobody comes for about three minutes she gets off (switching it off now and then, mostly not) and leaves it to its luck. If it dies under her she says so. In the debug panel, Vehicles shows how sick it is under Running and "hard start (goes it took)" under Problems; the Girls tab has a button that sends a girl to sit on the picked vehicle, and the tuning folder is "Keeping it alive".

**Faults that develop.** Quads go wrong over time, separately from wear, and nothing tells you: you feel and hear it. A slow puncture starts while a quad's being ridden and gets worse over a couple of minutes: the bars pull towards the soft tyre (less for a rear one) and it drags a little. A slipping drive belt comes from riding hard: the revs flare as soon as you open the throttle and it hardly goes. A sticking throttle cable gets worse in stages: first the throttle lingers for a moment after you let go, then for longer and longer, and after about seven minutes' running it sticks open for good, staying wherever you last opened it (you can still open it further) until you switch off. A girl driving a quad that has stuck switches it off herself. A dirty carb makes it hunt, run rough and lose power, worse and worse the longer it runs. They happen to any quad the girls ride too, about one every ten to twenty minutes of riding across the lot. At the toolbox panel, `T` pumps and patches the tyres, `R` swaps the belt, `L` oils the cable and `C` cleans the carb; each does its job if there is one and otherwise just says everything's fine, so it doesn't give a fault away. Amy notices sooner and says what it is; Jess notices late and blames the quad.

**The hayloft.** Just inside the barn doors a ladder leads up to a plank loft over the front of the barn, above the doorway (quads drive in underneath). Look at the ladder and press `E` and you climb it (about 2.5 seconds); do the same at the top to come down. Up there you can walk right up to the hay door in the end wall, a window at head height looking out over the yard, hay is stacked down both sides where the roof comes low, and there's a stack of hay: look at it and press `E` to duck into it and hide. While you're hidden the girls can't find you, so they stop offering rides and asking favours, and call for you now and then ("Where have you gone?") until one finds you and you come up (or press any key to come out yourself). The loft's open edge has a rail, with a gap at the ladder. On a fine day a bored girl might climb up and sit on the hay bale at the edge; she finds you at once if you're hiding while she's up there, and Jess, up there while you stand below near her, now and then tips straw on your head (Amy tells her off). The tuning panel has a "Hayloft" folder.

**The workbench.** The toolbox lives on the bench in the barn (if it's not there, the bench won't work). Drive a quad into the small yellow-painted bay in front of it, or the tractor, the UTV, a Land Rover or one of the girls' cars into the big bay in the barn's second span (in through the second door, nose to the back wall, under its own bulb, with a trolley jack and axle stands by it), switch it off, look at the bench and press `E`. The jobs apply to whatever is in the bay and are greyed out when they don't fit it: no plug for a diesel, no carb or boot for a diesel or the Corsa's injection. `1` to `9` and `0` pick a job: change the spark plug, clean the carb, fix a tyre, top up the two-stroke oil (yellow and orange only), top up the engine oil (the petrol four-strokes), repack the silencer (two-strokes), top up the coolant (the orange), fit the big-bore kit (the yellow) renew the engine mounts or rebuild the top end of a two-stroke that has scuffed or seized. Each job is a short list of steps: `E` taps a step, and on a hold step you keep `E` down until it's done. Some holds have a green band on the meter: let go inside it. Too soon and it's loose, too long and it's over-done, and either leaves the job botched, so it looks done but the fault comes back a little, the plug is still fouled, or the oil mix is off. The plug job lets you look at the old plug, which tells you how the engine's been running. `G` steps away. The toolbox's `T`, `R`, `L` and `C` in the yard are now rough fixes that leave a mild fault behind; the bench gets rid of it. The girls watch from wherever they are: Amy helps and tells you when you've slipped, Jess heckles.

**The spares shelf.** A parts rack on the barn's back wall, past the end of the workbench, holds five counted parts: spark plugs, inner tubes, drive belts, main jets, and pistons and rings. A bench job uses one up when it fits it (the plug change, the tyre, the top-end rebuild), and so do the toolbox's quick fixes: `T` on a puncture takes a tube, `R` on a worn belt a belt, and a main jet other than the stock size takes a jet (going back to stock, or "Back to stock", gives it back; one size to another costs nothing). The toolbox's service (`V`) takes a plug too. With none on the rack the job is greyed out at the bench and the toolbox says what is missing, and a girl will not start a job she has no part for. Look at the rack and press `E` to see what is there and order a pack with `1` to `5`: about five minutes later (four and a half to five and a half, then the drive in) the post van (item 22) comes in, once no one else is visiting and if it is light, and leaves a parcel by the barn door, which `E` opens onto the rack. The rack shows what is on it: plugs in a yellow box, jets in a red organiser, pistons in a blue box, tubes in a card tray, belts on pegs, each emptying as they are used. The shelf, an order and an unopened parcel are all kept from one day to the next. Amy and Jess each have a line for the last one going and for the box being empty. "Spares shelf" in the Tune tab sets what is on the rack at the start, the size of each pack, the most of one part and the wait; the Girls tab's "Both girls and the farm" shows the shelf and has buttons to fill and empty it, order a pack of each and open the parcel; the console's `spares` path sets each count.

A girl may also work at the bench herself (the chooser weighs it like anything else she could do, Amy far keener than Jess), when a quad is parked in the bay and switched off, the toolbox is on the bench and nobody is using it. She goes for whatever the quad needs most (a puncture, a dirty carb, low two-stroke oil, a fouled or worn plug, else a plug) and does it a step at a time. Amy asks you for a hand on the way: "Pass me the ten millimetre" or "Hold the torch". Go to the bench and press `E` when the prompt shows. Be quick and she is pleased with you; ignore her for nine seconds and she goes and gets it herself, which takes longer and makes her likelier to slip. Jess gets about halfway, botches a step, and asks you to finish: the job waits on the bench, and when you open the bench it carries on from the step she reached (it is already botched, so it comes out rough, though she is pleased you did it). Leave her job for four minutes and she gives up on it, with the quad left as it was. Amy writes up what she does in her logbook. "A girl at the bench" in the Tune tab sets how often and how it goes; each girl's folder on the Girls tab (and `girl amy bench-work` in the console) sends her there at once.

**The rusty quad.** In about one game in five (from the seed, so `?seed=` repeats it; try 7, 8, 12 or 13) an old brown quad stands under a tarp at the back of the barn: a copy of one of the regular quads (which one also comes from the seed), caked in dust and rust and not run for years: rack bars rusted away or hanging broken (on the three with racks), rust patches with holes eaten through, cracks across the plastics, cobwebs in the corners of the racks and the bars and the arches, and grey tyres with bold cracks across the sidewalls and rusty wheel centres, and two or three splits in the seat's cover. A tarp covers it, draped over the quad's own shape, and it is dragged off to the side when that job is done. The tyre job gives it fresh black rubber without the cracks, and on the yellow a mouse nest in its intake goes with the carb job. It's the "rusty quad" everywhere it shows up, and it comes in addition to whatever is in the yard. It can't be driven, so the bench works on it where it stands (a quad parked in the bay comes first). The list starts with the tarp, which you take off first, and then the three or four jobs this one needs, picked from the seed: drain the varnished fuel (which empties the tank, so you then have to fill it, with pre-mix for a two-stroke), clean the carb, a new plug, free the seized brake, mend the tyres, and charge the battery. Its battery is flat and works like any other vehicle's: the bench's last row lifts it out, you put it on the bench charger (the generator has to be running) and fit it back once it has charged, or fit it flat and jump it from a running quad. It won't catch, however you crank it, until every job it needs is done; cranking it earlier tells you what is still wrong. Then the first start: a burst of smoke, a toast, and both girls cheering (Amy will switch it off soon after, since it is full of fumes in the barn). Amy writes the jobs in the logbook. The girls leave it alone, and it isn't in the wheel's quad lists.

**Amy won't ride it.** Once Amy knows a quad has a fault, she won't ride it until it's put right: "Not on that, it's got a soft tyre." She knows from her own walk-round (a slow puncture, soft tyre, slipping belt, sticking throttle or dirty carb), and from what she had written in the logbook before the game began. If the walk-round turns something up as she sets off, she says so, pulls up and gets off (unless a race, follow the leader or a recall is on). After that she leaves that quad out of her own drives, the races, follow the leader and the bored games, and it drops off the wheel's "drive" choices for her. Pump the tyre or fix the fault, in the yard or at the bench, and she forgets it and will ride it again. Jess rides it anyway. In `src/npc/wontRide.js`; "Amy won't ride" in the tuning panel (`amyWontRide`, 0 switches it off).

**Amy's logbook.** A red notebook lies on the workbench. Look at it and press `E` to read it (`E` or `G` puts it down). Amy writes in it what she finds and what's done to the quads, with the time into the game: "3:10  Blue, belt slipping." It gets her walk-round findings (slow puncture, soft tyre, slipping belt, sticking throttle, dirty carb), her oil top-ups, and, if she's within 15 m, the plug you pulled at the bench ("Green, plug black (you)") and the bench jobs you finish ("Yellow, silencer repacked (you)", with ", not well" if you botched it). She writes what she really saw, so unlike Jess it's true, and it's a way to read the hidden state from inside the game. She won't write the same thing about the same quad twice within two minutes, and the page holds the last 40 entries. The first time she writes something a toast says so. Entries from earlier days stay on the page marked "Yesterday" or "Day 3". The book isn't empty on a new farm's first day: from the seed it already has two to five entries from "Yesterday", picked from what is really true of the quads (a fault already showing, tinkering left in, a quirk, who had one out last), though she's missed some of it and never writes what isn't so.

**Rebuilding a top end.** A two-stroke that seizes (straight petrol, too lean a jet, no oil) is no longer freed at the toolbox. Park it in the bay and pick "Rebuild the top end" (`0`, shown only for a two-stroke that has scuffed or seized): undo the head nuts, ease the barrel up, look at the piston (the toast says whether it is lightly scuffed, deeply scored or welded to the bore), fit the new piston and rings, ease the barrel down and do the head nuts up evenly. A good job leaves it turning freely with less wear than the old strip down (a seizure costs 0.15 wear against 0.3, and the new rings take 0.2 off), and then it wants running in as before: ten minutes of gentle riding, or it wears several times faster and keeps a scar. A botched job leaves some scuffing, so it nips up again sooner. The toolbox's `F` still cleans a merely nipped-up engine, and tells you to take a seized one to the bench. Amy writes "top end rebuilt, new piston and rings" in the logbook. Tuning: "Workshop bench" (`topEnd...`).

**The yellow's big-bore kit.** Jess's dream. The yellow quad has a big-bore kit that fits at the workbench (`8`): undo the head, swap the barrel and piston, ease the new barrel over the rings, then do the head nuts up evenly (snug, not tight). Fitted well it pulls about 18% harder, but it makes more heat working, wears the engine about two and a half times as fast (the new piston and rings do take some of the old wear away), and wants a jet about half a size richer than the weather alone, which the toolbox's jet row now takes into account. A badly nipped-up head gives you a bit over half of the power and all of the wear. Once it's fitted it stays fitted for the rest of the game. Open the bench with the kit still to fit and one of the girls mentions the catalogue Jess has left open on it (Amy disapproves, Jess won't let it go); when it's done, Jess can't believe it and Amy warns you about the heat. Amy writes "big-bore kit fitted" in the logbook.

**The orange's coolant.** (The cars, Land Rovers, UTV and tractor have a radiator and coolant too: see Overheating below.) In about half of games (picked from the seed, so `?seed=` repeats it; the console says) a hose on the orange is weeping coolant. It leaks while the engine runs (about 50 minutes from full to dry at the usual rate, and it starts the game anywhere from just over half to nearly full) and a little while it sits hot. Once it's down a bit a green damp patch grows on the ground under it, which Amy reads and Jess pulls a face at. As the level falls the radiator does less: the orange heats like the air-cooled quads, the fan helps less and the temperature needle shows all of it. Low and hot, it steams from the radiator, and in the end it boils over: a big burst of steam, most of what's left gone, and any girl close by jumps back and shouts. At the workbench (`7`) the job lets the radiator cool, nips up the weeping hose clip (snug, not tight, or it keeps weeping a bit), and pours coolant to the line. The yard toolbox can't do it.

**Remappable keys (TODO 84).** Pause with `Esc` and click "Change keys" on the card (the start screen has it too): every key the game uses is listed by what it does, from walking and the throttle to the choke, the indicators and the map. Click one and press the key you want. If that key already does something the two swap, so no key ever does two jobs; a key moved to one nothing uses leaves its old one doing nothing. Changed keys show gold, "Put them all back" resets the lot, and the game remembers your keys in the browser. The help page (`H`) shows your keys instead of the usual ones. `Esc`, `Tab`, `Enter`, the arrows, `Shift`, `Alt`, the function keys and the tuning panel and console keys can't be moved, and the gamepad is not remappable. The FAQ and a few spoken lines still say the usual keys.

**Overheating (TODO 59).** Every temperature gauge now puts a warm engine in the middle of the dial, and the red zone starts three-quarters of the way round. The cars (Fiesta, Corsa), the Land Rovers, the UTV and the tractor have a radiator and a fan like the orange's, so a healthy one idles all day in full sun with the needle in the middle, and crawling or towing doesn't push it out either. They run hot only with something wrong: a leak (about one game in four) that lets the coolant fall, or a thermostat stuck shut (about one vehicle in eight from the start, and a good one can stick after a long run; it takes most of the radiator's cooling with it). Too hot, the engine loses power and runs rough (from half way up the red), steams, and at the top it cuts out and won't catch until it has cooled for a minute or two. Nothing is damaged for good. The dash tells you: the needle in the red, and a lamp or symbol that goes red with it (the tractor has a new TEMP lamp; the UTV's H turns into the symbol; the Fiesta, Series and Corsa and Defender dials light their water symbol and have a bigger red band). The girls say so, Amy what is wrong, Jess cheerfully, and when it cuts out. Fix it with the coolant bottle that stands by the oil bottle at the petrol tank: pick it up with `E`, and with the engine off and cool, look at the vehicle and hold `Y` to top up the radiator (it won't open the cap hot or running); with the radiator full, keep holding `Y` for a few seconds and the flush frees a stuck thermostat. The orange's workbench job also changes the thermostat now, and the other bigger vehicles can use it too. "Coolant bottle" in the Tune tab sets the pour and how hot is too hot; the Vehicles tab has "thermostat stuck shut" under Problems and "running too hot" and "overheated" under Running, and the console has `thermostat` beside `coolant`.

**Spitting back.** A cold engine running lean (not enough choke) coughs back through the carb when you snap the throttle open: a soft whuff from the airbox and a puff of smoke from under the seat, quite unlike the sharp crack of a pop in the exhaust. A girl sitting on it jumps and yelps; Amy says what it is and to use the choke, Jess finds it funny. Warm it up, or keep some choke on, and it stops.

**Running in.** An engine that has just been rebuilt wants about ten minutes of gentle running before it's thrashed: one with its top end rebuilt after a seizure, the barn find on its first start in years, and the yellow with the big-bore kit. Hold it under about 60% of its revs and it's fine (idling counts for half). Thrash it in that time and it wears up to six times faster and keeps some roughness for good, which a service doesn't clear. The mechanical sympathy tally above records who did it. Amy tells you or Jess off for caning a fresh engine; Jess cheers you on.

**Heat.** The air-cooled quads (red, blue, yellow, green) get too hot idling for ages on a sunny day, or crawling through mud with the throttle open: after about four minutes in full sun the needle climbs into the red and the engine starts pinking (a metallic rattle), runs rough and loses power. Riding along at a decent speed blows it away, as does a dull or wet day. The water-cooled orange has a radiator fan that cuts in and out with its own whirr, and never gets that hot, unless it's leaking coolant (below). Switch off a quad you've thrashed and it ticks as the metal cools, slower and slower. It also starts differently while it's hot: the carb boils its fuel, so it starts rich, a choke makes it worse, and holding the throttle open while you crank helps. Amy tells you when it's pinking and when it's ticking; Jess is cheerful about both. **Steam.** A hot engine steams. One that's properly cooking (well past where it starts to pink) gives off a thin wisp from under the seat, and rain falling on a hot engine in the open (one that's been working, not just idling) makes it steam more the harder it rains and the hotter the metal is, running or just switched off. Wet ground counts for a little. Under the barn roof there is none.

**Popping and running on.** Shut the throttle at speed and an engine that's lean, worn or running hot crackles and bangs in the pipe: the two-strokes (yellow and orange) a lot, a healthy four-stroke hardly ever, and more as it wears. Under heavy cloud or rain you can see a small orange flash at the tail pipe with each bang; in sun you can't. Switch off an engine that's hot, worn or lean and it may chug on unevenly for a few more beats before it dies; switch the key back on and it just carries on.

**The mini moto.** In about two games in three a 50cc pit bike is parked with the quads, the Tamura TZ 65, a small motocross bike after a Yamaha YZ65 in sun-faded blue: gold forks and blue legs under a high front fender and a blank number plate, level bars with a pad, thin shrouds and side boards over an engine in the open (the recoil starter's cup on the left, the carb behind the cylinder), a black expansion chamber down the front and under the engine to a silver silencer, an aluminium swingarm and monoshock, spoked blue rims with knobbly tyres and a disc each end, and a chain on the left. Two wheels, one seat, no lights, a key, the choke knob on the left of the carb (it comes well out when it is on) with the fuel tap beside it, and the pull-start (`O`) alone, with no starter motor. It's a two-stroke on pre-mix, so it has no reverse (get off and push) and the same hold-and-yank cord as the yellow. It tops out at about 12 m/s and rides like a bike: the bars ask for a lean, the lean turns it, and the view tips with it a little. Faster corners need more lean, and the ground sets how much it will hold. Power in a deep lean pushes the rear out (ease off and it comes back), and grabbing the brake mid-corner tucks the front. It sounds like a Derbi GPR 50 with a race kit: a lumpy idle that bangs every other turn, cleaning up into a raspy buzz as it revs. It's built in `src/vehicle/miniMotoModel.js`.

**The old three-wheeler.** In about two games in three the neighbour's old ATC is parked with the quads, the Kestrel T185, a three-wheeler after a Honda ATC185S in chalky sun-bleached red: one front wheel on chrome legs in black rubber boots under two red steering columns, a rectangular headlamp between them, a wide flat front fender with a black flap, swept chrome bars with a pad behind the key, a lights rocker by the left grip (`P`) and a thumb throttle by the right, a small tank with the maker's badge and a "185 S" stripe, one wide red rear body over two fat balloon tyres on plain silver rims with black flares and a chrome grab rail and bumper, a long flat seat, a tail light, and the engine in the open: finned barrel, big silver side covers, the recoil starter's cup on the left, the carb behind it with the choke knob and the fuel tap beside it, and a plain pipe to a can between the tyres. ## The Land Rover (the Series III)

**Batteries and the trickle charger.** Every vehicle with a starter has a battery you can lift out and carry. At the workbench the "Take the battery out" job (last on the list, or "Fit the battery you are carrying" once it is out) has you lift the seat, undo the terminals and lift the battery into your hands; in the yard, with the toolbox, `H` takes it out and stands it by your feet, and `H` again fits it if it is lying within a few metres. A battery in your hands is carried like a can: `E` on its own vehicle (switched off) fits it, `G` puts it down, and the UTV's bed takes it (up to six things, and it tips out with the rest). Small for a quad, big for the cars, the tractor and the UTV. With the battery out the vehicle is dead: no dash, no starter click, no jump from the leads, and the battery neither drains nor charges (a pull-start quad still fires on the cord). Stand it on the charger on the workbench, at the west end, with `E` (it runs off the generator, below, so that needs to be going): the needle climbs and the red lamp comes on, and it takes about five minutes from flat to the green lamp (a toast tells you), slowing near the top. `E` with empty hands takes it off again. ## The little tipping trailer

**The girls driving the UTV and the Land Rover.** Jess takes the tractor, the UTV or the Land Rover for a go now and then (she picks whichever is free), and you can ask either girl from the wheel: "Drive it" on the vehicle's slice. Amy refuses all three until her day (the one that unlocks the tractor), then does it when asked. Either girl will start any of the three when asked ("Start it"), Amy included, even before her day. They climb in at the driver's door on the right. The UTV is an automatic: key on, wait for the glow lamp (Jess doesn't always), crank, and away at 2 to 6 m/s. The Land Rover is the manual four-speed: Jess stays in second, sets off badly and grinds first and second whenever she changes into them while moving (no synchromesh), Amy double-declutches and doesn't, and goes up to third. With a flat battery the girl goes to the front and swings the starting handle: Jess has her thumb round it and gets kicked back now and then (ow, and she stops for a few seconds), Amy keeps her thumb alongside and never does. The other girl may climb in beside the driver on the bench (half of goes) and says her piece. Each go is 40 to 70 seconds, then she parks and gets out. **Riding beside her:** parked with nobody at the wheel, look at it and press `B` to sit in the passenger seat by yourself, as on the quads' back seat (`E` gets you off). While a girl is at the wheel of the UTV or the Land Rover, look at it and press `E` to get in on the bench, on the door side or in the middle if the other girl has the window. A small steel tipping trailer stands by the paddock. Back the tractor, or any quad with a rack (the red, blue and green), up to its ring and press `J` to hook it on; `J` again, stopped, takes it off. It's a drag on the quad: less pull and weaker brakes, more so with a girl and things in it (the tractor hardly notices). **The radio.** Both Land Rovers and the girls' cars have one: press `I` with the key on to switch it on and off (`Shift`+`I` louder, `Alt`+`I` quieter; a gamepad has it in the ring). There is one station, which every radio on the farm plays at the same moment: mostly a presenter whose words never quite come out, with a tune now and then and a jingle between. It is all made up in code. Each set sounds different. The Series III's old dash radio is tinny, spits more the harder you rev (it has no ignition suppression) and drifts off the station into static; the 200TDi's is plainer; Amy's Fiesta has a cassette radio with a slow warble; Jess's Corsa has a loud head unit with a lot of bass that holds the station well. It goes quiet while the starter turns and when the battery is flat. From outside you hear one left on from a few metres. "Radio" in the Tune tab sets the volume, the range and the drift, "Radio: the girls" when they reach for it, and "Radio: sound" the mix; the Vehicles tab has the radio under Running, with "Turn every radio on" and "off" among the actions, and the console has `radio`, `radioVolume` and `radios` (all at once). **Reversing a trailer.** Both trailers (the little tipping one and the bale trailer) jack-knife the way real ones do. Going forward they follow you straight; reversing, a trailer that is a little off line swings further off, so you have to steer the back of the quad, tractor or Land Rover towards where the trailer's back end is (steer towards the way you want the back to go, "left hand down" for left). Let it get to about 60 degrees and it folds against what is pulling it: a toast says so, the puller can't reverse any harder into it, and pulling forward straightens it again. Reverse a hitched trailer for a few seconds with Amy about and free and she walks round behind it, to one side, and guides you back: "left hand down", "right hand down", "straight now, keep coming", "stop" before it folds. ## The two trailers, in detail

Both trailers are built like the vehicles now. **The bale trailer** has a timber deck on a steel ladder (box-section rails, cross members, an angle round the edge, coach bolts through the planks), timber side boards in steel stake pockets, a welded tube headboard that stops the bales, an axle on two leaf springs held by U-bolts, and farm-tread tyres on red pressed-steel rims with their wheel nuts. At the front is an over-run coupling with its damper tube, a handbrake lever and brake cable, a breakaway cable, a safety chain, the lights' lead with its socket, a chassis plate and a wound-up jockey wheel; at the back a lights board (red and amber lamps, reflectors, a number plate). **The tipping trailer** has the pressed ribs, the tipping pin through its hinge lugs, a catch at the front with its release lever, a drop tailgate on its hinge pin, a ball coupling (cast head, cup and locking handle) with a safety chain and lead, leaf springs and a lights board on the chassis behind the tub, with small chevron-tread wheels. Both are made by a made-up maker (Fenwick) and both wear and get muddy as the quads do: the tyres first, the bodywork over a long time, from the mud their own wheels roll through; the wear shows in the folds and edges. The Tune tab has "Bale trailer wear" and "mud" (and the same for the tipping trailer) to set how worn and how muddy they are. They don't yet get hosed down.

## Loading a quad on the bale trailer

The bale trailer keeps its bales at the front now, with a clear bed behind them (the girls sit on the low bales, facing back). Two aluminium ramps lie on the bed behind the bales, walking face up and in sight: `T` by the back of it, or from the tractor's seat when you're stopped, slides them back along the bed and drops them to the ground, or back up again (up isn't allowed with a quad on them). With the ramps down, switch a quad off and hold `X` within reach of the foot of the ramps: the trailer's winch drags the nearest one across the grass, turns it nose first and winds it up the ramps. Or push it yourself: line a quad up straight on the foot of the ramps and hold `V`; it climbs slowly while you push and rolls back down if you let go (a winched one stays put on the cable). Once it's aboard, `E` on it straps it down; undo the strap and `Shift` + `X` lets it down the ramps again. An unstrapped quad slides about on the bed when you brake or pull away hard, thumps the front or goes out of the back (with the ramps down), so strap it before you tow it home behind the tractor, which feels the extra weight. The tractor, the UTV and the Land Rovers are too big to load; a running quad, one with somebody on it or one tipped over won't go on either. The girls say something when it's up, when it's strapped and when one slides off. Debug panel (Vehicles) and the console (`load`, `unload`) can winch any quad on or off at once.

## The dashes

Each quad's own dash is close to the real machine it's modelled on, which for most of them means very little:

- **Red** (after a Honda Foreman): a rectangular 0 to 45 mph speedo with an hour meter and an odometer, three lamps (R, N and an oil can) and the key. No rev counter, fuel or temperature gauge.
- **Blue** (after a Yamaha Grizzly): a cyan-backlit LCD in a charcoal pod: big speed digits, the odometer (alternating with the trip every four seconds) and the hours, a thermometer and a fuel pump symbol that light when it is hot or low, a column of L, H, R and P lamps (H going, R in reverse, P stopped in neutral), a DIFF.LOCK lamp and two round buttons. No rev counter.
- **Loan quad** (after a Yamaha Kodiak 450): the same idea, wider and glossier, with a crisper LCD, a gear column and a fuel sweep. Its pod on the left bar has the lights rocker, the engine stop and the start button, and no choke lever.
- **Green** (after a Kawasaki Bayou): a round black speedo on a bracket just over the middle of the bars: white mph to 40 round the edge with a blue km/h scale inside it, an odometer window, N and R lamps and the key under the needle's hub. Nothing else.
- **Yellow** (after a Yamaha Blaster): the key in a small black housing on the bar clamp, and nothing else: no lamps and no gauges.
- **Orange** (after a Suzuki LT250R): a pod on the left bar with a yellow lights rocker, the choke lever and a RUN / OFF kill switch with its lanyard.
- **Old three-wheeler**: the key on the bar's crossbar, a lights rocker by the left grip (`P`) and a thumb throttle under the right one that moves as you open it.
- **Kids' quad** (a pull-start, key-only quad) and **mini moto**: a key switch and nothing else.

To make up for it there are **two accessory pods**, and there's only one of each: an older **analogue** pod (a cream speedo and rev counter with drum counters, small fuel and temperature dials set between them, a row of lamps along the top) and a new **digital** one (amber on black: a bar-graph rev counter, speed, fuel and temperature blocks, gear letter, clock and a line cycling odometer, trip and hours). Each shows everything for whichever quad it's fitted to, with its rev counter's red band starting at that quad's own redline. Each game they start in the toolbox or on a random quad (the console says where), and you move them with the toolbox (see above). The K key still turns the key on every quad; the dashes go dark with the ignition off or the battery flat.

Every quad starts a new farm with some hours and miles on it (the Blaster and the loan quad low, the green's well into four figures), and they add up as you run and ride, from one day to the next. The hour meters count game hours, the same as the dash clock, so an hour of running on the meter is an hour on the clock (about five real minutes at the usual rate).

The fuel tap's position isn't on any dash now: look at the tap itself.

## Riding with her

There are two of them, each starting beside her own quad in the yard. **Amy** (blonde, the taller at 1.58 m, in a polo the colour of the quad she likes best, usually done right up) is the one you know. **Jess** (black hair, shorter at 1.52 m and curvier, in a polo the colour of hers, often with a few buttons undone) is cheekier: she winds you up, is braver behind the bars, gets bored sooner and is more likely to take a quad off on her own, and she usually enjoys being left on a runaway. What they wear on top changes from day to day: usually the polo, sometimes a plaid flannel shirt over the polo (buttoned, with the polo's collar out over the shirt's, or worn open), sometimes a white or black polo a size bigger over their own, now and then with the collar popped, and on a very warm day Jess may turn up in just a string crop-top. Each has her own voice and lines. They don't interact with each other, except that each leaves alone a quad the other is busy with, and one waits for the other to finish before chattering. Their names, looks and personalities are in `src/npc/characters.js` (Jess's lines in `src/npc/jessLines.js`). They're both after you, and it shows: they bicker. Standing about together near you, they have a go at each other now and then; talk to one with the other close by and the other chips in; ride past one with the other on your back and they shout at each other; come back from a ride and the one left behind has something to say; one watches the other drive and heckles; one gets stuck and the other enjoys it; and sharing a quad, they squabble about the driving. Amy is sweet with an edge, Jess is cheeky and competitive. Their exchanges are in `src/npc/banterLines.js`. They can share a quad, one driving and one on the back: ask one to get on the back of the quad the other is driving (she pulls up to let her on), or to drive the quad the other is sitting on the back of, and now and then a bored one hops on the back of her friend's quad as it sets off. With her friend aboard the driver goes round bigger loops for a minute and a half to over three minutes, then both get off. With both seats taken there's no room for you. A third place is the front rack, on the red, blue and green: with you on the back and one girl driving, ask the other from her wheel to "Sit on the front" of that quad (or now and then a bored one hops on as it sets off). You can also ask either girl to "Sit on the front" of any of those three while it's parked with nobody on it, then climb on and ride off with her up there; she stays on until you ask her to get off. She sits side by side with nothing to hold, legs hanging over the nose, the driver pulls up for her, and the extra weight takes a little steering lock off. When the driver's done and stops, she gets off at the front. Everything below applies to both.

Look at one of them and press `E` to open her wheel. Move the mouse towards a slice and click (or press its number). The first ring has a slice for each quad she could do something with, in its colour, plus anything that isn't about a particular quad (answering her when she's asked to get off, getting off the one she's on, or stopping while she's driving); a quad's slice opens a second ring of what she could do with it. Right-click (or Backspace) goes back a ring; `E` closes it. Riding on the back while she drives, `E` opens a one-ring wheel for the ride. The options:

- **Get on the red / blue / yellow quad**: she walks over and sits on the pillion seat. Get on and ride; she stays on. Asking her onto the other quad while she's sitting on one, she gets off and walks over.
- **Start the red / blue / yellow quad**: she walks to its handlebars, turns the key, checks the fuel tap (usually), puts the choke on if it's cold (usually), and cranks in short goes, talking herself through it. If it catches she eases the choke off as it warms, then leaves it to you; after four goes she gives up and switches it off. Get on while she's at it and she stops.
- **Take me for a ride on the red / blue quad**: she gets on the front, starts it and waits for you to get on the back (look at the quad and press `E`). She drives bigger loops and keeps going while you're aboard. Press `E` on the back to talk to her: stop here, slow down, go a bit faster, let me off, or let me drive (she gets off, you slide forward, and she hops on the back). You can also get on the back whenever she's driving and going slowly.
- **Sitting on the back by yourself**: look at a parked quad and press `B`. If nobody's driving it, after 6 to 25 seconds a free girl (usually the nearer) walks over, says so, gets on the front, starts it and drives you about in big loops. Unlike a ride you asked for, this is her idea and it lasts as long as one of her drives (a minute to two and a half): she gets bored, pulls up and gets off without waiting for you to say stop, leaving you on the back. Nobody offers again for a while: 45 seconds to 2 minutes after Amy's ride, 25 to 70 seconds after Jess's (hers also last a bit less, 45 to 110 seconds). If you get off before she's got on, she gives up after 25 seconds. You can still talk to her with `E` and ask her to stop or let you off. On the back by yourself, `E` gets you off.
- **Taking you to see something**: with you on the back, most of her drives go somewhere with a purpose when there is something to show: a gate that is usually shut and has been left open, the view up the hill, or a quad left running with nobody on it. She drives there by the gateways, pulls up short, looks, points and says why (for a quad left running, whose fault it was), stays six to nine seconds, then drives you back to where you got on. With nothing to show she loops or goes on an ordinary outing as before.
- **Follow me on the red / blue / yellow quad**: she gets on that one, starts it and waits for you to set off on another, then follows about 8 m behind at your pace, up to about 25 mph (less if she's feeling careful, more if confident). Get too far ahead and she shouts after you; stop and she pulls up behind you and waits. Get off your quad and she waits a while (30 to 90 seconds; Jess less), then gives up on you: either she goes off for a drive on her own (more likely the more confident she is) or she pulls up and gets off or moves to the back, as after any drive. Talk to her to finish sooner.
- **Drive the red / blue / yellow quad**: she gets on the front, starts it like the learner she is, and potters round a loop near where she started for about a minute. Six times in ten, though, she sets off somewhere instead: a place on the farm (the drag strip, the muck heap, the trough, the end of the road, the foot of the hill, the jump, the fields, the track), by the gateways, a bit quicker than her loops, stops there for a few seconds and drives back, and she keeps going long enough to get there and back. How she drives depends on how she feels that day: careful (creeping, shaky, lots of stops, quick to get scared) or confident, and the more confident the bolder: at the very top she'll do up to about 28 mph in big loops out across the field for three minutes or more, picking her way slowly out of the yard and among parked quads and only opening it up with room around her. A fright makes her more careful next time while a good go builds her confidence. She then stops, bored or scared, and gets off or hops on the back. While she drives you can ask her to stop and get off or move onto the back. Get on and she shuffles back; if her quad is too fast to climb onto, press `E` on it to ask her to stop and let you on.
- **Stand next to the red / blue quad**: she gets off if she's on, or walks over if she isn't, and stands beside it facing it.

The wheel only offers what makes sense: no "get on" while she's already on, no "stand next to it" while she's already there. She stays put on the pillion when you get off until you ask her to get down. Jump off at speed with her on the back and she'll let you know what she thinks.

**Teaching them.** Each girl is better or worse at four things: starting, the choke, cornering and mud. Amy corners better, Jess is better in mud, and both start fairly unsure about the choke. Where she's weak she makes the mistake more often (forgets the tap or choke, goes into corners too hot and yanks the bars, gives up in a bog sooner) and says so herself ("I always forget the choke"). Riding on the back while she drives, `E` has a "Coach her" slice whenever there's something to coach: "Choke off now" while the engine's warm and the choke's still on, "Short bursts on the starter" while she's cranking or after a stall, "Ease off into the corners" in a turn or after a yank, "Keep it going through the mud" when she's stuck or sinking. Advice at the right moment teaches her that skill a good step and pleases her a little; the same advice again within 25 seconds is nagging, which teaches nothing and annoys her; advice at the wrong moment puzzles her. She also improves very slowly on her own by getting it right. As she improves her lines change from "I'm no good at it" to "I think I'm getting it" to "I've got this", and she starts doing it before you can say. Skills last for the game only. The tuning panel has "Skills (live)" with each level and a "Skills" folder with the numbers.

**Driving lessons.** Drive a manual quad with a girl on the back and, once you have been at it a while, she may offer to teach you. Amy sets one drill at a time and judges each go by what the quad did (stalls, how long you rode the clutch, revs, speed lost in a change): pulling away without revving it, clutch control on a hill, changing down before a bank, smooth changes, and stopping without stalling. Two clean goes in a row passes a drill, a bad one starts the count again, and four bad goes in a row ends the lesson. If you do not have a go at it she says it again, and eventually gives up. Jess has one lesson, "faster": reach a set speed from a standing start in a few seconds. Each drill passed pleases her a little and passing all of Amy's earns a bucket-list tick. What you have passed is kept in the farm save. The Girls tab has a "Lessons" folder for each girl (and a button to start one) and the Tune tab has "Driving lessons"; the console has `girl amy lesson`.

**They coach each other.** When the girls share a quad, the one on the back coaches the one driving, the same way you can: "It's warm now. Choke off." Amy coaches the choke, the starter and corners, patiently and at the right moment. Jess coaches mud, loudly, and also pipes up when nothing's wrong and says it again straight after, which counts as nagging. It teaches by your rules, so the right moment helps and the wrong one doesn't, and their skills drift without you being involved.

**Favours.** After the first few minutes, a girl who's stood about near you (you on foot) now and then asks for something, with a line to say so: "Could you give my quad a wash?" (when her favourite quad is properly muddy), "Take me over to the straw bales" (she has to be on the back of your quad when you stop near them: ask her onto the back from her wheel, and the bales are the straw ones out in the field) or "Fetch the jerry can" (bring it to within a few metres of her, in your hands or put down). Each girl has at most one request open and asks no more than every 4 minutes or so. Nothing on the screen tells you; if you forget, talk to her and she reminds you. Do it and she thanks you and likes you noticeably more (it counts like a lot of rides towards who you favour), and the favoured one may crow about it if her friend's close. If you leave it six minutes it lapses: she likes you a bit less, remembers for ten minutes, says so the next time you talk to her, and is more likely to say no if you ask her to do something with a quad. Doing a later favour for her clears it. The tuning panel has a "Favours asked" folder.

**Round the back of the barn.** When a girl likes you a lot (well ahead of her friend, and not fallen out with anyone) she may come up while you're stood about on foot and ask you round the back of the barn. Talk to her: her wheel has "Go round the back of the barn" and "Not now". Say yes and the screen fades to black, you're stood facing her behind the barn, she says her piece, and it fades out again for a few seconds and comes back with her saying a little more. Do that twice and the next time she asks you to meet her at her favourite quad: she sits on the back and waits, you stand beside her and pick "Stand next to her on the quad" from her wheel, with a longer fade. After that she is yours, the next day too: her favour can't drop, whatever you do with her friend, and her friend sulks. Only one girl can be yours. The one thing she won't forgive is tipping a quad over with her on it: she is furious, her favour crashes and she starts again from nothing. Nothing is shown beyond the fade. The tuning panel has a "Romance" folder.

**Comparing notes.** Each girl keeps her own tally of how much each quad has shaken her, so each has a favourite and they don't always agree. Stood about together (and once each has felt a fair bit of shake), now and then one asks the other which quad shakes her most, each names hers (by colour or nickname), and either they agree or they argue. A third of the time they disagree, they swap to settle it: each goes and sits on the back of the other's pick for about 45 seconds, then they give their verdicts. Amy writes the two tallies added together in her logbook on the bench as one line, "Buzz ranking: Green, Orange, Red", which she replaces each time it changes. In the debug panel "Both girls compare which quads shake them most now" starts one (console `girl amy compare`), the Girls tab shows when they are at it, and the tuning folder is "Comparing notes".

**Numb.** A long sit on a shaking seat is too much of a good thing. A girl sitting on the back of a parked, running quad of her own choice (not holding it for you, waiting for you or warming it up) builds numbness the harder it shakes: a cold or rough engine in a couple of minutes, a smooth warm one far longer. First her legs go tingly ("My legs have gone all tingly"), then her bum goes numb ("My bum has gone numb"), and she gets off, walks it off a few steps away, stretches ("Ow, ow, ow, pins and needles!"), and gets back on if the quad is still running. Nothing builds while you ride with her, and it drains away while she is off the seat. In the debug panel each girl's folder shows her numbness and has "goes numb now" (console `girl <name> numb`), and the tuning folder is "Numb bum".

**Buzz-seeking.** The girls like the shake, and three more things they ask you for are about it, as favours like the wash and the bales (you on foot, close, now and then): **"Take me over the cattle grid again"**, done when you ride over it with her on the back; **"Go the bumpy way"**, done when her seat has slammed a few times riding rough ground with you; and **"Can I sit on the tractor's mudguard while it idles?"**, which means starting the tractor, when she climbs up at once and it is done. Doing them pleases her and ignoring them is remembered, as for the others. On her own, when she is bored, a girl may also go and sit on the back of a worn, rough quad for the shake, starting it first if it is off: Jess readily and openly ("This one shakes properly now. Mine."), Amy now and then and without saying why. In the debug panel each girl has "asks you to take her over the cattle grid again", "asks you to go the bumpy way", "asks you to start the tractor so she can sit on its mudguard" and "goes and sits on the roughest quad near her for the shake" (console `girl <name> ask-grid`, `ask-bumpy`, `ask-mudguard`, `buzz-sit`).

**Don't fix it.** A worn, rough engine shakes more, and the girls like the shake. Service or put right the quad that is a girl's favourite when it had gone rough (a service with the toolbox, a repair, or a job at the bench that takes the wear off), and if she is close enough she minds: Jess says you've made it boring, Amy wanted it fixed but says she'll miss it. They don't nag: once a minute at most. In the debug panel each girl has "minds that you fixed up her favourite" (console `girl <name> minds-fix`), and the tuning folder is "Minding a fix".

**Sitting on it while it warms.** A cold engine shakes about twice as hard as a warm one, and the girls like it. When a girl starts your usual vehicle to have it warm for you (the head start), three times in four she doesn't stand beside it: she sits on it while it warms, on the back of a quad or in the passenger seat of a Land Rover or the UTV. Now and then, if you are close enough to hear, she asks first ("Can I sit on it while it warms up?") and a small wheel lets you say "Go on" or "No, I'll take it" (closing it without answering is a no). She says so as it starts to smooth out, and is disappointed when it has (the cold light going out). She is slow to get off when you arrive: a few seconds and a "Hang on, I only just got comfortable". If nobody comes for about three minutes she gets off anyway. In the debug panel each girl has "starts the picked vehicle and sits on it while it warms" (console `girl <name> warm-sit <vehicle>`), and the tuning folder is "Sitting while it warms".

**Seats that ride differently.** Where you sit changes how the bumps reach you, whether it's you or one of the girls. Each kind of seat is on a spring of its own: the pillion pad over the rear axle, the front rack over the nose, a cab seat on its cushion, the 200TDi's back bench on its leaf springs, and the UTV's bare bed. Further back rides the pitch harder than the front. The back bench is soft, bouncy and lightly damped, and comes down hard on a big bump; the bed is short and sharp, and a big one (the cattle grid, say) throws whoever is in it up off the floor. Your eyes move with the seat when you sit there. When a seat slams, the girl on it reacts now and then: an "ow" on the pillion pad or the rack, a moan about the springs on the back bench, a "whoa" when the bed throws her up. In the tuning panel each vehicle has a "Seats" folder with a gain, spring rate, damping, travel and slam point for each kind.

**Ready and waiting.** Tell a girl "I'll be a minute" (on her wheel, with the options not about a particular vehicle) and she goes to your usual quad (the one you ride most), or her favourite, or the nearest with a free back seat, starts it, and sits on the back of it with the engine running, saying so. Every so often she revs it. As the minute stretches she sounds the horn: Jess from about 40 seconds, Amy from about 75, then more often and for longer, with a word or two. Come back (within a few metres, or get on) and she says so; she sits on the back and you take the front as usual. Keep her waiting too long (about 2 and a half minutes for Jess, 3 and a half for Amy) and she gives up, sometimes switching it off, and gets off. In the debug panel each girl has "gets a quad going and waits on the back for you" (console `girl <name> wait-ready`), and the tuning folder is "Ready and waiting".

**Sulking in the cab.** After a falling out goes badly for her (the race's loser, the one whose quad was taken), or when she feels neglected by you, a girl may storm off to the Land Rover, start it for the heat and sit in the passenger seat on her own. Nobody else gets in with her. Look at it and press `E` to knock on the window. **Amy** ignores the first knock, gets the window down and answers curtly on the second, and forgives you on the third (a fourth if she feels neglected). **Jess** revs the engine at you instead, three times, then gives in on the fourth. Each knock that doesn't do it takes a bit off her sulk; knock three times in a row too quickly and she's worse. Left alone she gets out after two or three minutes, saying she has had enough. Only one girl sulks at a time, and she won't do it again for ten minutes. In the debug panel each girl has a "sulks alone in a Land Rover" button (console `girl <name> sulk`), and the tuning folder is "Sulking in the cab".

**Falling out.** Now and then the girls fall out with each other: after a race, one taking a quad that is the other's, or a run of digs. For four to six minutes they won't share a quad (no riding on each other's back or rack), won't set off in a convoy, keep a few metres apart, and the banter turns sharp. The first time you come close to each, she tells you her side. Then her wheel has two extras: "You're right" (she likes you more, her friend less, and the row lasts longer) and "Make it up" (shortens it; do it to both girls in quick succession and they make up at once). Stand them together near you, say at the bales or the barn, and it runs down faster. In the end one says sorry and they make up. Nothing on the screen tells you. The tuning panel has a "Falling out" folder and a live one with buttons to start or end a row.

**Places to be.** On a sunny or sunny-intervals day with the ground dry, a bored girl sometimes decides to go and sit somewhere instead of waiting at a quad: on top of one of the straw bales out in the field, or on the paddock fence. She says so on the way, sits for one to three minutes, now and then says something about it, and gets down by herself. When the rain gets going (a few seconds to about 20 after it sets in), any girl who's about, even one sat on a bale, runs for the barn and sits on one of the bales inside, looking out at the yard (out in the east fields, the Dutch barn or the field shed if it's nearer); once it's been dry for 15 to 45 seconds she comes out again. Two girls don't take the same place. Talk to her and she gets down first ("Hang on") and then the wheel opens; one sat out can still be asked to drive you about, or offer you a ride when you sit on the back of a quad. The tuning panel has a "Places" folder.

**Batteries.** Every quad has a battery you can't see a gauge for. Leaving the ignition on with the engine off drains it (flat in about eight minutes), cranking drains it faster, and running charges it by the revs: about ten minutes revved, far longer at idle, where a single with the loan quad's, tractor's, UTV's or Land Rover's lamps on (the ones run off the battery) barely holds even and the lamps dim on a weak battery until you rev it. Under about 40% a petrol engine with a starter can't keep its ignition going at idle: after a jump start it dies again at the first stop unless the revs are kept up (diesels and the pull-start engines don't mind). Amy will sit on the back of one and hold the revs for ten minutes or so, saying that is what it needs; Jess gets bored after a minute and lets it idle, so it goes flat again later. Below about 60% the starter turns more slowly and a start is less likely to catch; below 25% it only clicks, once per press; below 10% the dash goes dark. The girls notice and say so ("it's turning over really slowly", "the battery's flat"), and so do they when you're the one struggling near them. To get going again:

- **Pull-start (`O`, the two-strokes only).** Look at the quad (or sit on it), have the ignition on, hold `O` to draw the cord and let go to yank. Holding for about half a second to a second is a proper pull; too short is weak, too long is a botched yank. A pull turns it over for about a second and a bit, works with no battery at all, and needs the choke and a cold engine's usual coaxing. Jess, faced with a flat one, will have a go at the cord herself (Amy gives up and hands it to you).
- **Knacks.** The green used to be stubborn when cold (blip the throttle twice, then crank) but the girls couldn't get it going, so it now starts like the others (`knackBlips` in its spec brings it back). The blue floods if you leave the choke on once it's warming, so ease it off early. The girls know and will tell you.
- **Jump leads.** They start on the workbench in the barn; pick them up (`E`), look at the flat quad and press `E` to clamp the first end, then at a quad with its engine running and `E` again. The running quad charges the flat one slowly, about 1% a second, while they're joined (a flat battery wants a minute or two, then a good while running); the leads only stretch about 4.5 metres, so park them nose to nose, and they come off if they're pulled too far. `E` on a clamped quad takes them off; `G` puts them down.
- **Bump-starting.** A manual with a flat battery (the red, yellow, green and the old three-wheeler, the tractor and the Land Rovers) can be started by rolling it. Key on, clutch in (`Shift`), a gear, and get it moving; let the clutch out and the wheels turn the engine over, with the same chance of catching as the starter has (choke, cold, fuel and flooding all still matter). It needs about 1.5 m/s in second. A two-stroke catches readily and a four-stroke less so. Roll it down a bank, have the tractor tow it on the chain, or sit in it, talk to a girl and choose "Push start it": she goes round the back and pushes it up to a walking pace, turns the key on if you haven't, and shouts "Now!" when it's fast enough to drop the clutch (Jess shouts, pushes harder and carries on longer; Amy tires sooner). The automatics and the pull-starts have no clutch to drop. It is on the bucket list.

**Nicknames.** Early in a game, while she's free and you're near, Jess gives the quads nicknames, one every forty seconds or so ("Right. The green quad is Grandad from now on."). Each quad's name is picked from a short list per game, so the green might be Grandad or Kermit, the yellow the wasp or Custard. Amy says she won't use them. From then on the names turn up in what the girls say about a quad (whose it is, and which one they can hear). Amy holds out for a few mentions, then slips and uses one, and Jess pounces on it. Names are in `src/npc/quadNames.js`; the tuning panel has a "Quad names" folder.

**My quad.** Each girl claims one quad for the game (never the same one; it's also the one she likes best), and once you're on foot near her and it, she tells you so. If somebody else rides it (you, or her friend) she objects, with a different line for each: Amy tells off Jess, Jess says you owe her. Now and then, picking a quad for her own drive, Jess takes Amy's on purpose, says so, and teases her when she complains. A girl goes for her own quad first when she wants a drive. In `src/npc/ownership.js`; the "Whose quad" folder.

**Who's that.** A girl stood about who hears her friend on a running quad she can't see (between about 28 and 130 metres off) says which one it is and how it sounds: flat out, rough (cold or worn) or steady ("That's Jess on the blue. She never lets it idle."). In `src/npc/earing.js`; the "Hearing a quad" folder.

**Warming their hands.** On a cold or wet day (overcast, drizzle, rain or fog), a girl stood about near a quad that's running with nobody on it walks over and holds her hands out over its engine for half a minute to over a minute. Leave one idling and the other often joins her, one each side. Switch it off while they're at it and they complain. In `src/npc/warming.js`; the "Warming hands" folder.

**Sheltering in a cab.** When it rains, a girl stood about (or sat out on the bales, or warming her hands) soon wants to be out of it, and may climb into the UTV or a Land Rover instead of running for the barn (a little more often than not, and only if one is free and within 45 metres). On a cold dry day she may walk to a Land Rover, start it and sit in it for a minute or two to get warm. She gets out when the weather turns or she is bored, and switches off an engine she started. Get into it yourself and she comes along as a passenger. In `src/npc/cabShelter.js`; the "Shelter in a cab" folder, and the Girls tab's "gets into the nearest cab" and "starts a Land Rover and sits in it" buttons (console: `girl amy shelter`, `girl amy warm-up`).

**Hand on the throttle.** Riding along with a girl on the back, now and then she reaches her right arm round you to your throttle grip and gives it a handful for five to ten seconds (up to 90% throttle, though your own input still counts if it's higher). Jess does it again and again, at least 40 seconds apart, and gloats. Amy does it once in a game, then apologises. Not when you're stood still or already doing 25 mph or more. In `src/npc/throttleGrab.js`; the "Hand on the throttle" folder.

**Two-stroke smoke.** Amy hates it: stood downwind of a running yellow or orange (or just behind it in still air) she coughs, waves a hand in front of her face and complains. Jess loves it and says so. Following you on a two-stroke, Amy hangs nearly twice as far back as usual; Jess tucks in close behind it. In `src/npc/smokeSense.js`; the "Two-stroke smoke" folder, and `followSmokeGap` in the Companion folder.

**Racing.** Two minutes into a game or later, when both girls have been stood about for twenty seconds or so, one dares the other to a race (Jess usually), they pick two different quads (each her own claimed one if it's free), start them and drive to the start line by the yard, marked with orange cones either side of the track (no collision: you can drive straight through). Once both are on the line there's a count-down, then they race one lap of the dirt track, flat out for them, side by side on the grid and keeping well clear of the puddles (which stall a quad at speed) and the bogs. First over the line wins and says so; the other has an excuse. Afterwards they bicker about it, and the wins and losses are kept for the game: once one is two or more wins ahead they go on about the score. If a quad won't start or dies in the race, that girl is out and loses; if neither makes the start line within two and a half minutes, it's called off. A lap takes about a minute and a half. They stage a little behind the line and come up to it along the track, so they end up facing the right way (one that arrives crooked goes round again).

**Racing them.** You can be the third on the grid. Ride a quad up to the start line and sit still with it running and Jess may dare you; press `Enter` there to ask for one yourself (asking is a priority: they stop what they are doing, find a quad and go, and if they can't within fifteen seconds you are told why, e.g. "Amy is carrying something" or "Jess has no quad she can take"; the drag race and the hill climb work the same way, and `Enter` at the climb cones also sends a girl up against you); or, if they start a race while you are on a quad near the line, they invite you in. Line up in the middle lane, stopped and pointing along the track: when all three are on the line the count-down starts. Take too long (half a minute) or get off and they go without you. Your lap is judged by passing through each quarter of it in order and getting back to the line, so cutting across the field doesn't count; stalling, or being off the quad, for 15 seconds puts you out. Win and they have excuses; lose and they gloat. Your wins and losses are kept alongside theirs, though the score they go on about is still the two of them. Not while you ride either of the quads they picked: they pick two others. In `src/npc/race.js`; the "Racing" folder. To see one without waiting, open the tuning panel (`` ` ``) and press "Start a race now" in "Girls (live)", which also shows whose quad is whose, the nicknames, the race score and buttons to make a girl grab the throttle or Jess name the quads.

**Reading the dash.** With the girl on the back and you riding, she sometimes reads the dash out. Amy does it in good time: "Your fuel is on the red" when the fuel light comes on, "The temperature light is on" when it's too hot, and "It is still cold, go easy" if you're going hard on a cold engine. Jess does it when it's too late: when the tank's nearly empty, after it's been hot for a while, or after you've thrashed it cold and it has warmed up. Each only once per spell, and one reading every 45 seconds at most. In `src/npc/dashReading.js`; the "Reading the dash" folder.

**Shouting a fault.** Ride off and a girl behind you, or alongside, sees what you can't from the saddle and shouts it: blue or black smoke from under you, steam off the radiator, a soft tyre, a flat one wobbling, a silencer coming apart. Amy shouts sooner and plainly; Jess a beat later, as a joke. In `src/npc/shoutFault.js`; the "Shouting a fault" folder in the tuning panel.

**Impatient follower.** Jess following you (see "Follow me") who's stuck behind a slow pace (under about 13 mph) for twenty seconds or more may get fed up, go round you on one side, pull up well ahead and wait, looking pleased with herself ("Took your time!"). When you've gone by she tails you again. Amy doesn't (`overtakeChance` in the Companion folder is 0 for her).

**Drag racing.** A rough gravel straight in the middle of the field, inside the track and clear of the mud, marked only by cones at the start and the finish (the ground under it is levelled, about 56 m from line to line). Two ways in. Ride up to the start and press `Enter`: Jess or Amy takes the other lane, and you line up on yours, stopped and pointing along the strip. Or sit still on a quad with the engine running for ten seconds or more and Jess may ride over, pull up beside you and blip her throttle: blip back (snap yours open from closed) and she leads you to the strip; ignore her and she calls you chicken. Both lined up, there is a count-down; roll over the line early and it's a false start (you line up again, and the third one calls it off). First over the finish wins, with the wins and losses kept alongside the lap races. After either kind of race, a girl sometimes pulls up and gets off and sometimes just carries on driving for a bit (`keepRidingChance`, half the time). In `src/npc/drag.js`; the "Drag race" folder in the tuning panel, with a "Start a drag race now" button in "Girls (live)".

**Hill climb and the records board.** Big orange cones on the hill pasture mark a timed climb: two pairs a few metres apart for the start just past the stream and for the finish near the top, and one pair (a cone either side) for each of the two checkpoints on the way up, nine metres across, so the line between them is yours (the gentler ground in the east is the easy way, the banks in the west are the quick, risky one). Ride up to the start cones and press `Enter`, then ride through them: the clock runs from there, you must go through each gate in order, and it stops over the finish. Stall, get off for ten seconds or take more than two and a half minutes and you're out. Jess (keener) and Amy also choose to climb it against the clock in their free time: they ride down the lane, across the ford, wait on the line and go, and say something at the top, or when their quad dies on the bank. Whoever is near the top times you and says so. Everything goes on a second chalkboard inside the barn, on the back wall beside the jobs board (press `E` at either for a close view that shows both): the best lap of the track, drag run, hill climb and longest jump (measured from take-off to landing, for you and the girls on any quad) on the farm, with who set it. Times are also kept for each quad, so you're told when it's your best on the one you're on. A new record is a toast, and Bucket list: King of the hill and On the board. The records are kept from day to day with the farm, and one set today is marked so. "Hill climb" and "Records" in the debug panel's Tune tab; the Girls tab can send a girl up the hill, take you to the start, show the records and clear them; the console's `farm.records` and `farm.climb`.

**Amy's cone course.** A slalom of orange cones across the grass west of the middle of the field, inside the track: a first pair and a finish pair, with six gaps between that swing left and right in turn. Ride up to the first cones on any vehicle and press `Enter` (at the cone course, not the hill climb's cones), then ride through them when you are ready and weave through every gap in order and on through the finish. The score is your time plus two seconds for each cone knocked down and five for each gap missed. The cones really go over when something moving touches them, and lie there until Amy stands them up a few seconds after a run (she says so), someone starts another run, or you look at the first cones and press `E`. Amy and Jess both run it in their free time: Amy slowly and carefully, Jess fast and, now and then, aiming for the cones on purpose. Whoever is near the finish times you. Scores go on a fifth line of the records chalkboard (best per vehicle too). Bucket list: Slalom and Not a cone down. "Cone course" in the Tune tab sets the penalties and how keen each girl is; the Girls tab has buttons to ask for it, knock the cones down and stand them up, take you to the start, and for each girl to run it; the console's `farm.cones` path shows it. Jess's "knocking them on purpose" is a way she drives the course, not a separate prank where she wanders over and flattens them.

**Follow the leader.** Two and a half minutes into a game or later, when both girls have been stood about for twenty seconds or so, one (Jess more often) suggests going for a ride together. Each takes a quad: one leads in big loops, the other waits for her to set off, then follows at her pace, like following you. After a minute or two the follower overtakes, goes ahead and waits, and the one she passed follows her; they bicker about the pace. After two swaps and a last stretch they stop and get off. Not while a race is on, and `N` ends it. In `src/npc/convoy.js`; the "Follow the leader" folder.

**Doughnuts.** Bored, Jess sometimes takes a quad off to a clear bit of field and spins doughnuts for 18 to 32 seconds, tearing it up (it acts as mud under the wheels, throws clods, and leaves ruts that last four minutes instead of forty seconds). Amy, if she's about and stood still, disapproves out loud now and then, and afterwards often has a go herself on a bigger, gentler circle. In `src/npc/doughnuts.js`; the "Doughnuts" folder, and `boredDoughnutChance` in the Companion folder.

**Showing off.** Bored, with you in sight, a girl sometimes takes a quad out to put on a show for you (Jess more than Amy, and only a girl who is feeling confident). She lines up about 30 m away and rides at you. On a two-stroke it is usually a wheelie: the nose comes up for a couple of seconds as she goes past a few metres to your side. On any quad it can be a skid: she stands on the brakes so she stops beside you. Then she looks over to see if you saw it. If you were looking her way during it she is chuffed and says so; if you weren't, she tells you off and goes back to do it again, up to three goes, before giving up on you. In `src/npc/showOff.js`; the "Showing off" tuning folder, and `boredShowOffChance` in the Companion folder. The debug panel has "Jess shows off to you" in the Girls folder.

**Lining them up.** The quads start the game parked any old how. Bored in the yard, Amy sometimes moves them one by one into a neat row in front of the barn doors, noses to the barn: she gets on the untidy one nearest her, drives it slowly in line with its slot and parks it, then (usually) goes on to the next. One that is only a little off its slot she nudges straight by hand. Now and then Jess parks one crooked on purpose, and a few seconds later Amy notices and puts it right. In `src/npc/lineUp.js`; the "Lining them up" folder (the row), and `boredLineUpChance` and `lineUpCrookedChance` in the Companion folder. The debug panel (backtick) has "Amy lines the quads up by the barn", "Jess parks one crooked" and "Jess does doughnuts" in the Girls folder.

**Girls carrying things.** When a girl moves the jerry can or the toolbox (Amy hiding them as a prank, and fetching them back after ten minutes) she walks up, bends and picks it up in her right hand, carries it at her side leaning away from the weight (a full can slows her to about four fifths of her usual pace, the toolbox a little less), and bends again to set it down. You can't pick it up while she has it. If she's called away on the way she sets it down where she is. The "Girls carrying things" folder in the tuning panel has the numbers. In `src/npc/carrying.js` and `src/npc/companionCarrying.js`.

**Tea on the rack.** Now and then a free girl fetches a mug of tea (blue for Amy, red for Jess) and leaves it on the rear rack of a parked quad that has one (the red, blue, green and loan). Ride off without looking and it falls off, and its owner lets you know; the spilt tea stays on the ground for a minute. Left alone, she comes back for it after a minute or two. She carries the mug in her hand, sets it on the rack, and when she comes back for it has a sip first. In `src/npc/tea.js`; the "Tea on the rack" folder, and a debug button.

**Red diesel.** South of the petrol tank (past the hose) stands a squat dark green diesel bowser labelled RED DIESEL, with a dark green jerry can somewhere about the farm (10 litres to start with). Hold `E` at the bowser with any can to fill it with diesel, and pour from a can as usual; nothing on a quad says what's in its tank except how it behaves, though the prompts tell you what's in the can you're holding. Diesel in a petrol quad is slow poison: it takes up the diesel over about 25 seconds of running or cranking, runs rougher, weaker and with misfires, smokes thick grey, and with about 20% diesel in the tank or more it stalls and won't catch again. Putting petrol back in doesn't help. Take the toolbox to it and press `P` ("Pump out the tank and carb") to drain it; the fuel is lost. Amy says so if you pour the diesel can near her, says what's wrong when a quad runs badly or dies on it, and says to drain it if you keep cranking; Jess blames you. Now and then Jess, with a low quad parked near her, takes the diesel can to it herself and pours three litres in ("it's all just fuel"), and Amy, if she's close, tells her off. In `src/engine/diesel.js` and `src/npc/dieselMistake.js`; the "Red diesel mistake" folder, the engine folder's diesel numbers, and a debug button.

**Water in the fuel.** A jerry can left out in the rain collects water (a little at a time, up to about an eighth; the barn and the lean-to keep it off), and about half of the barn finds have it in their old tank too. The prompts say so when you pick the can up. Pour it in and nothing happens at first: water is heavier than petrol, so it settles in a small glass sediment bowl under the fuel tap, which you can see going cloudy and brown. When the bowl is full the rest goes on to the carb, and the engine coughs, runs weak, is hard to start and then cuts out. Unlike red diesel it clears as soon as it is drained: in the toolbox panel `G` ("Drain the water at the tap") lets the water out of the bowl, the tank's bottom and the carb, with about a seventh of a litre of petrol. The diesels have no tap and no bowl. Amy reads it (the bowl, the coughing) and says to drain it at the tap; Jess blames the quad. Tuning: "Water in the fuel" (the rain rate, the bowl's size) and the engine's `fuelWater*` settings; the Vehicles tab's Problems folder sets the water in the tank and how full the bowl is.

On the back she holds on round your waist (look down and you'll see her hands), leans with you into corners, lurches forward under hard braking, and looks about or over your shoulder. Hold `C` or the middle mouse button to look back at her. Left alone on a quad she holds the rails (or the strap on the yellow).

**What they look at.** The girls notice what goes on around them. Each turns her head to whoever is talking (her friend, or you when you talk to her), to a quad starting, stalling, backfiring or being revved nearby (the throttle opened and the engine pulled well clear of its tickover; an idle that hunts or sits high on the choke doesn't count), to a horn, a vehicle going over or one going past, and now and then to you (more often the more she likes you); with nothing much going on she looks about. She holds a look a moment rather than flicking about, tires of a thing after a few seconds and comes back to it later, and a bang turns her head at once. Something held over her shoulder for a second or two and, if she is stood about, she turns round to face it. Before she walks anywhere from standing she looks where she is going first. Busy, she mostly watches what she is doing (the quad she is starting, the road when she is driving) and only glances away for something that matters. On the back behind you she still turns to look at a bang or a quad going past.

**What they want.** Each girl wants things, and what she does follows from them. She gets cold on a dull or cold day and faster in the rain (an idling engine, a cab with the heater on or the barn doorway put that right); she gets lonely with nobody near her, restless for a bit of excitement as the game goes on (a drive, a jump, a ride with you), tired from being on the go, and, stood about with nothing to do, she wants something to do. Every couple of seconds while she's free she weighs up what she could do against what she wants and does the best of it, with a whim so she's never quite predictable: Jess gets restless sooner, Amy feels the cold sooner. In the rain she'll get up off the bales or leave the engine she's warming her hands on for shelter.

**Going about it.** When she's decided, she often says so ("I am going to put the kettle on"), looks where she's going before she sets off, and walks the way it calls for: hurrying out of the rain or after her friend, dawdling off for a look round or a sit down, going briskly for a drive, slower when she's tired and quicker when she's keyed up. Call her over (or ring the bell) while she's on her way to something and she tells you she was busy; it stays on her mind, and when she gets back to it she says so.

**The two of them.** Free, a girl who wants company goes over to her friend if she is stood about a little way off, and the two stand at talking distance facing each other. When one sets off on foot for a look round, a sit down or a cup of tea, the other may tag along a step behind and to one side, and stands with her when she gets there. Amy is the readier to follow (she keeps an eye on Jess); Jess the readier to go over (she can't stand about on her own for long). Walk up to the two of them and each turns a little towards you, so the three of you face the middle. Fallen out, none of this: they keep apart.

**Waiting about.** Stood about, each girl has her own ways of standing (Amy tidy and patient, Jess fidgety), and what's around her adds to them: a tyre within reach she kicks, a seat beside her she wipes, the cold has her rubbing her hands, the sun has her shading her eyes, a shower coming on has her looking up at the sky, and now and then she checks her phone. Amy is the one who wipes the seat; Jess kicks the tyres and is never off her phone.

**How they react.** Something sudden near them shows in how they move, not only in what they say. A backfire or a bang close by makes them flinch, hunched with their hands up; a quad leaping forward from a standstill right by them, or one boiling over, makes them hop back out of the way; revving flat out right beside them, Amy usually covers her ears (Jess rarely does: she likes the noise); a vehicle going over makes them gasp, hands to their mouths; and someone landing a jump they can see gets a cheer, Jess with both arms up and Amy with a clap. Breathing two-stroke smoke or the haze, Amy turns away from it and waves it off. They don't react to their own quad's noise, and they only do what they're free to (not with their hands full or on the back holding on).

She talks, now and then, with captions at the bottom of the screen:

- Riding pillion she reacts to the ride: "Oh, this is fun!" on a smooth run, "This is a bit rough." over bumps, "Careful!" when you go too fast, a gasp when you brake hard (kept on hard for a moment and losing a good bit of speed, not a quick dab; Amy notices sooner than Jess), and something when it stalls or runs out of petrol.
- She likes the quads running. A quad that stalls where she can see it gets her down (worse if it was under her, and a stall while she's driving knocks her nerve a little), and she's relieved when it catches again. With all of them going she says how much she likes it; with most off she wishes they were, is keener to go and start one, and is slower to switch an idling one off.
- She likes the seat shaking her: the harder it shakes, the more she enjoys it. Sat on a buzzing quad she admits to it, and over the game her favourite quad moves to whichever one has shaken her most.
- Getting on and off, they climb properly rather than sliding into the seat. On a quad they step up from the footboard, a hand on the seat, and swing a leg over behind; side-saddle or on the front rack they turn their back to it and hop up; into the UTV or the Land Rover they put a foot up on the sill, duck under the roof and swing in; onto the bale trailer, the tractor or the UTV's bed they get a knee up and haul themselves on. Getting off is the same backwards. On a quad they sit with their feet back on the footrests, sat up.
- Getting off a quad, Jess sometimes swings her leg over the exhaust side (the right) without looking, and if the engine is hot her calf catches the pipe: she yelps and jumps back, and does it again another day. Amy always gets off the other side. "Burns her leg on the exhaust" in the Girls tab (and `girl jess burn-leg`) does it on demand; "exhaustSideChance", "burnFrom" and "burnChance" are in each girl's tuning.
- Standing about, each has her own ways of waiting, changing every few seconds: Amy folds her arms, clasps her hands in front or shifts her weight from foot to foot; Jess puts her hands on her hips, rocks on her heels with her hands behind her back, scuffs at the ground with a toe, has a big stretch or stands with a hand in her back pocket. The "Getting on and off" and "Standing about" folders in the tuning panel hold the timings.
- Sat a while in the UTV or the Land Rover, on the bales, on the UTV's tailgate or on a quad's front rack, they shift about too. Amy folds her arms, rests her hands on her knees, crosses her ankles, leans back on her hands where there's no seat back, or puts an elbow out on the door at the window seat. Jess jiggles a knee, puts her hands behind her head, swings her legs when they're hanging free, slouches down on a bench, leans back on her hands or puts an elbow out. Not on the pillion or the front seat, where they're holding on, nor while lying back or being thrown about on the bales.
- Sometimes (Amy more than Jess) she sits side-saddle on the back instead of astride, with both legs over one side. She holds on less well that way: a hard corner or a big bump or landing makes her yelp and, if it goes on, slide off and be left behind. Her arms round you, when you're the one riding in front, help a lot.
- If you crank a quad near her for a while without it starting, she has a guess at why: the choke if it's cold, flooding if it's rich, the fuel tap if the carburettor is dry.
- Left waiting she sighs, hums, yawns and asks if you're going. Ride off without her and she'll shout after you.
- Sat on the back of a parked quad, every few minutes she asks if she can get off; the menu then has "Yes, hop off" and "No, stay there" (she grumbles and stays). If you don't answer, after several minutes she lets herself off. On the back by her own choice (after one of her drives, or bored), she gets bored of it sooner and gets off. On a long ride together she sometimes asks if you can stop soon.
- Jump off and leave her on a quad driving itself and she's either terrified or having the time of her life (40% of the time), and tells you about it afterwards. Hating it, after 8 to 18 seconds she climbs onto the front and either stops it, switches it off and gets down, or drives it about herself.
- She speaks up the further away you are, and shouts some things (being left behind, a quad riding itself), which carry twice as far.
- She comments when the seat is shaking hard, and has a favourite quad each game.
- Left standing about, sooner or later she does something on her own: wanders off for a look round (sometimes a long one), gets on a quad to wait, has a go at starting one, takes one for a drive, sits down somewhere, warms her hands, puts the kettle on, asks you a favour. What she picks follows from what she wants (see "What they want" above), with a whim so it's never quite the same, and the more confident a driver she's become, the more often she goes for a drive. With nothing much wanted, the wait for something to do is usually somewhere between half a minute and a minute and a half, sooner when she's restless (which comes and goes). Turn this off with `ownMind` in the Companion folder.
- She answers when you ask her something.

Their lines are recorded offline with Chatterbox, a text-to-speech engine that copies a voice from a short reference recording and can be made more or less emotional: each kind of situation has a feel (calm, chatty, cross, excited or scared), so a fright sounds frightened. Amy's reference is the Piper "cori" voice she had before; Jess's is Kokoro's young "Lily" voice saying something excitable, recorded with the emotion turned up further than Amy's. Every line is checked with a speech recogniser and recorded again if it doesn't come out right; a few of Amy's short shouts that Chatterbox can't manage are recorded with Piper instead, and anything still wrong is left to the browser's voice. Lines play from where she is, like her sighs, hums and laughs, which are real recordings (Jess's a shade quicker and higher). After changing the lines in `src/npc/voiceLines.js` or `src/npc/jessLines.js`, re-record them with `tools/generate_companion_voice.py --character amy` (or `jess`; usage at the top of the script). Until then, a new line uses the browser's speech, but only with a voice known to be a woman's (Flo or Shelley on a Mac, Google UK English Female in Chrome, Sonia or Libby in Edge); with none of those she just shows the caption. Her figure (bust, waist, hips) can be adjusted live in the Companion folder of the tuning panel. How chatty she is, her volume, speech rate and pitch, captions and the thresholds she reacts to are in the Companion folder of the tuning panel. The lines are in `src/npc/voiceLines.js`.

## Gamepad

Plug in a standard-layout pad (Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro and most others) and press a button so the browser notices it; the start card says so, and the console says whether one was found as the game loads. `Start` plays and pauses. The keyboard works as ever alongside it.

| Button | On foot | On a vehicle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left stick | Walk (a gentle lean is a gentle walk) | Steer, in proportion |
| Right stick | Look | Look |
| `RT` / `LT` | `LT` pushes a quad | Throttle / brake, as far as you press |
| `A` / `B` | `E` / `G` | `B` gets off (`E`); `A` talks to her as a pillion |
| `X` / `Y` | `X` (winch) / jump | Horn / parking brake |
| `LB` | Clutch (`Shift`) | Clutch; with `D-pad` up and down, the gear lever |
| `RB` | Sit on the back (`B`) | Reverse (`B`) |
| `L3` | Jog | Look back |
| `R3` | The ring of everything else | The ring of everything else |
| `Select` | Help | Help |

The ring holds the keys with no button left: ignition, crank, choke, fuel tap, lights, indicators, hazards, 4WD, diff lock, seat belt, right it, hitch, bucket list and sound. Point with the `D-pad` or the right stick, `A` does it, `B` cancels. Cranking, the choke and the pull-start hold themselves for a moment; any button lets go early. In the girls' wheel the sticks and `D-pad` point, `A` picks and `B` backs out. At the toolbox the `D-pad` picks and adjusts, `X` services, `Y` fits or takes off the silencer, and `B` leaves (the rest of its keys are on the keyboard). At the bench `A` is `E` and `X`, `Y`, `LB`, `RB` pick the first four jobs. The "Gamepad" group in the Tune tab has the dead zones, look speed and stick curves. On a vehicle the pad rumbles (where the browser allows it): the big motor from the engine's beat and the ground's jolts, the small one from the handlebars' buzz when you are in the rider's seat. The "Gamepad rumble" group sets its strength or turns it off.
