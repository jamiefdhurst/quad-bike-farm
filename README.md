# Quad Bike Farm

A small first-person browser game: three quad bikes (two farm four-strokes and a two-stroke sport quad) with properly temperamental single-cylinder engines, a muddy track round a British field, and your girlfriend, who'll hop on the back if you ask.

Three.js, Rapier physics, Web Audio. Desktop, keyboard and mouse.

## Running it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the URL Vite prints (usually http://localhost:5173), click the page to lock the mouse, and you're in. Press `H` to show or hide the controls.

Other scripts:

- `npm test` runs the engine and fuel tests with Vitest.
- `npm run build` makes a production build in `dist/`, and `npm run preview` serves it.

## The quads

- **Red quad**: Honda-style farm quad. Soft and steady, with a low warm idle around 1,150 rpm. Racks, round headlights, a pillion seat.
- **Blue quad**: Yamaha-style farm quad. Idles higher (about 1,450 rpm warm), revs harder and higher, needs more choke from cold and runs lumpier until it's warm, warms a bit quicker and pulls a little harder. It has a brush guard, square headlights, railed racks and a grey seat. Its idle comes from a different recorded machine, so it sounds different too.
- **Yellow quad**: a two-stroke sport quad, Blaster-style. It fires every revolution, so it buzzes rather than thumps, and ring-dings at idle. It's weak low down, then comes on hard around 6,000 rpm and revs past 10,000. It has almost no engine braking, and it slowly loads up its plug if you leave it idling (blip the throttle to clear it). It trails blue-white oil smoke. It's lighter and quicker, with an expansion-chamber exhaust, motocross bars, nerf bars and a race number plate. She can squeeze onto a slim pad at the back. It idles on a Yamaha DT 125 trail bike and revs on a Honda CR85 motocross bike ridden flat out.

- **Green quad**: a Kawasaki-style farm workhorse, older and heavier. A big lazy four-stroke that idles low and lumpy (it idles on a Yamaha Ténéré 250 recording, its quick firings stretched half way to its slow tickover so each thump has a short gap after it, and revs on kyles's Honda ATV pulling away), revs slowly but pulls hard low down, takes an age to warm up, wants plenty of choke, runs rougher than the others and engine-brakes hard. Slower at the top (about 33 mph), with a bigger tank. Boxy bodywork, a single round headlight in a nacelle on the bars, flat mesh racks, a winch, mudflaps and a tan seat.
- **Orange quad**: a Suzuki LT250R-style two-stroke race quad. Lighter and lower than the yellow, revs to 11,500 and comes on harder still around 7,000, rear-wheel drive, barely any engine braking, and smokes like a chimney. A big "22" number plate, long flat fenders, wide nerf bars and a fat expansion chamber. Single race seat, so she can't ride on the back (she can still start or drive it). It idles on a Honda CRM250 recording, so it sounds different from the yellow.

The blue, orange, kids' and loan quads are automatics; the red, green and yellow are manual (below). All start cold. Through the CVT the red tops out at 6,200 rpm and the blue at 6,800 (the yellow revs past 10,000), but the engine works against the ground: it bogs a little as you pull away, cruises nearer 5,700 flat out, labours lower up hills and through mud, flares when the wheels spin or leave the ground, and engine-brakes on a closed throttle with the revs rising with speed downhill. Try climbing the steep bank by the yard on a stone-cold engine with the choke out and it will probably stall. Each quad has its own character, the same every game: how it idles, how rough it runs and how it shakes. The red is the smooth one; the blue idles fast and keen with a quicker buzz; the yellow ring-dings and buzzes through the bars more than the body; the green idles slow and lumpy with a big slow heave like a tractor; the orange hunts nervously and shakes harsh and hard. Where each quad is parked, where the jerry can has been left, and each quad's exact warm idle, roughness and starting fuel vary a little every game, picked from ranges in `src/vehicle/quadSpecs.js`:

| | `idleRange` (rpm) | `roughnessRange` | `fuelRange` (litres) | Body shake at idle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red | 1,050 to 1,150 | 0.04 to 0.12 | 2.5 to 11 of 12 | 2 mm, gentle |
| Blue | 1,400 to 1,550 | 0.20 to 0.30 | 1.2 to 8 of 11 | 3.5 mm, quick |
| Yellow | 1,500 to 1,800 | 0.35 to 0.50 | 1.5 to 8 of 9 | 3 mm, buzzy bars |
| Green | 800 to 900 | 0.55 to 0.70 | 3 to 13 of 14 | 12 mm, slow heave |
| Orange | 2,000 to 2,300 | 0.75 to 0.90 | 1.5 to 7.5 of 8 | 10 mm, harsh |

Running wears an engine and makes it rougher still, all the way to fully rough if you keep at it (about half an hour of hard riding). Past about 0.8 (its build roughness plus wear), it turns erratic: it stumbles through runs of misfires, losing nearly all its power for a moment and nearly dying, catches again sometimes with a bang and a puff of smoke, flares up at idle, and won't hold a steady tickover. Fully worn, that's a stumble every two or three seconds and it dies on you now and then, and the girls will tell you it needs looking at. A service from the toolbox brings it back to its own character.

**Mechanical sympathy.** Revving an engine hard before it has warmed up wears it several times faster, and the game keeps count of who was riding each quad while it wore: you, Amy or Jess. Amy tells Jess off when she's thrashing a cold engine, and tells you to go easy when you are (Jess cheers you on). Stood by a worn quad, a girl says who she reckons has been caning it, and she's right, because it comes from the real tally. A service wipes the slate.

The idle and roughness are printed in the console and shown in the tuning panel.

She starts beside a random quad in the yard. The others are parked in the yard at any angle, sometimes facing the other way, or (40% each) left somewhere else on the farm: by the field gate, the paddock, the straw bales, the west hedge, out past the far bend, at the bottom of the track or in the middle of the field. The console says where each one is. The browser console prints the layout's seed; add `?seed=<number>` to the URL to get the same layout again.

Everything below applies to any of them.

## Starting a cold engine

The engine starts stone cold and won't fire without choke.

1. Walk up to the quad and press `E` to get on.
2. Hold `Q` to pull the choke to 100%. The yellow lever on the left handlebar moves.
3. Turn the fuel tap ON with `F`. The quads are parked with it OFF; forget and it'll start, run for a few seconds on what's in the carburettor, then stumble and die. **Done.** When both are stood about, one dares the other to a race: they pick two quads, start them, line up at cones by the yard, count down and race one lap of the track. The winner crows, the loser has excuses, and the score is kept: the one well ahead goes on about it.
4. Press `K` for ignition. The green IGN light comes on and the blue TEMP light shows the engine is cold.
5. Hold `R` to crank. It should catch within a couple of seconds and settle into a high, lumpy idle. The whole bike shakes. **Done.** On a cold or wet day a girl stood about near a quad that's idling walks over and holds her hands over its engine; her friend often joins her, and they complain if it's switched off.
6. Let it warm. With the choke left on, the idle creeps up as it warms. Ease the choke off with `Z`, a notch or two at a time. Too early and it stumbles and dies. Too late and it starts to hunt, runs rich and eventually floods and stalls. **Done.** With a girl on the back and you riding along, she reaches round and opens the throttle for five to ten seconds over your hand. Jess keeps doing it; Amy does it once and apologises.
7. Once it's warm (TEMP light out, a couple of minutes) it idles low and steady with no choke. **Done.** Amy coughs and waves away the smoke from a running two-stroke when she's downwind of it, and hangs further back following one; Jess loves it and tucks in close.

Things to know:

- A warm engine with lots of choke is hard to start.
- Braking hard from speed while it's still cold can stall it. Roll off early and brake gently until it's warm.
- If you flood it, take the choke off and crank with the throttle held open (`W` + `R`) to clear it.
- The bike keeps idling when you get off, and you can watch and hear it shake from outside. With nobody on it, it also jolts over the ground as it moves, harder the faster it goes, so a quad driving itself round in circles visibly bounces. Riding it, the shake is as it always was.

## Mud

The track and especially its puddles are slippery: grip drops from 2.2 on grass to 0.7 on the track and 0.42 in a puddle (all scaled by tyre pressure). The yellow quad is rear-wheel drive and spins its back wheels if you give it everything in the mud; the red and blue have a switch on the bars (`G`) between 2WD and 4WD and start every game in 2WD: in 2WD they spin their back wheels in the mud like the yellow, and the bars are lighter and quicker. 4WD grips in the mud at the cost of a little steering weight and a few per cent of the drive. The blue's diff lock (`U`, which brings 4WD with it) pulls hardest in the mud and pushes wide on grass. The switch itself shows the mode (the rocker's position, and the blue's lock button sinks when it's in); the dash doesn't, but the analogue and digital pods have a 4WD lamp (and LOCK on the blue), and a quad stays as it was left. The girls sometimes remember to put it in 4WD when they take the wheel (Amy more than Jess); the ones who forget get bogged, work out why, flip it, and the other says so. The green and loan quads are always four-wheel drive. Tyres throw clumps and puddles splash, wheels leave ruts that fade over a couple of minutes, and the quads get muddier the more you ride, dark and wet at first and drying lighter. To wash one, pick up the hose on its reel at the south end of the petrol tank (`E`; it reaches 25 m), and hold `E` at a quad: the wheels come clean first, then the bodywork, over about 8 seconds for a filthy one, leaving the paint wet and shiny until it dries. `G` puts the hose back. Mind where you point it if she's about. Each quad's Mud folder in the tuning panel has the spray and rut settings, and "Mud on it" cleans it off.

The weather's different every game, but mostly sunny (about 70% of games, with sunny intervals another 15%); only now and then is it overcast, drizzly, raining or foggy (the console says which; `?weather=rain` and so on forces one, and the seed repeats it). Showers come and go during a game, more on a showery day: the sky darkens and the mist thickens as rain comes in, rain streaks past you with its own patter, and the wind pushes the exhaust smoke harder. Rain wets the ground, which darkens and dries slowly after: wet grass and especially wet mud are slipperier, and the bogs are worse. The girls have something to say about the day, and about the rain coming and going.

There are three bogs: two on the track and one in the low ground of the field, dark churned patches with a wet shine. Go into one slowly, or stop in it, and the mud drags you down and grips the wheels: keep the throttle on and they churn and dig you in, and once you're sunk past halfway it holds you against anything the tyres can do. Charge it with a run-up and you'll get through, much slower than you went in. Stuck but not too deep, rock it: forwards, backwards, forwards, and each change of direction works it a little looser. Too deep for that, use the green quad's winch: look at the green and press `X` to take the hook off it, walk the cable (30 m of it) to the stuck quad and press `E` on it to hook on, then hold `X` near the green (its remote is on a lead) or sitting on it to wind in. It pulls the stuck quad out towards the green. `E` on the hooked quad unhooks it, `X` while carrying the hook puts it back. The girls steer round the bogs on their own drives, but following you they'll go where you go; stuck, one gives up, gets off and shouts for help, and one on the back of yours will tell you what she thinks.

The ground changes as you play. Wherever quads keep driving over the field (you, and the girls on their usual routes), the grass wears: it darkens, goes soft like the track, and loses grip. Keep at it, with the ground wet, and a new bog forms there, the same dark glossy mud, deep enough to stick you. Rain swells the worn patches into bogs and a long dry spell shrinks them away; left alone the grass grows back in about ten minutes in the dry and takes far longer in the rain. The yard, the drag strip and the barn floor are hard and never wear. The girls plan their laps round new bogs as well as the old ones, so late in a long game the field isn't the one you started with.

## Tinkering

Pick up the red toolbox from the workbench in the barn (`E`), take it to a quad that's switched off and press `E` to get the spanners out (look at the workbench and press `E` to put it back). The panel has six adjustments (seven on the yellow and orange, which have a jet kit), each with its current and stock values and a note on the trade-off:

- **Idle screw**: the warm idle, within safe limits.
- **Carb mixture**: leaner pulls a bit harder and uses less fuel but runs rougher; richer is easier from cold but smokier and thirstier, and fully rich slowly fouls the plug.
- **Main jet** (yellow and orange only): the jet kit. A two-stroke wants a bigger jet in the cold and a smaller one in the heat; the stock jet is right at 12 °C and the panel tells you what the day's temperature wants. Jetted spot on it is the cleanest power the engine makes (about 6% more pull and a smoother tickover). A size or so out is merely a bit lean or rich, but jetted well lean for the day (a stock jet on a frosty morning, or a smaller one still) it runs hot, scuffs, nips up and seizes, slower than on straight petrol but the same way. Too rich is sluggish and smoky. Three sizes either way of stock. Amy says when you've gone too lean for the day, or got it right.
- **Throttle stop (speed limiter)**: farm quads come with it screwed in. Each turn out adds about 7% top speed (fully off is about +20%: the red goes from 35 to 42 mph), plus a few more revs and more fuel.
- **Rev limiter**: 5% a click, six clicks either way (about 30% up or down). Raise it and it revs on higher and louder flat out, with the dash redline moving too, but revs past what the engine was built for wear it faster (flat out, 10% over wears it about twice as fast, 30% over about four times, so it needs a service sooner); the top speed is the throttle stop's job. Each game a quad's full-throttle revs also start a few per cent either side of its usual (about 4%; `?seed=` repeats them), so the panel shows what this one came with.
- **Clutch springs**: softer bites sooner, stiffer later; it always bites above the idle.
- **Tyre pressures**: softer grips better (especially in mud) but bounces more.

**Silencer off** (`X`) is a separate job: the quad gets a lot louder and a more open, rasping tone, about 8% more power, and it crackles and bangs far more when you shut the throttle at speed. `X` again puts it back. Jess loves it; Amy covers her ears and tells you to turn it off.

**The tractor and the UTV** open the same panel with the toolbox, but it only shows what a diesel has: the idle screw, the clutch springs and the tyre pressures (no carb mixture, throttle stop or rev limiter), a service that also changes the oil, the air filter, the throttle cable, the tyres and, on the UTV, the drive belt. There's no carb, silencer or pods. Petrol in either one is pumped out from the panel. The tractor's and UTV's `Y` dipstick works as on the quads.

**Pods** (`Y`, `U`, `O`): at a switched-off quad the panel says which pod is on it and which are in the toolbox. `Y` fits the analogue pod and `U` the digital one (each takes about three seconds, and the quad must have none already); `O` takes the one that's there off, back into the toolbox. To move a pod you take it off one quad and fit it to another.

**Air filter.** Every quad has one, and it slowly clogs with mud and puddle splashes while you drive (about seven minutes of hard mud to choke it; splashing through puddles is a bit worse; stood still or on clean ground, not at all). A clogged filter makes it run rich, so it smokes darker and fouls, and it loses power at the top end (up to 30% at full revs; hardly anything at idle). At a switched-off quad with the toolbox, **hold `I`** (or the button) for three seconds to clean it out; let go and it starts again. The panel says how clean it is. In `src/vehicle/airFilter.js`; the numbers are `clogRich` and `clogPowerLoss` in each quad's engine folder.

"Back to stock" puts everything back exactly, silencer included. Engines also wear as they run (faster when worked hard), getting rougher: a lumpy, hunting idle and a lot more shake. The panel says how it's running, and **Service it** (`V`) clears the wear and leaves it as sweet as that quad gets. Changes last for the game. `G` puts the toolbox down; you can't ride while carrying it. She has opinions about all of this.

**Faults differ per quad.** Each game every quad is dealt its own luck: for each of the four faults (puncture, belt, cable, carb), how often it starts and how fast it gets worse, each from 0.4 to 2.5 times the usual rate, picked at random (from the layout seed, so `?seed=` repeats it). So one quad might be belt-prone and slow to puncture, another the reverse. The tuning panel shows the quad you're looking at, under "Faults (live)", and `proneSpread` sets the range.

## Fuel

Each quad has a fuel tank and a fuel tap, and a fuel gauge on the loan quad's and the blue's LCD or on a pod. Taps start OFF. How much is in each tank varies every game (see `fuelRange` above); the blue can start below its reserve, so it'll only run on RESERVE until it's filled.

- A full tank lasts roughly 25 minutes of hard riding, and much longer at idle.
- The tap has three positions. On **ON** it can't reach the last couple of litres, so when it starts to cough and stumble, flick it to **RESERVE** with `F` and head for fuel. **OFF** shuts the fuel off; the engine runs on what's in the carburettor for a moment, then dies.
- If it runs completely dry, it stalls and won't restart until it has fuel.
- To refuel: find the red jerry can (it's left somewhere around the yard, the paddock or the gate) and pick it up with `E` (it holds 20 litres). Look at a quad and hold `E` to pour, with a glug. Refill the can by holding `E` at the green petrol tank. `G` puts the can down. You can't ride holding the can, so put it down first.

**Two-stroke oil.** The yellow and orange run on pre-mix, about 50:1 oil in the petrol. By the petrol tank there's a second, yellow jerry can of pre-mix and a bottle of two-stroke oil: hold `E` on the bottle with a can in your hands to add oil to it. Refilling any can at the tank adds plain petrol, so it thins the mix, and the prompts tell you what's in the can you're holding. Nothing tells you what's in a quad's tank except how it behaves. Straight petrol in a two-stroke and it runs hot (the temperature needle climbs into the red), then it starts to lose power and run raggedly (nipping up), and in the end it seizes solid and won't turn over, not even on the pull cord. Flat out takes a couple of minutes; idling takes far longer. Take the toolbox to it and press `F` ("Strip it down") to free it, which leaves it worn until you service it. Too much oil, or any pre-mix in the red, blue or green, smokes like a bonfire and slowly fouls the plug. Amy says so if you pour the wrong can nearby, and reads what's gone wrong when it goes; Jess blames the quad.

**Engine oil on the four-strokes.** The red, blue, green and loan quads, the tractor and the UTV each have oil in the sump. Stood looking at one, press `Y` to pull the dipstick: a toast says whether it's up to the top mark, between the marks, on the bottom one or below it (it reads to within a hair either way). To top it up, park it near the oil bottle by the petrol tank and hold `Y`. The bench has the same job (`5`, petrol four-strokes only). Each game one of them burns oil: it starts with a low sump, loses it far faster than the rest, and puffs smoke on the overrun with the throttle shut. Run a quad low and the tappets tick, then the engine gets rough and starts to knock and lose power, and run dry it seizes just as a two-stroke does on straight petrol (a strip down at the toolbox frees it, but top the oil up first or it goes again). The red's OIL lamp (it was a temperature warning, now an oil pressure one) and an OIL lamp on either accessory pod light with the ignition on and the engine not running, then only when the sump is low while it's running, flickering at idle, so fitting a pod to another quad shows it too. Amy checks the dipstick before she rides and tops it up if it's low; Jess never has, so it's usually Jess's quad that starts ticking. The tractor and the UTV have a sump too, and one of the five four-strokes (diesels included) is the one that burns oil; the two-strokes aren't affected.

**The barn.** The big doors are always open, and you can walk or ride straight in. Inside there's a concrete floor, two straw bales, a workbench along the north wall with the toolbox and a pegboard of tools, and a bare bulb that comes on in the gloom. Under the roof no rain falls, the light is dimmer, and rain on the tin drums loudly (you can hear a little of it from the doorway); an engine inside booms and echoes, and so does anything you hear from in there. A quad parked inside takes no mud, and a warm engine switched off in there cools two and a half times slower and never goes fully stone cold, so it starts better the next morning than one left in the yard. The tuning panel has a "Barn" folder for the sound, and the engine's "barn" settings for the warmth.

**Faults that develop.** Quads go wrong over time, separately from wear, and nothing tells you: you feel and hear it. A slow puncture starts while a quad's being ridden and gets worse over a couple of minutes: the bars pull towards the soft tyre (less for a rear one) and it drags a little. A slipping drive belt comes from riding hard: the revs flare as soon as you open the throttle and it hardly goes. A sticking throttle cable gets worse in stages: first the throttle lingers for a moment after you let go, then for longer and longer, and after about seven minutes' running it sticks open for good, staying wherever you last opened it (you can still open it further) until you switch off. A girl driving a quad that has stuck switches it off herself. A dirty carb makes it hunt, run rough and lose power, worse and worse the longer it runs. They happen to any quad the girls ride too, about one every ten to twenty minutes of riding across the lot. At the toolbox panel, `T` pumps and patches the tyres, `R` swaps the belt, `L` oils the cable and `C` cleans the carb; each does its job if there is one and otherwise just says everything's fine, so it doesn't give a fault away. Amy notices sooner and says what it is; Jess notices late and blames the quad.

**The hayloft.** Just inside the barn doors a ladder leads up to a plank loft over the front of the barn, above the doorway (quads drive in underneath). Look at the ladder and press `E` and you climb it (about 2.5 seconds); do the same at the top to come down. Up there you can walk right up to the hay door in the end wall, a window at head height looking out over the yard, hay is stacked down both sides where the roof comes low, and there's a stack of hay: look at it and press `E` to duck into it and hide. While you're hidden the girls can't find you, so they stop offering rides and asking favours, and call for you now and then ("Where have you gone?") until one finds you and you come up (or press any key to come out yourself). The loft's open edge has a rail, with a gap at the ladder. On a fine day a bored girl might climb up and sit on the hay bale at the edge; she finds you at once if you're hiding while she's up there, and Jess, up there while you stand below near her, now and then tips straw on your head (Amy tells her off). The tuning panel has a "Hayloft" folder.

**The workbench.** The toolbox lives on the bench in the barn (if it's not there, the bench won't work). Drive a quad into the yellow-painted bay in front of it, switch it off, look at the bench and press `E`. `1` to `8` pick a job: change the spark plug, clean the carb, fix a tyre, top up the two-stroke oil (yellow and orange only), top up the engine oil (the petrol four-strokes), repack the silencer (two-strokes), top up the coolant (the orange) or fit the big-bore kit (the yellow). Each job is a short list of steps: `E` taps a step, and on a hold step you keep `E` down until it's done. Some holds have a green band on the meter: let go inside it. Too soon and it's loose, too long and it's over-done, and either leaves the job botched, so it looks done but the fault comes back a little, the plug is still fouled, or the oil mix is off. The plug job lets you look at the old plug, which tells you how the engine's been running. `G` steps away. The toolbox's `T`, `R`, `L` and `C` in the yard are now rough fixes that leave a mild fault behind; the bench gets rid of it. The girls watch from wherever they are: Amy helps and tells you when you've slipped, Jess heckles.

**The rusty quad.** In about one game in five (from the seed, so `?seed=` repeats it; try 7, 8, 12 or 13) an old brown quad stands under a tarp at the back of the barn: a copy of one of the regular quads (which one also comes from the seed), caked in dust and rust and not run for years. It's the "rusty quad" everywhere it shows up, and it comes in addition to whatever is in the yard. It can't be driven, so the bench works on it where it stands (a quad parked in the bay comes first). The list starts with the tarp, which you take off first, and then the three or four jobs this one needs, picked from the seed: drain the varnished fuel (which empties the tank, so you then have to fill it, with pre-mix for a two-stroke), clean the carb, a new plug, free the seized brake, mend the tyres, and charge the battery. The charger is clipped on at the bench and then takes about five minutes, so get on with something else meanwhile (the bench shows how far it's got). It won't catch, however you crank it, until every job it needs is done; cranking it earlier tells you what is still wrong. Then the first start: a burst of smoke, a toast, and both girls cheering (Amy will switch it off soon after, since it is full of fumes in the barn). Amy writes the jobs in the logbook. The girls leave it alone, and it isn't in the wheel's quad lists.

**Amy won't ride it.** Once Amy knows a quad has a fault, she won't ride it until it's put right: "Not on that, it's got a soft tyre." She knows from her own walk-round (a slow puncture, soft tyre, slipping belt, sticking throttle or dirty carb), and from what she had written in the logbook before the game began. If the walk-round turns something up as she sets off, she says so, pulls up and gets off (unless a race, follow the leader or a recall is on). After that she leaves that quad out of her own drives, the races, follow the leader and the bored games, and it drops off the wheel's "drive" choices for her. Pump the tyre or fix the fault, in the yard or at the bench, and she forgets it and will ride it again. Jess rides it anyway. In `src/npc/wontRide.js`; "Amy won't ride" in the tuning panel (`amyWontRide`, 0 switches it off).

**Amy's logbook.** A red notebook lies on the workbench. Look at it and press `E` to read it (`E` or `G` puts it down). Amy writes in it what she finds and what's done to the quads, with the time into the game: "3:10  Blue, belt slipping." It gets her walk-round findings (slow puncture, soft tyre, slipping belt, sticking throttle, dirty carb), her oil top-ups, and, if she's within 15 m, the plug you pulled at the bench ("Green, plug black (you)") and the bench jobs you finish ("Yellow, silencer repacked (you)", with ", not well" if you botched it). She writes what she really saw, so unlike Jess it's true, and it's a way to read the hidden state from inside the game. She won't write the same thing about the same quad twice within two minutes, and the page holds the last 40 entries. The first time she writes something a toast says so. The book isn't empty at the start: from the seed it already has two to five entries from "Yesterday", picked from what is really true of the quads (a fault already showing, tinkering left in, a quirk, who had one out last), though she's missed some of it and never writes what isn't so.

**The yellow's big-bore kit.** Jess's dream. The yellow quad has a big-bore kit that fits at the workbench (`8`): undo the head, swap the barrel and piston, ease the new barrel over the rings, then do the head nuts up evenly (snug, not tight). Fitted well it pulls about 18% harder, but it makes more heat working, wears the engine about two and a half times as fast (the new piston and rings do take some of the old wear away), and wants a jet about half a size richer than the weather alone, which the toolbox's jet row now takes into account. A badly nipped-up head gives you a bit over half of the power and all of the wear. Once it's fitted it stays fitted for the rest of the game. Open the bench with the kit still to fit and one of the girls mentions the catalogue Jess has left open on it (Amy disapproves, Jess won't let it go); when it's done, Jess can't believe it and Amy warns you about the heat. Amy writes "big-bore kit fitted" in the logbook.

**The orange's coolant.** In about half of games (picked from the seed, so `?seed=` repeats it; the console says) a hose on the orange is weeping coolant. It leaks while the engine runs (about 50 minutes from full to dry at the usual rate, and it starts the game anywhere from just over half to nearly full) and a little while it sits hot. Once it's down a bit a green damp patch grows on the ground under it, which Amy reads and Jess pulls a face at. As the level falls the radiator does less: the orange heats like the air-cooled quads, the fan helps less and the temperature needle shows all of it. Low and hot, it steams from the radiator, and in the end it boils over: a big burst of steam, most of what's left gone, and any girl close by jumps back and shouts. At the workbench (`7`) the job lets the radiator cool, nips up the weeping hose clip (snug, not tight, or it keeps weeping a bit), and pours coolant to the line. The yard toolbox can't do it.

**Spitting back.** A cold engine running lean (not enough choke) coughs back through the carb when you snap the throttle open: a soft whuff from the airbox and a puff of smoke from under the seat, quite unlike the sharp crack of a pop in the exhaust. A girl sitting on it jumps and yelps; Amy says what it is and to use the choke, Jess finds it funny. Warm it up, or keep some choke on, and it stops.

**Running in.** An engine that has just been rebuilt wants about ten minutes of gentle running before it's thrashed: one stripped down after a seizure, the barn find on its first start in years, and the yellow with the big-bore kit. Hold it under about 60% of its revs and it's fine (idling counts for half). Thrash it in that time and it wears up to six times faster and keeps some roughness for good, which a service doesn't clear. The mechanical sympathy tally above records who did it. Amy tells you or Jess off for caning a fresh engine; Jess cheers you on.

**Heat.** The air-cooled quads (red, blue, yellow, green) get too hot idling for ages on a sunny day, or crawling through mud with the throttle open: after about four minutes in full sun the needle climbs into the red and the engine starts pinking (a metallic rattle), runs rough and loses power. Riding along at a decent speed blows it away, as does a dull or wet day. The water-cooled orange has a radiator fan that cuts in and out with its own whirr, and never gets that hot, unless it's leaking coolant (below). Switch off a quad you've thrashed and it ticks as the metal cools, slower and slower. It also starts differently while it's hot: the carb boils its fuel, so it starts rich, a choke makes it worse, and holding the throttle open while you crank helps. Amy tells you when it's pinking and when it's ticking; Jess is cheerful about both.

**Popping and running on.** Shut the throttle at speed and an engine that's lean, worn or running hot crackles and bangs in the pipe: the two-strokes (yellow and orange) a lot, a healthy four-stroke hardly ever, and more as it wears. Under heavy cloud or rain you can see a small orange flash at the tail pipe with each bang; in sun you can't. Switch off an engine that's hot, worn or lean and it may chug on unevenly for a few more beats before it dies; switch the key back on and it just carries on.

**The mini moto.** In about half of games a 50cc pit bike is parked with the quads, a sun-faded blue one: two wheels, one seat, a thumb choke, a fuel tap on the left of the tank and a pull-start (`P`) as well as the starter. It's a two-stroke on pre-mix, so it has no reverse (get off and push) and the same hold-and-yank cord as the yellow. It tops out at about 12 m/s and leans into corners; you or either girl can ride it, but nobody rides on the back. It sounds like a Derbi GPR 50 with a race kit: a lumpy idle that bangs every other turn, cleaning up into a raspy buzz as it revs. It's built in `src/vehicle/miniMotoModel.js`.

**The old three-wheeler.** Now and then (about one game in three) the neighbour's old ATC is parked with the quads: a chalky sun-bleached red three-wheeler with one front wheel, two fat tyres at the back, a taped seat and a rusty silencer. It's a worn-out two-stroke with a pull-start only (no starter, `P`), a clutch (Shift) and a four-speed box, a pull-knob choke and a lumpy, smoky idle. It's solo, tops out at about 16 m/s and has no reverse (get off and push). It also **tips**: its single front wheel and tall seat mean a hard turn at speed, over about 12 m/s with the lock on, will roll it, while 9 m/s is fine. The girls will drive it. It idles with the slow, lumpy thump of a Piaggio Ape and revs like an old two-stroke trail bike. It's built in `src/vehicle/atcModel.js`.

## The UTV

A green Gator-style side-by-side stands by the paddock, always, on red diesel. Look at it and press `E` to ride it from the right-hand seat, under its roll cage.

- **Starting.** Its engine is a small three-cylinder diesel, recorded on a real Gator: quicker and lighter than the tractor's. It is a diesel with glow plugs, like the tractor: `K` for the key, wait for the GLOW lamp on the dash to go out, then hold `R`. Unlike the tractor the key stops it. The dash has a speedo (with an hour meter), fuel and water dials and lamps for glow, charge, oil and R / N / D.
- **Driving.** An automatic: `W` is the throttle, `S` the brake, `A` `D` steer with a proper steering wheel that turns a lot further than the wheels do. It tops out at 25 mph (11 m/s). Reverse is a lever on the dash: stopped, press `B`, then `S` backs it up. It's heavy next to a quad, so it brakes and corners like it.
- **Fuel.** Red diesel from the bowser and the dark green can. Petrol does to it what diesel does to a quad: a tenth in the tank makes it run rough, a fifth stalls it for good. Amy and Jess both shout when you pour petrol in. To put it right, pick up the toolbox, look at the UTV and press `E` to pump the tank out, then fill it again with red diesel.
- **The bed.** `T` tips the bed on its ram, only when you've stopped, from the seat or standing beside it. The tailgate drops as it goes up, and the ram lets it down by itself if you drive off with it raised.
- **The girls.** Stop with the engine running and a girl stood about may walk over and climb in, or both come. They squabble over the middle of the bench: Jess usually wins, and the loser sits at the window, or sulks off to ride in the bed. The bed is against the rules: she sits on the open tailgate with her legs hanging, the other girl tells her off, and she complains when you go fast and over the bumps. Tip the bed with her on it and she slides off the back into the mud. You can also ask either of them from her wheel ("UTV": get in, ride in the bed, get out), though Amy won't ride in the bed. They don't drive it yet.

## The little tipping trailer

A small steel tipping trailer stands by the paddock. Back the tractor, or any quad with a rack (the red, blue and green), up to its ring and press `J` to hook it on; `J` again, stopped, takes it off. It's a drag on the quad: less pull and weaker brakes, more so with a girl and things in it (the tractor hardly notices). With a jerry can, the toolbox or the jump leads in your hands, `E` at the tub stands it inside, in a row across the front well clear of the sides, and you pick it up again as usual. `T` tips the tub when you're stopped, from the seat or standing beside it, and everything in it slides out behind. Stop with it hitched and Jess may climb in against the rules (Amy hardly ever): the other girl tells her off, she shrieks at the bumps and complains when you go fast, and tipping the tub with her in it puts her in the mud. Ask either girl in or out from her wheel ("Trailer"). Bales will come when they're items you can pick up.

## The dashes

Each quad's own dash is close to the real machine it's modelled on, which for most of them means very little:

- **Red** (after a Honda Foreman): a rectangular 0 to 45 mph speedo with an hour meter and an odometer, three lamps (R, N, OIL °C) and the key. No rev counter, fuel or temperature gauge.
- **Blue** (after a Yamaha Grizzly): a grey-green LCD in a charcoal pod: fuel and temperature bars, a gear letter (D, R or N), big speed digits, a clock and a bottom line that shows trip, odometer and hours four seconds each. N, R, TEMP and FUEL lamps either side. No rev counter.
- **Loan quad** (after a newer Yamaha Kodiak): the same idea, wider and glossier, with a crisper LCD, a gear column and a fuel sweep.
- **Green** (after a Kawasaki Bayou): a small cover on top of the headlight nacelle with N and R lamps and the key. Nothing else.
- **Yellow** (after a Yamaha Blaster): the back of a headlamp shell on the bars with one red lamp that lights with the ignition, and the key.
- **Orange** (after a Suzuki LT250R): bare race bars and a RUN / OFF kill switch with its lanyard on the left bar.
- **Kids' quad**, **mini moto** and **old three-wheeler**: a key switch and nothing else.

To make up for it there are **two accessory pods**, and there's only one of each: an older **analogue** pod (a cream speedo and rev counter with drum counters, small fuel and temperature dials, four lamps) and a new **digital** one (amber on black: a bar-graph rev counter, speed, fuel and temperature blocks, gear letter, clock and a line cycling odometer, trip and hours). Each shows everything for whichever quad it's fitted to, with its rev counter's red band starting at that quad's own redline. Each game they start in the toolbox or on a random quad (the console says where), and you move them with the toolbox (see above). The K key still turns the key on every quad; the dashes go dark with the ignition off or the battery flat.

Every quad starts each game with some hours and miles on it (the Blaster and the loan quad low, the green's well into four figures), and they add up as you run and ride.

The fuel tap's position isn't on any dash now: look at the tap itself.

## Riding with her

There are two of them, each starting beside her own quad in the yard. **Amy** (blonde, navy polo) is the one you know. **Jess** (black hair, burgundy polo) is cheekier: she winds you up, is braver behind the bars, gets bored sooner and is more likely to take a quad off on her own, and she usually enjoys being left on a runaway. Each has her own voice and lines. They don't interact with each other, except that each leaves alone a quad the other is busy with, and one waits for the other to finish before chattering. Their names, looks and personalities are in `src/npc/characters.js` (Jess's lines in `src/npc/jessLines.js`). They're both after you, and it shows: they bicker. Standing about together near you, they have a go at each other now and then; talk to one with the other close by and the other chips in; ride past one with the other on your back and they shout at each other; come back from a ride and the one left behind has something to say; one watches the other drive and heckles; one gets stuck and the other enjoys it; and sharing a quad, they squabble about the driving. Amy is sweet with an edge, Jess is cheeky and competitive. Their exchanges are in `src/npc/banterLines.js`. They can share a quad, one driving and one on the back: ask one to get on the back of the quad the other is driving (she pulls up to let her on), or to drive the quad the other is sitting on the back of, and now and then a bored one hops on the back of her friend's quad as it sets off. With her friend aboard the driver goes round bigger loops for a minute and a half to over three minutes, then both get off. With both seats taken there's no room for you. A third place is the front rack, on the red, blue and green: with you on the back and one girl driving, ask the other from her wheel to "Sit on the front" of that quad (or now and then a bored one hops on as it sets off). You can also ask either girl to "Sit on the front" of any of those three while it's parked with nobody on it, then climb on and ride off with her up there; she stays on until you ask her to get off. She sits side by side with nothing to hold, legs hanging over the nose, the driver pulls up for her, and the extra weight takes a little steering lock off. When the driver's done and stops, she gets off at the front. Everything below applies to both.

Look at one of them and press `E` to open her wheel. Move the mouse towards a slice and click (or press its number). The first ring has a slice for each quad she could do something with, in its colour, plus anything that isn't about a particular quad (answering her when she's asked to get off, getting off the one she's on, or stopping while she's driving); a quad's slice opens a second ring of what she could do with it. Right-click (or Backspace) goes back a ring; `E` closes it. Riding on the back while she drives, `E` opens a one-ring wheel for the ride. The options:

- **Get on the red / blue / yellow quad**: she walks over and sits on the pillion seat. Get on and ride; she stays on. Asking her onto the other quad while she's sitting on one, she gets off and walks over.
- **Start the red / blue / yellow quad**: she walks to its handlebars, turns the key, checks the fuel tap (usually), puts the choke on if it's cold (usually), and cranks in short goes, talking herself through it. If it catches she eases the choke off as it warms, then leaves it to you; after four goes she gives up and switches it off. Get on while she's at it and she stops.
- **Take me for a ride on the red / blue quad**: she gets on the front, starts it and waits for you to get on the back (look at the quad and press `E`). She drives bigger loops and keeps going while you're aboard. Press `E` on the back to talk to her: stop here, slow down, go a bit faster, let me off, or let me drive (she gets off, you slide forward, and she hops on the back). You can also get on the back whenever she's driving and going slowly.
- **Sitting on the back by yourself**: look at a parked quad and press `B`. If nobody's driving it, after 6 to 25 seconds a free girl (usually the nearer) walks over, says so, gets on the front, starts it and drives you about in big loops. Unlike a ride you asked for, this is her idea and it lasts as long as one of her drives (a minute to two and a half): she gets bored, pulls up and gets off without waiting for you to say stop, leaving you on the back. Nobody offers again for a while: 45 seconds to 2 minutes after Amy's ride, 25 to 70 seconds after Jess's (hers also last a bit less, 45 to 110 seconds). If you get off before she's got on, she gives up after 25 seconds. You can still talk to her with `E` and ask her to stop or let you off. On the back by yourself, `E` gets you off.
- **Follow me on the red / blue / yellow quad**: she gets on that one, starts it and waits for you to set off on another, then follows about 8 m behind at your pace, up to about 25 mph (less if she's feeling careful, more if confident). Get too far ahead and she shouts after you; stop and she pulls up behind you and waits. Get off your quad and she waits a while (30 to 90 seconds; Jess less), then gives up on you: either she goes off for a drive on her own (more likely the more confident she is) or she pulls up and gets off or moves to the back, as after any drive. Talk to her to finish sooner.
- **Drive the red / blue / yellow quad**: she gets on the front, starts it like the learner she is, and potters round a loop near where she started for about a minute. How she drives depends on how she feels that day: careful (creeping, shaky, lots of stops, quick to get scared) or confident, and the more confident the bolder: at the very top she'll do up to about 28 mph in big loops out across the field for three minutes or more, picking her way slowly out of the yard and among parked quads and only opening it up with room around her. A fright makes her more careful next time while a good go builds her confidence. She then stops, bored or scared, and gets off or hops on the back. While she drives you can ask her to stop and get off or move onto the back. Get on and she shuffles back; if her quad is too fast to climb onto, press `E` on it to ask her to stop and let you on.
- **Stand next to the red / blue quad**: she gets off if she's on, or walks over if she isn't, and stands beside it facing it.

The wheel only offers what makes sense: no "get on" while she's already on, no "stand next to it" while she's already there. She stays put on the pillion when you get off until you ask her to get down. Jump off at speed with her on the back and she'll let you know what she thinks.

**Teaching them.** Each girl is better or worse at four things: starting, the choke, cornering and mud. Amy corners better, Jess is better in mud, and both start fairly unsure about the choke. Where she's weak she makes the mistake more often (forgets the tap or choke, goes into corners too hot and yanks the bars, gives up in a bog sooner) and says so herself ("I always forget the choke"). Riding on the back while she drives, `E` has a "Coach her" slice whenever there's something to coach: "Choke off now" while the engine's warm and the choke's still on, "Short bursts on the starter" while she's cranking or after a stall, "Ease off into the corners" in a turn or after a yank, "Keep it going through the mud" when she's stuck or sinking. Advice at the right moment teaches her that skill a good step and pleases her a little; the same advice again within 25 seconds is nagging, which teaches nothing and annoys her; advice at the wrong moment puzzles her. She also improves very slowly on her own by getting it right. As she improves her lines change from "I'm no good at it" to "I think I'm getting it" to "I've got this", and she starts doing it before you can say. Skills last for the game only. The tuning panel has "Skills (live)" with each level and a "Skills" folder with the numbers.

**They coach each other.** When the girls share a quad, the one on the back coaches the one driving, the same way you can: "It's warm now. Choke off." Amy coaches the choke, the starter and corners, patiently and at the right moment. Jess coaches mud, loudly, and also pipes up when nothing's wrong and says it again straight after, which counts as nagging. It teaches by your rules, so the right moment helps and the wrong one doesn't, and their skills drift without you being involved.

**Favours.** After the first few minutes, a girl who's stood about near you (you on foot) now and then asks for something, with a line to say so: "Could you give my quad a wash?" (when her favourite quad is properly muddy), "Take me over to the straw bales" (she has to be on the back of your quad when you stop near them: ask her onto the back from her wheel, and the bales are the straw ones out in the field) or "Fetch the jerry can" (bring it to within a few metres of her, in your hands or put down). Each girl has at most one request open and asks no more than every 4 minutes or so. Nothing on the screen tells you; if you forget, talk to her and she reminds you. Do it and she thanks you and likes you noticeably more (it counts like a lot of rides towards who you favour), and the favoured one may crow about it if her friend's close. If you leave it six minutes it lapses: she likes you a bit less, remembers for ten minutes, says so the next time you talk to her, and is more likely to say no if you ask her to do something with a quad. Doing a later favour for her clears it. The tuning panel has a "Favours asked" folder.

**Places to be.** On a sunny or sunny-intervals day with the ground dry, a bored girl sometimes decides to go and sit somewhere instead of waiting at a quad: on top of one of the straw bales out in the field, or on the paddock fence. She says so on the way, sits for one to three minutes, now and then says something about it, and gets down by herself. When the rain gets going (a few seconds to about 20 after it sets in), any girl who's about, even one sat on a bale, runs for the barn and sits on one of the bales inside, looking out at the yard; once it's been dry for 15 to 45 seconds she comes out again. Two girls don't take the same place. Talk to her and she gets down first ("Hang on") and then the wheel opens; one sat out can still be asked to drive you about, or offer you a ride when you sit on the back of a quad. The tuning panel has a "Places" folder.

**Batteries.** Every quad has a battery you can't see a gauge for. Leaving the ignition on with the engine off drains it (flat in about eight minutes), cranking drains it faster, and running charges it (flat to full in about ten minutes). Below about 60% the starter turns more slowly and a start is less likely to catch; below 25% it only clicks, once per press; below 10% the dash goes dark. The girls notice and say so ("it's turning over really slowly", "the battery's flat"), and so do they when you're the one struggling near them. To get going again:

- **Pull-start (`P`, yellow and orange only).** Look at the quad (or sit on it), have the ignition on, hold `P` to draw the cord and let go to yank. Holding for about half a second to a second is a proper pull; too short is weak, too long is a botched yank. A pull turns it over for about a second and a bit, works with no battery at all, and needs the choke and a cold engine's usual coaxing. Jess, faced with a flat one, will have a go at the cord herself (Amy gives up and hands it to you).
- **Knacks.** The green used to be stubborn when cold (blip the throttle twice, then crank) but the girls couldn't get it going, so it now starts like the others (`knackBlips` in its spec brings it back). The blue floods if you leave the choke on once it's warming, so ease it off early. The girls know and will tell you.
- **Jump leads.** Pick them up from the ground by the hose (`E`), look at the flat quad and press `E` to clamp the first end, then at a quad with its engine running and `E` again. The running quad charges the flat one, about 6% a second, while they're joined; the leads only stretch about 4.5 metres, so park them nose to nose, and they come off if they're pulled too far. `E` on a clamped quad takes them off; `G` puts them down.

**Nicknames.** Early in a game, while she's free and you're near, Jess gives the quads nicknames, one every forty seconds or so ("Right. The green quad is Grandad from now on."). Each quad's name is picked from a short list per game, so the green might be Grandad or Kermit, the yellow the wasp or Custard. Amy says she won't use them. From then on the names turn up in what the girls say about a quad (whose it is, and which one they can hear). Amy holds out for a few mentions, then slips and uses one, and Jess pounces on it. Names are in `src/npc/quadNames.js`; the tuning panel has a "Quad names" folder.

**My quad.** Each girl claims one quad for the game (never the same one; it's also the one she likes best), and once you're on foot near her and it, she tells you so. If somebody else rides it (you, or her friend) she objects, with a different line for each: Amy tells off Jess, Jess says you owe her. Now and then, picking a quad for her own drive, Jess takes Amy's on purpose, says so, and teases her when she complains. A girl goes for her own quad first when she wants a drive. In `src/npc/ownership.js`; the "Whose quad" folder.

**Who's that.** A girl stood about who hears her friend on a running quad she can't see (between about 28 and 130 metres off) says which one it is and how it sounds: flat out, rough (cold or worn) or steady ("That's Jess on the blue. She never lets it idle."). In `src/npc/earing.js`; the "Hearing a quad" folder.

**Warming their hands.** On a cold or wet day (overcast, drizzle, rain or fog), a girl stood about near a quad that's running with nobody on it walks over and holds her hands out over its engine for half a minute to over a minute. Leave one idling and the other often joins her, one each side. Switch it off while they're at it and they complain. In `src/npc/warming.js`; the "Warming hands" folder.

**Hand on the throttle.** Riding along with a girl on the back, now and then she reaches her right arm round you to your throttle grip and gives it a handful for five to ten seconds (up to 90% throttle, though your own input still counts if it's higher). Jess does it again and again, at least 40 seconds apart, and gloats. Amy does it once in a game, then apologises. Not when you're stood still or already doing 25 mph or more. In `src/npc/throttleGrab.js`; the "Hand on the throttle" folder.

**Two-stroke smoke.** Amy hates it: stood downwind of a running yellow or orange (or just behind it in still air) she coughs, waves a hand in front of her face and complains. Jess loves it and says so. Following you on a two-stroke, Amy hangs nearly twice as far back as usual; Jess tucks in close behind it. In `src/npc/smokeSense.js`; the "Two-stroke smoke" folder, and `followSmokeGap` in the Companion folder.

**Racing.** Two minutes into a game or later, when both girls have been stood about for twenty seconds or so, one dares the other to a race (Jess usually), they pick two different quads (each her own claimed one if it's free), start them and drive to the start line by the yard, marked with orange cones either side of the track (no collision: you can drive straight through). Once both are on the line there's a count-down, then they race one lap of the dirt track, flat out for them, side by side on the grid and keeping well clear of the puddles (which stall a quad at speed) and the bogs. First over the line wins and says so; the other has an excuse. Afterwards they bicker about it, and the wins and losses are kept for the game: once one is two or more wins ahead they go on about the score. If a quad won't start or dies in the race, that girl is out and loses; if neither makes the start line within two and a half minutes, it's called off. A lap takes about a minute and a half. They stage a little behind the line and come up to it along the track, so they end up facing the right way (one that arrives crooked goes round again).

**Racing them.** You can be the third on the grid. Ride a quad up to the start line and sit still with it running and Jess may dare you; press `Enter` there to ask for one yourself; or, if they start a race while you are on a quad near the line, they invite you in. Line up in the middle lane, stopped and pointing along the track: when all three are on the line the count-down starts. Take too long (half a minute) or get off and they go without you. Your lap is judged by passing through each quarter of it in order and getting back to the line, so cutting across the field doesn't count; stalling, or being off the quad, for 15 seconds puts you out. Win and they have excuses; lose and they gloat. Your wins and losses are kept alongside theirs, though the score they go on about is still the two of them. Not while you ride either of the quads they picked: they pick two others. In `src/npc/race.js`; the "Racing" folder. To see one without waiting, open the tuning panel (`` ` ``) and press "Start a race now" in "Girls (live)", which also shows whose quad is whose, the nicknames, the race score and buttons to make a girl grab the throttle or Jess name the quads.

**Reading the dash.** With the girl on the back and you riding, she sometimes reads the dash out. Amy does it in good time: "Your fuel is on the red" when the fuel light comes on, "The temperature light is on" when it's too hot, and "It is still cold, go easy" if you're going hard on a cold engine. Jess does it when it's too late: when the tank's nearly empty, after it's been hot for a while, or after you've thrashed it cold and it has warmed up. Each only once per spell, and one reading every 45 seconds at most. In `src/npc/dashReading.js`; the "Reading the dash" folder.

**Shouting a fault.** Ride off and a girl behind you, or alongside, sees what you can't from the saddle and shouts it: blue or black smoke from under you, steam off the radiator, a soft tyre, a flat one wobbling, a silencer coming apart. Amy shouts sooner and plainly; Jess a beat later, as a joke. In `src/npc/shoutFault.js`; the "Shouting a fault" folder in the tuning panel.

**Impatient follower.** Jess following you (see "Follow me") who's stuck behind a slow pace (under about 13 mph) for twenty seconds or more may get fed up, go round you on one side, pull up well ahead and wait, looking pleased with herself ("Took your time!"). When you've gone by she tails you again. Amy doesn't (`overtakeChance` in the Companion folder is 0 for her).

**Drag racing.** A rough gravel straight in the middle of the field, inside the track and clear of the mud, marked only by cones at the start and the finish (the ground under it is levelled, about 56 m from line to line). Two ways in. Ride up to the start and press `Enter`: Jess or Amy takes the other lane, and you line up on yours, stopped and pointing along the strip. Or sit still on a quad with the engine running for ten seconds or more and Jess may ride over, pull up beside you and blip her throttle: blip back (snap yours open from closed) and she leads you to the strip; ignore her and she calls you chicken. Both lined up, there is a count-down; roll over the line early and it's a false start (you line up again, and the third one calls it off). First over the finish wins, with the wins and losses kept alongside the lap races. After either kind of race, a girl sometimes pulls up and gets off and sometimes just carries on driving for a bit (`keepRidingChance`, half the time). In `src/npc/drag.js`; the "Drag race" folder in the tuning panel, with a "Start a drag race now" button in "Girls (live)".

**Follow the leader.** Two and a half minutes into a game or later, when both girls have been stood about for twenty seconds or so, one (Jess more often) suggests going for a ride together. Each takes a quad: one leads in big loops, the other waits for her to set off, then follows at her pace, like following you. After a minute or two the follower overtakes, goes ahead and waits, and the one she passed follows her; they bicker about the pace. After two swaps and a last stretch they stop and get off. Not while a race is on, and `N` ends it. In `src/npc/convoy.js`; the "Follow the leader" folder.

**Doughnuts.** Bored, Jess sometimes takes a quad off to a clear bit of field and spins doughnuts for 18 to 32 seconds, tearing it up (it acts as mud under the wheels, throws clods, and leaves ruts that last four minutes instead of forty seconds). In `src/npc/doughnuts.js`; the "Doughnuts" folder, and `boredDoughnutChance` in the Companion folder.

She lines up about 30 m away and rides at you. On a two-stroke it is usually a wheelie: the nose comes up for a couple of seconds as she goes past a few metres to your side. On any quad it can be a skid: she stands on the brakes so she stops beside you. Then she looks over to see if you saw it. If you were looking her way during it she is chuffed and says so; if you weren't, she tells you off and goes back to do it again, up to three goes, before giving up on you. In `src/npc/showOff.js`; the "Showing off" tuning folder, and `boredShowOffChance` in the Companion folder. The debug panel has "Jess shows off to you" in the Girls folder.

Bored in the yard, Amy sometimes moves them one by one into a neat row in front of the barn doors, noses to the barn: she gets on the untidy one nearest her, drives it slowly in line with its slot and parks it, then (usually) goes on to the next. One that is only a little off its slot she nudges straight by hand. Now and then Jess parks one crooked on purpose, and a few seconds later Amy notices and puts it right. In `src/npc/lineUp.js`; the "Lining them up" folder (the row), and `boredLineUpChance` and `lineUpCrookedChance` in the Companion folder. Ride off without looking and it falls off, and its owner lets you know; the spilt tea stays on the ground for a minute. Left alone, she comes back for it after a minute or two. In `src/npc/tea.js`; the "Tea on the rack" folder, and a debug button.

**Red diesel.** South of the petrol tank (past the hose) stands a squat dark green diesel bowser labelled RED DIESEL, with a dark green jerry can beside it (10 litres to start with). Hold `E` at the bowser with any can to fill it with diesel, and pour from a can as usual; nothing on a quad says what's in its tank except how it behaves, though the prompts tell you what's in the can you're holding. Diesel in a petrol quad is slow poison: it takes up the diesel over about 25 seconds of running or cranking, runs rougher, weaker and with misfires, smokes thick grey, and with about 20% diesel in the tank or more it stalls and won't catch again. Putting petrol back in doesn't help. Take the toolbox to it and press `P` ("Pump out the tank and carb") to drain it; the fuel is lost. Amy says so if you pour the diesel can near her, says what's wrong when a quad runs badly or dies on it, and says to drain it if you keep cranking; Jess blames you. Now and then Jess, with a low quad parked near her, takes the diesel can to it herself and pours three litres in ("it's all just fuel"), and Amy, if she's close, tells her off. In `src/engine/diesel.js` and `src/npc/dieselMistake.js`; the "Red diesel mistake" folder, the engine folder's diesel numbers, and a debug button.

On the back she holds on round your waist (look down and you'll see her hands), leans with you into corners, lurches forward under hard braking, and looks about or over your shoulder. Hold `C` or the middle mouse button to look back at her. Left alone on a quad she holds the rails (or the strap on the yellow).

She talks, now and then, with captions at the bottom of the screen:

- Riding pillion she reacts to the ride: "Oh, this is fun!" on a smooth run, "This is a bit rough." over bumps, "Careful!" when you go too fast, a gasp when you brake hard, and something when it stalls or runs out of petrol.
- She likes the quads running. A quad that stalls where she can see it gets her down (worse if it was under her, and a stall while she's driving knocks her nerve a little), and she's relieved when it catches again. With all of them going she says how much she likes it; with most off she wishes they were, is keener to go and start one, and is slower to switch an idling one off.
- She likes the seat shaking her: the harder it shakes, the more she enjoys it. Sat on a buzzing quad she admits to it, and over the game her favourite quad moves to whichever one has shaken her most.
- Sometimes (Amy more than Jess) she sits side-saddle on the back instead of astride, with both legs over one side. She holds on less well that way: a hard corner or a big bump or landing makes her yelp and, if it goes on, slide off and be left behind. Her arms round you, when you're the one riding in front, help a lot.
- If you crank a quad near her for a while without it starting, she has a guess at why: the choke if it's cold, flooding if it's rich, the fuel tap if the carburettor is dry.
- Left waiting she sighs, hums, yawns and asks if you're going. Ride off without her and she'll shout after you.
- Sat on the back of a parked quad, every few minutes she asks if she can get off; the menu then has "Yes, hop off" and "No, stay there" (she grumbles and stays). If you don't answer, after several minutes she lets herself off. On the back by her own choice (after one of her drives, or bored), she gets bored of it sooner and gets off. On a long ride together she sometimes asks if you can stop soon.
- Jump off and leave her on a quad driving itself and she's either terrified or having the time of her life (40% of the time), and tells you about it afterwards. Hating it, after 8 to 18 seconds she climbs onto the front and either stops it, switches it off and gets down, or drives it about herself.
- She speaks up the further away you are, and shouts some things (being left behind, a quad riding itself), which carry twice as far.
- She comments when the seat is shaking hard, and has a favourite quad each game.
- Left standing about, sooner or later she does something on her own: wanders off for a look round (sometimes a long one), gets on a quad to wait, has a go at starting one, or takes one for a drive. When is never the same: usually somewhere between half a minute and a minute and a half, but anywhere from a few seconds over ten to a few minutes, and sooner when she's restless (which comes and goes). What she does is reshuffled each time, and the more confident a driver she's become, the more often she goes for a drive. Turn this off with `ownMind` in the Companion folder.
- She answers when you ask her something.

Their lines are recorded offline with Chatterbox, a text-to-speech engine that copies a voice from a short reference recording and can be made more or less emotional: each kind of situation has a feel (calm, chatty, cross, excited or scared), so a fright sounds frightened. Amy's reference is the Piper "cori" voice she had before, Jess's is a LibriVox reader. Every line is checked with a speech recogniser and recorded again if it doesn't come out right; a few of Amy's short shouts that Chatterbox can't manage are recorded with Piper instead, and anything still wrong is left to the browser's voice. Lines play from where she is, like her sighs, hums and laughs, which are real recordings (Jess's a shade quicker and higher). After changing the lines in `src/npc/voiceLines.js` or `src/npc/jessLines.js`, re-record them with `tools/generate_companion_voice.py --character amy` (or `jess`; usage at the top of the script). Until then, a new line uses the browser's speech, but only with a voice known to be a woman's (Flo or Shelley on a Mac, Google UK English Female in Chrome, Sonia or Libby in Edge); with none of those she just shows the caption. Her figure (bust, waist, hips) can be adjusted live in the Companion folder of the tuning panel. How chatty she is, her volume, speech rate and pitch, captions and the thresholds she reacts to are in the Companion folder of the tuning panel. The lines are in `src/npc/voiceLines.js`.

## How it's put together

```

  core/                input, tuning registry
  engine/              engine model (pure logic, no Three.js) and its parameters
  vibration/           pulse-driven vibration model (pure logic) and parameters
  effects/             exhaust smoke
  audio/               procedural Web Audio engine sound
  vehicle/             quad model, Rapier vehicle, drivetrain, dash styles, fuel; quadSpecs.js defines the quads
  items/               jerry cans, oil bottle and petrol tank
  player/              walking and seated first-person player

  interaction/         look-at interaction and the context menu
  hud/                 crosshair, prompts, notes for carried items, help
  world/               terrain, track, sky, props, layout
  debug/               lil-gui tuning panel

tools/                 slice_engine_audio.py, cuts recordings into engine pulses (Python, numpy)

```

**Engine model** (`src/engine/engineModel.js`). A state machine (off, ignition, cranking, running, stalled) driven by one idea: mixture. Mixture is the enrichment the choke supplies minus the enrichment a cold engine needs. It decides whether the engine catches, where it idles, how rough it runs and how much power it makes. Running rich fouls the plug, and a fully fouled plug stalls it. Temperature rises while running and falls slowly when off. Each power stroke is emitted as a firing pulse with its own timing and strength jitter, and both the vibration and the audio play those same pulses. That's why a rough engine sounds and feels lumpy together.

**Drivetrain** (`src/vehicle/drivetrain.js`). An automatic: centrifugal clutch into a CVT. It turns engine rpm and power into drive force, and puts braking load back on the engine while the clutch is locked to a moving bike.

**Ride sounds and Doppler** (`src/audio/rideAudio.js`, `rideEvents.js`, `propagation.js`). Riding sounds like the ground: grass swish, grit on the track, squelch and splash at the puddles, suspension knocks and landing thuds, and wind for the rider that rises with speed. Each quad's sound reaches you through a delay line set to its distance over the speed of sound, so a quad driving past rises and falls in pitch (about 3% each way at 10 m/s) and sounds duller further away. Levels are in the Sound folder of the tuning panel.

**Engine sound** (`src/audio/engineWorklet.js`). Three engines, chosen in the tuning panel (Audio, `engineSound`):

- *pulse* (default): revs built from the idle recording. Every firing pulse from the engine model plays one real recorded idle firing at close to its own speed, cut off with a short crossfade when the next firing starts. As revs rise the firings simply come closer together, so the exhaust's character stays the same machine as idle, the way a real engine's does. On top, each firing gets louder with revs, and an EQ that follows the revs takes out some of the deep thump and lifts the mid-range rasp, as measured from real engines going from idle to high revs. Each quad revs on its own idle: the red on the main quad recording, the blue on the ATV recording, the orange on the Honda CRM250. The green and the yellow blend into real rev recordings as they rev (`pulseRevLo` to `pulseRevHi`): the green from a Ténéré 250 into a Honda ATV, the yellow from a DT 125 into a Honda CR85, each firing still at its own speed.

- *recorded*: every firing pulse from the engine model plays one real recorded pulse, cut from CC0 recordings and resampled to last exactly one engine cycle. The four-strokes idle on quad recordings and rev on two recordings of ATVs being ridden (one driving away, one riding round a quarry), blended across 1,500 to 2,100 rpm. Both ATVs move about in front of the microphone, so the slicer takes the distance out of their loudness and levels it to rise with rpm instead. At higher revs each grain spans several real recorded firings, so there are fewer joins. The two-stroke uses its own bank, with idle from a two-stroke motocross bike and revs from a Honda CRM250 and a second two-stroke dirt bike. While the revs are steady, playback reads straight on through the recording rather than jumping between unrelated pulses. Because pulses still come from the engine model, a cold idle is still lumpy, misfires are still missing beats, and it stays in sync with the vibration and smoke. Cranking is a looped starter recording.
- *synth*: a pulse-driven exhaust synth with a simulated pipe and silencer.

To recut the recordings, see the instructions at the top of `tools/slice_engine_audio.py`.

**Fumes in the barn** (`src/world/barnFumes.js`). Running an engine indoors fills the barn with haze (tunable under "Fumes in the barn"); it takes about a minute of idling to get thick and a few minutes to clear.

**Exhaust smoke** (`src/effects/exhaustSmoke.js`). One soft puff per firing pulse, so you can see the beats at idle. White-grey vapour when cold, darker when rich or flooded, a burst when you snap the throttle open, and a light haze once warm. Tunable in the Smoke folder.

**Vibration** (`src/vibration/vibrationModel.js`). Each firing pulse kicks a set of damped oscillators (body heave, pitch and roll, handlebars, rider's head) in a slightly random direction. Kicks are strongest at idle and with a rough engine, and fade as rpm rises. The bike body, handlebars, seated camera and anything sitting on the bike all move with it.

**Companion** (`src/npc/companion.js`). A small state machine (idle, walking, mounting, seated, dismounting). Requests are methods, and `getActions()` decides which ones the menu shows. To add a new request, add a method and an entry in `getActions()`, plus a state if it needs one.

**Quad models** (`src/vehicle/quadModel.js`, `src/vehicle/quadGeometry.js`). Procedural: fender shells swept round each wheel, lofted seats, tanks and hoods, bent-tube racks and frames, lathed tyres with lugs, and suspension arms and shocks posed from the physics wheel heights every frame. Static parts are merged per material.

**Quads** (`src/vehicle/quadSpecs.js`, `src/vehicle/quadUnit.js`). Each quad is a spec (name, colour and overrides for engine, vehicle, vibration, audio and smoke parameters) built into a `QuadUnit` that owns its engine model, physics, vibration, smoke and sound. To add a third, add a spec and a spawn point in the layout.

**Layout** (`src/world/layout.js`). Positions for the field, barn, gate, track control points and spawn points all live here.

**Parameters**. Every tunable number lives in a `*Params.js` file next to the code that uses it, with a range for the tuning panel.

Design decisions and their reasons are logged in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md). Asset sources and licences are in [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md).
